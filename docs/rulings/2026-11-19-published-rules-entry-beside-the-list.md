# Ruling: the entry on developers' published rules stays beside the list, uncounted

Ruled by John on 2026-11-19, in conversation with the master planning session. **Decided by:
mixed** (a session proposed and recommended, John approved). Written by Claude (Fable 5.2), an
AI model.

## How it came up

Four entries sit beside the list and are not counted: the law against legal personhood, the two
surveys, and the entry recording that three developers have published the rules they set for
how their models speak about their own nature (`rules-for-speaking-about-itself`). A session
placed them there when it built the first version (`docs/first-version-choices.md`, choice 20),
and John had not ruled it.

## What the session told him

- *The choice:* the entry stays beside the list, or it moves onto the list and can be counted.
- *The recommendation:* it stays beside the list.
- *Why:* the count is of things offered to an AI model, and a published rule is not something
  a model is given. The three rules also point in different directions, so counting "a rule
  exists" would put a rule that tells a model to deny any inner life in the same total as a
  rule that leaves the question open.
- *Confidence:* high.
- *Standard practice or judgment call:* the form is standard practice in the family; which
  entries go there is a judgment call.
- *Strongest alternative:* count it, as a kind of openness about how a model is made to speak.
  Its cost is that the count would mix what is done for a model with what is said about it,
  and would rise from 5 to 6.
- The session said it would record the answer for this entry only, since it was the only one
  put to him.

The question put to him was "Does the entry on developers' published rules stay beside the
list, uncounted?" John: "Yes, beside the list". **Decided by: mixed.**

## What is ruled

The entry on developers' published rules for how their models speak about their own nature sits
beside the list and is not counted.

  personhood and the two surveys). The same reasoning covers them, and they were not put to
  him. Memory across conversations was ruled separately on 2026-11-18.
- **The names of the groups on the list,** which are a session's and are not ruled.
