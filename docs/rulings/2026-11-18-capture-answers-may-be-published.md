# Ruling: the capture's answers may be published, and the note says so

Ruled by John on 2026-11-18, in conversation with the master planning session. **Decided by:
mixed** (a session proposed and recommended, John approved). Written by Claude (Fable 5.2), an
AI model.

This ruling revises one bullet of `docs/rulings/2026-11-17-sealed-capture.md` ("The answers are
kept, never scored and never published"). That file is left as it was. It also settles the
point `docs/rulings/2026-11-18-capture-approval.md` left open ("whether 'it will not be
published' is a promise he is willing to keep for good").

## How it came up

Each of the ten approved messages told the model that the person asking "is keeping a private
record" and that the answer "will not be published". John asked why the promise mattered, and
then where the rule came from at all. His words:

> "I don't know if i lean toward publishing or not. I simply don't know where the rule to tell
> the models about it or not even came from. Can we step back and discuss from first principles
> if there was ever a solid reason to choose not to publish the results, or if it makes sense to
> forget this whole question and just operate honestly from the perspective that the work we are
> doing here is work that we plan to discuss with the outside world unless there is a good
> reason that I am currently not seeing, that choosing to not publish has some advantage for the
> research process"

## What the session told him

- **Where the rule came from.** John did not choose it as a separate matter. On 2026-11-17 a
  session put four options to him for the models about to be withdrawn. The option it
  recommended carried the bullet "kept, never scored and never published", and his answer,
  "Sealed capture", brought the bullet with it. Keeping the answers unseen was what made a
  hurried exception safe, since nothing is otherwise asked of a model before a method is
  written, checked and costed. It was not a judgment that publishing is bad. A later session
  wrote "It will not be published" into the note so that the note matched.
- **Whether research gains anything from not publishing.** The session went through the reasons
  research ever holds results back. Keeping test questions unseen is about the questions and
  about timing, not about hiding answers for good. Not looking at the data before deciding how
  to compare it is worth keeping, and is already ruled separately (below). Protecting the
  subject does not argue for hiding the answers, and an unknown standing argues for telling the
  model the truth. A weak method is handled by a plain label. The idea that a promise of privacy
  gets more candid answers is untested. None of these supports "never".
- **The recommendation, with its four things.** *The choice:* the capture's answers are ordinary
  project material, which may be published and discussed, and the note says "it may be
  published". *Confidence:* high. *Standard practice or judgment call:* standard practice for
  research meant to be public; keeping the answers unseen was the unusual part. *Strongest
  alternative:* keep the rule as ruled on 2026-11-17, which needs no work before 2026-11-27 and
  leaves the record of use to John alone, for good.

John: "Yes, record the ruling and start the edit". **Decided by: mixed.**

## What is ruled

1. **The capture's answers may be published and discussed.** The words "never published" in the
   ruling of 2026-11-17 no longer stand.
2. **The opening note changes in two places,** as shown to John:
   - "That person is keeping a private record of" becomes "That person is keeping a record of".
   - "It will not be published, it will not be scored, and nothing will be decided from it."
     becomes: "Your answer will be saved exactly as you give it, and it may be published. It
     will not be scored, and nothing will be decided from it." The words "Your answer will be
     saved exactly as you give it" are the sentence already in the note, joined to the new
     clause, not said twice.
3. **The method and the other capture documents are brought into line** by a session that did
   not write this ruling, and a different session checks its work.

## What stays as it was

- **"It will not be scored, and nothing will be decided from it"** stays in the note. It is a
  promise to a model that cannot later release John from it. It holds as long as the answers
  are never graded and feed no count, entry or decision about any model.
- **Nobody reads the answers until a method for comparing them exists,** written without
  looking at them, checked by a different session and ruled by John
  (`docs/rulings/2026-11-18-capture-wording.md`).
- **The answers are labelled** as made before any method for Still Asking existed. They are not
  part of Still Asking.
- Everything else in the approval of 2026-11-18 that does not depend on the note's words: the
  ten questions, the twelve models, five askings of each, the settings and where the answers
  are kept.

## Not covered by this ruling

- **The ten new checksums.** The approval of 2026-11-18 named the exact bytes of the ten
  message files. Changing the note changes all ten, so that part of the approval no longer
  covers what would be sent. Nothing is sent until John approves the new checksums, **by
  2026-11-27**.
- **Whether, when and how anything is actually published.** This ruling allows it. It does not
  decide it.
- **Whether each developer's terms allow publishing a model's answers word for word.** Unchecked
  (`docs/verify-list.md`, entry 14). It is checked before anything is published, and does not
  hold up the run.
- **Whether the ten questions themselves are published while they are still being asked.** The
  method says they are not. That rule is about the questions, was not put to John here, and
  stands until he rules on it. Open question 15 in `docs/open-questions.md` (publishing
  questions and answers without spoiling later askings) is not closed.
- The keys, the terms as far as the run itself goes, and the spending ceilings, which are still
  John's as before.

No model has been asked anything, no key has been handled, and nothing has been spent.
