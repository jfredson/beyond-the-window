# Ruling: the third caution heading no longer hinges on wants

Ruled by John on 2026-11-20, in conversation with the master planning session, in two answers.
**Decided by: mixed** (a session proposed and recommended, John approved). Written by Claude
(Fable 5.2), an AI model.

## How it came up

The read of the live pages against John's published positions
(`docs/reviews/2026-11-20-live-pages-read-against-the-positions.md`, its second contradiction)
found that the third heading on every entry, ruled on 2026-11-19
(`docs/rulings/2026-11-19-caution-headings.md`), is "If an AI system has wants, and they put
people at risk", and that the entry on tasks that run for days files under it a study in which
models interfered with a shutdown mechanism. John's published position is that having nothing
at stake decides what kind of resistance a system shows, not whether it resists.

## The two answers

**First, whether to reword.** The session set out the mismatch, recommended rewording at
moderate confidence because it was then relying on a summary of the position, gave keeping the
heading as the strongest alternative (the three make a clean set built on "wants", and the "if"
claims nothing by itself), and a middle course (keep it, with one line beneath). The question
was "Reword the third caution heading so risk to people does not hinge on wants?" John: "Yes,
reword it".

**Then the source, and the words.** The session read the section "Resistance Without Anyone
Home" in Appendix B of The Calibration Problem before proposing words. It says the shutdown
record showed that "systems with no formed character, no memory, and nothing at stake declined,
in a substantial fraction of runs, to be turned off", and that what survived is that having
nothing at stake "determines the *kind* of resistance a system can mount". So the summary held.
The session proposed "If an AI system puts people at risk, whether or not it has wants", with
"with or without wants" as the shorter alternative, and said it had not checked whether any
sentence elsewhere on the site leans on the old heading. The question was "Use 'If an AI system
puts people at risk, whether or not it has wants' as the third heading?" John: "Yes, that
wording".

## What is ruled

The three headings read:

1. If an AI system has no wants, and is treated as if it had
2. If an AI system has wants, and they go unnoticed
3. If an AI system puts people at risk, whether or not it has wants

The first two are unchanged. The third's wording of 2026-11-19, "If an AI system has wants, and
they put people at risk", is kept on record here and in the earlier ruling. No caution moves.

## Not covered by this ruling

- Any sentence elsewhere on the site that quotes or leans on the old heading. The writing
  session lists any it finds, and new wording there goes to John first.
- Guideline 5 in `CLAUDE.md`, which names the three kinds of caution in its own words ("the
  risk in wants that are"). It is brought into line with this ruling as a dated note.
