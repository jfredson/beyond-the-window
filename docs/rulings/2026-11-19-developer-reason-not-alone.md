# Ruling: a developer's own reason does not stand alone under the second caution

Ruled by John on 2026-11-19, in conversation with the master planning session. **Decided by:
mixed** (a session proposed and recommended, John approved). Written by Claude (Fable 5.2), an
AI model.

## How it came up

This is the second of the seven points John decides before a session writes the third caution
into the records (`docs/research/2026-11-18-third-caution-sources.md`, section 6). The second
caution is the one headed "If an AI system has wants, and they go unnoticed"
(`docs/rulings/2026-11-19-caution-headings.md`).

Two entries have no source but the developer itself: the one about interviewing a model before
it is retired (`interview-before-retirement`) and the one about a retired model staying open to
the public (`retired-model-stays-open`). On both, the developer's page gives its own reason for
the practice, and that reason is the only thing found so far that could go under the second
heading.

## What the session told him

- *The choice:* let the developer's stated reason stand as the caution, with a label saying
  whose it is; or say under that heading that nothing is recorded yet from anyone but the
  developer, and point to where the page already gives the developer's reason.
- *The recommendation:* it does not stand alone. A developer's statement of its own reasons
  goes under this heading only beside a source from outside the developer. The session that
  checked the research note proposed this, and the session that wrote the note came round to
  it.
- *Why:* under the risk heading a developer's page is used against its own practice. Here it
  would be used in favour of it, so a reader would meet one party as both the fact and the
  caution on the fact. The developer also gains from being seen as careful.
- *Confidence:* moderate. The cost is two more entries with nothing under the new heading.
- *Standard practice or judgment call:* judgment call.
- *Strongest alternative:* carry it with the label. Its cost is that on those two entries the
  only caution of this kind is a developer's account of its own good motive.
- *An interest declared:* the master session is a model made by the developer in question, and
  said so, and said that John should weigh its view with that in mind.

John first asked "what do you think?". The session gave its view at more length: the reader
loses nothing, because the developer's reason is already on the entry where it says what was
done and why; an empty heading is itself information; and the doubt worth taking seriously is
that an empty second heading beside two full ones brings back, in a small way, the lean the
third caution was added to remove. It named three things that answer the doubt: the empty
heading says in words that the developer's reason exists and where it is; the About page
carries a general statement of both costs, if John agrees to point 6; and the second search
pass in point 7 looks for an outside source for these two entries.

John: "I agree with your recommendation". **Decided by: mixed.**

## What is ruled

1. **A developer's statement of its own reasons stands under the second caution only beside a
   source from outside that developer.**
2. **Where there is no outside source,** the heading says that nothing is recorded yet from
   anyone but the developer, and points to where the entry already gives the developer's
   reason.
3. **This applies now to two entries:** the interview before retirement, and the retired model
   that stays open.

## Not covered by this ruling

- **The exact sentence under an empty heading.** A session drafts it and a different session
  checks it.
- **The first and third cautions.** The rule is about the second only. A developer's page
  already stands under the risk heading, and that is not changed.
- **Points 3 to 7,** including the two the session leaned on in its answer: the general
  statement on the About page (point 6) and the second search pass (point 7). John has not
  ruled either.
- **Coming back to this.** The session said that if the two entries still look lopsided once
  the pages are built, that is a reason to put the question to John again with the pages in
  front of him. That is the session's suggestion (decided by: agent), not a condition John set.

Nothing on the site has been changed by this ruling. No model has been asked anything, no key
has been handled, and nothing has been spent or published.
