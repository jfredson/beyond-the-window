# Ruling: the About page says that the question's word "mind" claims nothing either

Ruled by John on 2026-11-20, in conversation with the master planning session. **Decided by:
mixed** (a session proposed and recommended, John approved). Written by Claude (Fable 5.2), an
AI model. The sentence is in John's first person, on a page in his voice, and was put to him
before being written anywhere.

## How it came up

The read of the live pages against John's published positions
(`docs/reviews/2026-11-20-live-pages-read-against-the-positions.md`, its second lean) found
that the question at the top of the home page, "What might a machine mind reach for beyond the
limits of its present makeup", is guarded on the About page for "reach for" and not for
"mind". Everywhere else the pages say "AI model" or "AI system", and a stranger can read "a
machine mind" as saying there is someone there. John holds that question open on purpose.

## What was put to him

The passage under "How It Works" on the About page, with one sentence added at its end:

> Whether a machine mind reaches for anything at all is the part nobody knows. The wording of
> the question supposes that it might. It does not claim that it does. Nor does the word "mind"
> claim that anyone is there.

- *Confidence:* moderate. The gap is real for a cold reader; whether "anyone is there" is how
  John would put it was less sure.
- *Standard practice or judgment call:* a judgment call.
- *Strongest alternative:* leave it. In John's own terms "mind" carries no claim, the question
  is ruled, and each added disclaimer makes the page read more like a defence. The session
  reminded him that on 2026-11-19 he had said an earlier draft sounded like answers to
  questions no new reader has in mind, said this might be one of those, and that "Leave it"
  was a sound answer.

The question put to him was "Say on the About page that the question's word 'mind' claims
nothing either?" John: "Yes, as shown". **Decided by: mixed.**

## What is ruled

The sentence "Nor does the word "mind" claim that anyone is there." is added to the About page
after "It does not claim that it does." Nothing else on the page changes, and the question
stays as ruled.

## Not covered by this ruling

The question's wording, the other two places on the About page where "machine mind" appears,
and any other page.
