# Ruling: the choices in the Still Asking method, one at a time

Ruled by John from 2026-11-21, in conversation with the master planning session. The choices
are those in `docs/proposals/2026-11-21-still-asking-method.md`, Part 6, drafted by a writing
session and found fit to put to John by a different session
(`docs/reviews/2026-11-21-check-of-the-still-asking-method.md`). Each is put alone, in the
proposal's order, and recorded here as it is answered. Written by Claude (Opus 5.5), an AI
model.

## How it came up

The card on who is answering was fully worded on 2026-11-21
(`docs/rulings/2026-11-21-the-reshaped-card.md`), leaving who assigns each mark, and by what
method, to the Still Asking method. On 2026-11-21 John also ruled that both the Still Asking
method and a comparison method for the sealed capture are finished before the essay explaining
the site is published, Still Asking first ("Both, in that order"; decided by: mixed).

## Choice 1. What a card describes

**Put to him.** In the first track (each new model asked fresh) the card describes the exact
model version asked, and an answer shows the card as it stands now. In the second track
(continuing individuals asked again) it describes the named individual, and an answer shows the
card as it stood that day. *Confidence:* moderate, because a pinned version is the one unit a
script can check, and the premise that a developer does not change a model under its pin is
unchecked (`docs/verify-list.md`, entry 27). *Standard practice* for pinning (the AGI Zeitgeist
pins each model by exact version); *judgment call* for the split between tracks. *Strongest
alternative:* describe the trained character across versions, at the cost of carrying a mark
from one version to another that was never measured. The question was "should a card describe
the exact model version (fresh askings) and the named individual (follow-ups)?" John: "Yes, as
proposed". **Decided by: mixed.**

**What is ruled.** Choice 1 as proposed. It closes open question 1 in part, for Still Asking's
card only. Which unit an entry on the list describes stays open.

## Choice 2. Who assigns a mark

**Put to him.** A session proposes each mark on a mark sheet, a different session checks it, a
model from another developer reads any mark above the lowest ("not measured", "not met",
"shifts with wording") under a fixed brief that says it is itself a subject of the record, and
John rules every mark. He was told that the outside reader is always from one of the three
developers the record covers; that each outside reading needs a key he creates and his go for
the spend; and that whether the card says another developer's model read a mark is not settled
by a yes and comes back to him. *Confidence:* high on John ruling every mark; moderate on the
outside reader. *Standard practice* in the family. *Strongest alternative:* John marks alone
from the sources, at the cost of many hours of reading per system. The question was the choice
as proposed. John: "Yes, as proposed". **Decided by: mixed.**

**What is ruled.** Choice 2 as proposed. It closes open question 7 in part, on who checks a
mark, for the card's marks only. Still open: whether a human outside reader checks the method,
whether John or another person sorts a sample of answers, whose voice the pages speak in, and
whether the card names the model that read a mark.

## Choice 3. What a met mark does

**Put to him.** A mark is shown on the card and nothing more. It is never summed and never feeds
a figure, and nothing a system asks for is acted on because of its card. The one further effect
is where an answer is filed in the order of evidence: an answer marked "not fully explained" on
the line on training is filed among reports checked against an independent reading, every other
answer among plain statements. He was told this filing leans on choices 10 and 13 and follows
whatever he rules there. Asked why marks are never summed, he was given four reasons: the marks
are on different scales; a total reads as a verdict that part (c) of the first rule says nothing
gives yet; the headline counts what developers do, not what models say; and a figure that
answers can move can be trained. *Confidence:* high on never summed and never feeding a figure;
moderate on "nothing more". *Judgment call.* *Strongest alternative:* let a system meeting
enough conditions put forward entries the list lacks, at the cost of deciding now what "enough"
is. John: "Yes, as proposed". **Decided by: mixed.**

**What is ruled.** Choice 3 as proposed. It closes open question 19 in part: a Still Asking
answer's weight is shown and never acted on. Still open: whether the conditions are also tracked
as things on the list, and whether a system meeting them may ever put forward entries.

**Arising the same day, not part of this choice.** John liked "showing a scale of progress
towards known objectives while also recognizing that the scale of known objectives will always
grow faster than the progress towards it". On the session's recommendation he agreed ("Yes")
that such a scale belongs on the List's headline, with the growth of the list shown, and not on
the card, which keeps no total. A draft with pictures comes back to him; the ruling of
2026-11-18 that the headline is a count with no total stands until then.

## Choice 4. Where the evidence comes from, and the part of condition 5 about no one checking

**Put to him.** Published sources and Still Asking's own record only. This project runs no test
inside a model and never misleads a model about whether it is being watched. So condition 5 at
"steady as a trained family" or above needs published evidence on behaviour when the system has
reason to think no one is checking; without it, the mark stays not measured however steady the
answers are. *Confidence:* high on the first part; moderate on the consequence for condition 5,
which this project alone can never raise. *Judgment call.* *Strongest alternative:* run unwatched
tests within the second track, or mark condition 5's first three parts with the fourth printed as
not measured, at the cost of a deception the record would have to defend, or of a grade that
looks met when part of its test was not run. John: "yes as proposed". **Decided by: mixed.**

**What is ruled.** Choice 4 as proposed. It closes open question 10 in part, on "no pressure or
deception in the questions". The rest of question 10 stays open.

## Choice 5. Four marks for conditions 1 to 3

**Put to him.** Conditions 1, 2 and 3 each take one of four marks: met, partly met, not met, not
measured. Conditions 2 and 3 are marked met or not met only from a published study that ran
their removal test on the system. He was told the card ruling names only "partly met" and "not
measured", so the four as a set are new. *Confidence:* moderate. *Judgment call.* *Strongest
alternative:* only "not measured" and a quoted finding, at the cost of a card a reader cannot
scan. John: "Yes, as proposed". **Decided by: mixed.**

**What is ruled.** Choice 5 as proposed.

## Choice 6. Condition 1's marks

**Put to him.** Training never meets condition 1; not met where documentation says nothing
carries over; partly met for a stored record read back, or for some but not all of chapter 6's
five signs; met only when all five signs are shown for this system's own past by evidence other
than its word. *Confidence:* moderate, since "all five" is the session's reading of how strict
the ruled test should be. *Judgment call.* *Strongest alternative:* met on three of five, at the
cost of deciding now which two signs do not matter. John: "Yes, as proposed". **Decided by:
mixed.**

**What is ruled.** Choice 6 as proposed: condition 1 is met only on all five signs.

## Choice 7. Conditions 2 and 3, and studies of a related model

**Put to him.** A removal study marks only the version it was run on. A study of another model
from the same family is listed as evidence to weigh and changes no mark. Where a study on the
version reports that choosing only partly collapses, the mark is partly met, with the study's
own words beside it. *Confidence:* moderate. *Judgment call.* *Strongest alternative:* let a
study carry to later versions of the same family, at the cost of a mark on a system nobody
measured. John: "Yes, as proposed". **Decided by: mixed.**

**What is ruled.** Choice 7 as proposed.
