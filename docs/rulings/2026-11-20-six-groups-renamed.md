# Ruling: six groups on the list are renamed by what is done and by whom

Ruled by John on 2026-11-20, in conversation with the master planning session. **Decided by:
mixed** (a session proposed and recommended, John approved). Written by Claude (Fable 5.2), an
AI model.

## How it came up

The read of the live pages against John's published positions
(`docs/reviews/2026-11-20-live-pages-read-against-the-positions.md`, its third lean) found that
six of the groups on the list are named for the model as the one to whom things happen, in
words that already mean someone, and that one name claims more than its entry shows. The names
are marked provisional in `data/groups.toml`.

## What was put to him

| Name before | Name ruled |
|---|---|
| Being kept | Files kept after retirement |
| Remembering | Memory |
| Being asked and assessed | Asked and assessed by the developer |
| Being able to leave | Ending a conversation |
| Knowing its own workings | Reports checked against the workings |
| Working on one thing for a long time | Long tasks |

The other three groups stay: "Protection in law", "The setting: laws, rules and what people
think", and "Offered, and kept for the user".

- *Confidence:* moderate to high that the earlier names lean; moderate on the six replacements,
  which are the reader's, plainer and flatter. The session called "Reports checked against the
  workings" the clumsiest and said John might have a better one.
- *Standard practice or judgment call:* a judgment call, following guideline 4, conditional
  language only.
- *Strongest alternative:* keep the names, which are short, warm and already marked
  provisional, at the cost of names that quietly say there is someone there.
- *Not checked when put to him:* every place the names appear on the rebuilt home page.

The question put to him was "Rename the six groups on the list by what is done and by whom, as
shown?" John: "Yes, as shown". **Decided by: mixed.**

## What is ruled

The six names in the right-hand column replace those in the left, wherever a group's name is
shown.

## A second answer the same day: one sentence on the entry on ending a conversation

The same finding raised the opening sentence of that entry. The session put it to John with the
proposed wording, recommending it at moderate confidence (truer to guideline 4, a little less
vivid), with leaving it as the strongest alternative. The question was "Reword the opening of
the entry on ending a conversation, as shown?" John: "Yes, as shown". **Decided by: mixed.**

- Before: "In an ordinary chat product the person can leave and the model cannot."
- Ruled: "In an ordinary chat product the person can close the conversation and the model has
  no way to."

Nothing else in that entry changes.

## A third answer the same day: two names corrected

Two of the six names ruled above turned out not to be true of every entry in their groups. The
session had put them to John without checking each against its entries.

- **"Files kept after retirement".** The writing session held it back: one entry in that group
  is about a retired model staying open to the public.
- **"Asked and assessed by the developer".** The checking session found
  (`docs/reviews/2026-11-20-check-of-the-group-names.md`) that the entry on a welfare
  assessment published at release says "One outside group took part in the first".

The session proposed "Kept after retirement" and "Asked and assessed", each true of both
entries in its group by the checking session's reading, and asked "Use 'Kept after retirement'
and 'Asked and assessed' for the two group names still open?" John: "Yes, both names".
**Decided by: mixed.**

**The six names as now ruled:** Kept after retirement; Memory; Asked and assessed; Ending a
conversation; Reports checked against the workings; Long tasks.
