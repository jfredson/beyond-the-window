# Ruling: the home page opens with a headline number again, as the sibling sites do

Ruled by John on 2026-11-20, in conversation with the master planning session. **Decided by:
mixed** (John asked, the session recommended, John directed). Written by Claude (Opus 5.5), an
AI model, in the master planning session (which wrote with Fable 5.2 until that point the same
day).

## How it came up

John asked: "Did we lose the "headline number" concept for the homepage of the site that we are
using across all the other tracker sites? The site right now seems to just feature a headline
from a recent news article, but that shouldn't be the main headline for new readers visiting
the site. What is the main headline that we previously discussed for the project?"

The session answered that the rebuilt home page, published on 2026-11-20 by another planning
session on John's go ("Pursue the rebuild"; then "Yes, publish it", given before he had seen
the page; `docs/rulings/2026-11-20-pursue-the-rebuild.md`,
`docs/rulings/2026-11-20-go-to-publish-the-rebuilt-home-page-once-checked.md`), had set aside
the rulings that the home page opens with the count
(`docs/rulings/2026-11-18-home-page-count.md`) and the sentence beside it
(`docs/rulings/2026-11-19-home-page-sentence.md`), and no longer showed the founding question.
The new top line was one dated act. The session said it had seen the rebuild go out and had
not told him the count had left the home page, which it should have.

Asked why, the session set out the cold-reader audit
(`docs/proposals/2026-11-20-what-a-stranger-makes-of-the-site.md`): two AI readers per
version, on one phone screen, could not say what the count counted; the caveat under it read
as the site disowning it; four different counts showed in two screens; and nothing gave a
reason to care. Its view: the test showed the number was badly explained, not that a headline
number cannot work here, and the gains of the rebuild (the subject in plain words, the
developer named, the readings) could stay beneath a restored number.

## His words

John: "Yes please rebuild it with something as a headline just like we have for the other
tracker sites".

## What is ruled

The home page opens, as the sibling sites do, with a headline number: a short line saying what
the site is, the number, one plain sentence saying what is counted, and the question. The
rebuild's readings may stay beneath it.

## Not ruled yet

- **The exact number and sentence.** The ruled sentence of 2026-11-19 and a plainer one are to
  be drawn as alternatives, and the count must be worked out from the records, never typed in.
- **The layout,** which is built as a draft and shown to John as pictures before anything is
  published. Nothing reaches the live site until he has seen it.
- **The fraction.** The Upgrade Tree shows "28 of 35"; this site's ruling of 2026-11-18 is a
  count with no total beside it. That ruling stands unless John changes it.

## A second answer the same day: version B

A session built two drafts on the branch `claude/headline-draft-df7958`
(`docs/proposals/2026-11-20-headline-draft.md`, pictures in
`docs/proposals/pictures/2026-11-20-headline-draft/` on that branch). Both open with the short
line "A dated record of how the world is treating AI models", the computed count of 5, and the
founding question, with the rebuild's readings beneath and the site check again refusing a home
page whose first figure is not that count.

- **Version A:** John's sentence of 2026-11-19 word for word.
- **Version B:** "things a developer can do for the AI models it builds are done, or publicly
  committed to, by at least one developer today", then a line naming three of the five things,
  then a breakdown by developer ("5 by one developer, 1 by a second, 0 by the third this record
  covers").

The session recommended B at moderate confidence, with two changes first: the breakdown adds to
6 while the count is 5, because one thing is done by two developers; and the second developer's
"1" may still be a promise not in effect (its page says the change will "begin in the following
weeks"). The strongest case for A was that it is his ruled wording and B adds a third phrase for
"offered" to the site. The question was "Which version should open the home page?" John:
"Version B". **Decided by: mixed.**

**What is ruled.** Version B opens the home page, replacing the sentence ruled on 2026-11-19,
which is kept on record in its own file. The count stays as ruled on 2026-11-18, with no total
beside it.

**Not ruled.** The breakdown's final labels and figures after the two changes, which come back
to John before anything is published.

## A third answer the same day: the breakdown

The drafting session drew three ways of labelling the breakdown so that its figures do not
seem to add to 6 (one thing, ending a conversation, is done by two developers), and confirmed
from OpenAI's help page that its change was in effect on 2026-11-20, so its figure of 1 holds
under the ruled meaning of "offered". Option 2 keeps three small figures with labels that say
the overlap; option 3 uses figures that add to 5 with a line "One developer does all 5."; option
1 is a sentence. The session recommended option 2 at moderate confidence, with option 3 as the
strongest alternative. The question was "Which breakdown should sit under the 5?" John:
"Option 2". **Decided by: mixed.**

**What is ruled.** Under the count and its sentence the home page shows three figures with
these labels: "5, by one developer: all of them"; "1, by a second: one of the same 5"; "0, by
the third this record covers". The figures are worked out from the records.

## A fourth answer, on 2026-11-21: "in how it treats"

After the page went out (John: "Yes, publish now"), the check of the restored headline
(`docs/reviews/2026-11-20-check-of-the-restored-headline.md`, part 5) noted that OpenAI's own
pages describe its chat ending as a safety step for people, and that the entry says neither page
has the model decide or mentions the model's interests, so "for the AI models it builds" says
more than that developer says. The checker suggested "about"; the master session judged that
"about" reads as fixing a fault, and proposed instead "in how it treats", which counts the thing
without saying whom it is for and matches the short line above it. *Confidence:* moderate; the
wording was the session's and not yet checked against the five entries. *Strongest
alternative:* keep "for", which four of the five things plainly fit. The question was "Change
'for the AI models it builds' in the headline sentence?" John: "Yes, change it". **Decided by:
mixed.**

**What is ruled.** The sentence beside the count reads: "things a developer can do in how it
treats the AI models it builds are done, or publicly committed to, by at least one developer
today." The writing session checks it against all five counted entries before it is published.

## A fifth answer, on 2026-11-21: "done, or publicly committed to" stays

The same check (part 2) noted that the list page's meaning of offered is stricter ("a promise
that has not yet taken effect is not counted ... A commitment that is in force today is
counted") than the home page's "done, or publicly committed to". The master session recommended
leaving it, because the sentence describes the five counted things and is true of all five
(four done, one a commitment in force), it does not claim every promise is counted, and the
exact meaning is one tap away on the list page. *Strongest alternative:* "done, or under a public
commitment already in force", at the cost of a heavier first sentence. The question was "Leave
'done, or publicly committed to' as it is on the home page?" John: "Yes, leave it". **Decided
by: mixed.**

## A sixth answer, on 2026-11-21: the sentence under "Developers" matches

The writing session found that lower on the home page, under "Developers", the rebuild's
sentence "This record follows seven things a developer can do for the AI models it builds."
still said "for". The master session recommended matching it, so the two sentences about the
same set of things use the same words. The question was "Change the Developers sentence to 'in
how it treats' too, matching the headline?" John: "Yes, match it". **Decided by: mixed.**

**What is ruled.** It reads: "This record follows seven things a developer can do in how it
treats the AI models it builds."
