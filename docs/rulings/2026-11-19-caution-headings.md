# Ruling: the wording of the three caution headings

Ruled by John on 2026-11-19, in conversation with the master planning session. **Decided by:
mixed** (a session proposed and recommended, John approved). Written by Claude (Fable 5.2), an
AI model.

## How it came up

John's ruling of 2026-11-18 (`docs/rulings/2026-11-18-a-third-caution.md`) made the cautions on
every entry three in place of two, and left the wording of their headings unruled. The research
note on the new caution (`docs/research/2026-11-18-third-caution-sources.md`, section 4) set
out four sets of headings, and its section 6 listed seven points for John to decide before a
session writes the records. This is the first of the seven.

The present headings, in `scripts/export.py`, are "The worried reading" and "The cost of
seeing more than is there". The second assumes that nothing is there, which is the fault the
ruling of 2026-11-18 named.

## What the session told him

- *The choice:* which of the four sets, and with it whether the plural "wants" may stand in a
  heading on a public page. The project keeps "wanting" as a noun off public pages; "wants"
  is already in guideline 5 and on the About page.
- *The recommendation:* set D, the one proposed by the session that checked the research note,
  with "an AI system" in place of "a model", so that one wording fits the entries about laws
  and surveys as well as the entries about a single model.
- *Confidence:* moderate that set D is better than the shorter set A, because each of its
  headings says whose wants are meant; low that any of the four is the last word.
- *Standard practice or judgment call:* judgment call.
- *Strongest alternative:* set A ("If wants are assumed and none are there", and so on). It is
  shorter and uses the earlier ruling's own words. Its cost is that a reader on a page about a
  law must work out for themselves that "wants" means an AI system's.

The question put to him was "Which wording for the three caution headings?", with two answers
offered: set D with "an AI system", or set D as written. John: "D, "an AI system"". **Decided
by: mixed.**

## What is ruled

The three headings, in the order of the ruling of 2026-11-18:

1. **If an AI system has no wants, and is treated as if it had**
2. **If an AI system has wants, and they go unnoticed**
3. **If an AI system has wants, and they put people at risk**

The same wording is used on every entry, whatever the entry is about. The word "wants" stands
in these headings.

## Not covered by this ruling

- **Whether the plural "wants" is allowed everywhere on public pages.** John chose headings
  that contain it, and that is all he was asked. The session reads the rule on working words
  as covering the coined noun "wanting" and not the ordinary plural, and that reading is the
  session's (decided by: agent), not John's.
- **The order the three are shown in on an entry page.** The page shows the risk caution first
  today, and the ruling lists it third.
- **What goes under each heading**, which is points 2 to 7 of the research note's section 6.
- **The filing question from the site review's finding 5:** much of the text now under the
  risk heading is about people's beliefs and not about a system with wants. No heading mends
  that.
- **The other sentences on the pages** that say "two cautions". They change when a session
  writes the records, and a different session checks that work.

Nothing on the site has been changed by this ruling. No model has been asked anything, no key
has been handled, and nothing has been spent or published.
