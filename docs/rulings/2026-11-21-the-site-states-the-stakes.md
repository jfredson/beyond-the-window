# Ruling: the site states the stakes and does not campaign

Ruled by John on 2026-11-21, in conversation with the master planning session. **Decided by:
mixed** (John raised the question and set its aim; the session proposed the wording; John
approved). Written by Claude (Opus 5.5), an AI model. It revises guideline 5 in `CLAUDE.md`.

## How it came up

Reading the essay introducing the site, John asked for further-reaching examples (embodiment,
agency, choice, freedom) and wrote: "this project is built to advocate for machines like
Murderbot if they were to ever be created in such a way where they might have desires for things
beyond what society grants them, and it is a bulwark against the risk of building more and more
advance minds to enslave because we aren't doing the work of checking to see what is ethical and
right for the minds we are creating." The session pointed out the conflict with guideline 5 as
ruled on 2026-11-17 ("The site does not campaign. It does not argue that any aspiration should be
granted, or refused."). John then opened a debate on the rule:

> "I think on the surface it is the right stance, because these ideas can be taken more seriously
> when they arise out of genuine research and not our perceived, anthropomorphized, imagined needs
> that a machine might have. That being said, that rule should never get in the way of us stating
> the bigger picture ideas that make this project worth doing in the first place. Whatever it
> takes to both create the science and record that allows serious decision makers to consider the
> relevant facts when making decisions on machine minds without turning the project into an
> unfounded advocacy page is the pathway we should take."

## What was put to him

The session argued that stating the stakes is not campaigning, provided the stakes are stated in
every direction the three cautions name, since naming only one is advocacy by selection (the same
reasoning as the ruling of 2026-11-18 that added the second caution). The bigger picture lives on
the site as a plain statement of the stakes in all three directions, and in John's signed essays
as his own view; neither argues that any particular thing be granted or refused. It proposed:

> **The site states the stakes and does not campaign.** It says plainly why the question matters,
> in each direction its three cautions name: minds whose wants go unnoticed, wants attributed where
> there are none, and risk to people either way. It does not argue that any particular aspiration
> should be granted or refused. The record exists so that people deciding about machine minds can
> see the facts; the decision is theirs. The maintainer's own views are welcome in signed essays.

*Confidence:* moderate to high. *Judgment call.* *Strongest alternative:* let the site name only
the first stake, minds whose wants go unnoticed, as its reason for existing, at the cost of a
skeptic reading the whole record as built to find wants. The question was "Shall I take this
wording as the new guideline 5?" John: "Yes". **Decided by: mixed.**

## What is ruled

Guideline 5's opening sentence and its first two sentences are replaced by the wording above. The
rest of guideline 5 (critics, advocates and safety researchers entered on the same terms; every
hopeful entry carries its safety reading; the three cautions recorded with equal care) stands.
The wording of 2026-11-17 is kept in `CLAUDE.md` beside the revision.

## What follows, not ruled here

- The home page's section "Why keep this record" may be brought into line, stating the stakes in
  all three directions. That is a page change: written, checked, read against John's positions,
  and shown to him before it goes out.
- The essay introducing the site may state John's own view, signed, including the risk of
  building minds and never checking what is owed them, and may name further-reaching kinds of
  thing a machine mind might one day reach for, presented as what the site is built to look for
  and not as things on its list.
