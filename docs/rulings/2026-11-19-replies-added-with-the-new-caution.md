# Ruling: the replies and the limit are added in the same change as the new caution

Ruled by John on 2026-11-19, in conversation with the master planning session. **Decided by:
mixed** (a session proposed and recommended, John approved). Written by Claude (Fable 5.2), an
AI model.

## How it came up

This is the fourth of the seven points John decides before a session writes the third caution
into the records (`docs/research/2026-11-18-third-caution-sources.md`, section 6). John's
ruling of 2026-11-18 asked for one thing: a new caution on every entry, about the cost of
overlooking wants that are there. While looking for sources for it, the research session also
found writers who push the other way, and one who names a limit on a measure. The ruling did
not ask for those.

## What the session told him

- *The choice:* the session that writes the new caution also adds these in the same change, or
  it adds the new caution alone and the rest come later.
- *What would be added.* Under the first caution (the cost of treating an AI system as having
  wants when it has none): Erik Hoel on the entry about a model ending a conversation, and Anil
  Seth on the entries about protection in law and the public survey. Beside the second caution,
  as a limit and not as a caution: Bradford Saad on the entry about a developer keeping the
  files of retired models.
- *The recommendation:* yes, together.
- *Why:* if only the new caution lands, the entries lean the new way until the second change.
- *Confidence:* high that the two should land together. Moderate on where Saad's point sits,
  because the entry format has no place for a limit today.
- *Standard practice or judgment call:* judgment call.
- *Strongest alternative:* the new caution alone, replies later. Nothing is published, so the
  cost is only that the entries lean in between, and that a release in that gap would carry
  the lean.
- *What the session had not done:* the master session said it had not opened Hoel's, Seth's or
  Saad's pieces itself and was relying on the research note and its check for what they say.

The question put to him was "Add the replies to the first caution in the same change as the
new caution?" John: "Yes, together". **Decided by: mixed.**

## What is ruled

1. **The session that writes the new caution into the entries also adds, in the same change,**
   the replies under the first caution (Hoel on ending a conversation; Seth on protection in
   law and on the public survey) and the limit beside the second caution (Saad on kept files).
2. **Each source is opened by the session that writes it in,** and opened again by the
   different session that checks the work. Anything that does not say what the research note
   says it does is left out and reported.

## Not covered by this ruling

- **The exact words of each addition.** A session drafts them and a different session checks
  them.
- **How the entry format holds a limit.** The format has no place for one today. The writing
  session proposes one, and the proposal is marked PROPOSED until John has seen it.
- **Any further reply or limit** found in the second search pass (point 7). This ruling names
  four additions and no more.
- **Points 5 to 7.**

Nothing on the site has been changed by this ruling. No model has been asked anything, no key
has been handled, and nothing has been spent or published.
