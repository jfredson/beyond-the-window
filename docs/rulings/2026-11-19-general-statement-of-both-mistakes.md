# Ruling: the About page says in general what the two mistakes are, drawn from two writers

Ruled by John on 2026-11-19, in conversation with the master planning session. **Decided by:
mixed** (a session proposed and recommended, John approved). Written by Claude (Fable 5.2), an
AI model.

## How it came up

This is the sixth of the seven points John decides before a session writes the third caution
into the records (`docs/research/2026-11-18-third-caution-sources.md`, section 6). After the
rulings made earlier on 2026-11-19, several entries will say "nothing recorded yet" under the
second heading ("If an AI system has wants, and they go unnoticed"). A reader meeting that has
no account anywhere on the site of what the caution is about.

## What the session told him

- *The choice:* one or two sentences on the About page saying what the two mistakes are and
  that writers weigh them differently, or no general statement.
- *The recommendation:* yes, drawn from two writers who weigh the costs differently. Eric
  Schwitzgebel, who states a dilemma: treat such systems as persons and risk giving up real
  human interests for nothing, or do not and risk doing serious wrong
  ([summary page](https://faculty.ucr.edu/~eschwitz/SchwitzAbs/FullRightsDilemma.htm)). And
  Emilia Kaczmarek of the University of Warsaw, who argues that crediting too much is the
  nearer danger because its harms are already happening, while granting that crediting too
  little could do more harm in the end
  (["The Moral Status of AI and the Precautionary
  Principle"](https://diametros.uj.edu.pl/diametros/article/view/2071)).
- *Why:* the pair is balanced, and the statement gives the empty headings something to stand
  on.
- *Confidence:* moderate. The research session read Schwitzgebel by his summary page only and
  Kaczmarek's paper in part. Neither has been read in full.
- *Standard practice or judgment call:* judgment call.
- *Strongest alternative:* no general statement. The About page stays shorter and makes no
  claim resting on a partly read source. Its cost is that "nothing recorded" under a heading
  tells a reader nothing about what the heading means.
- *Two limits the session said it would hold the sentence to:* Schwitzgebel's dilemma is about
  personhood and full rights, a stronger subject than wants, so the sentence must not stretch
  his argument over every entry; and both sources are read in full before the sentence is
  written.

The session also corrected itself before this point. It had passed on the research note's
claim that the About page already tells readers where the first caution comes from. The About
page's paragraph on the cautions does not say so.

The question put to him was "Should the About page say in general what the two mistakes are?"
John: "Yes, from those two". **Decided by: mixed.**

## What is ruled

1. **The About page carries a short general statement** of what the two mistakes are, and that
   writers weigh them differently.
2. **It is drawn from Schwitzgebel and Kaczmarek.**

## Conditions the session set (decided by: agent)

John was told these before he answered. They are the session's, and he did not rule them
separately.

- Both sources are read in full before the sentence is written. If either does not say what
  the research note says it does, the sentence is changed or dropped, and that goes back to
  John.
- The sentence does not stretch Schwitzgebel's argument, which is about personhood and full
  rights, over entries that are about less.

- **A third writer.** The ruling names two.
- **Point 7.**

Nothing on the site has been changed by this ruling. No model has been asked anything, no key
