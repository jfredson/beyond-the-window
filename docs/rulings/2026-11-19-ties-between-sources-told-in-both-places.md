# Ruling: the reader is told about the ties between sources in each caution and on the About page

Ruled by John on 2026-11-19, in conversation with the master planning session. **Decided by:
mixed** (a session proposed and recommended, John approved). Written by Claude (Fable 5.2), an
AI model.

## How it came up

This is the fifth of the seven points John decides before a session writes the third caution
into the records (`docs/research/2026-11-18-third-caution-sources.md`, section 6, with the
count in its section 3).

The research session counted where the sources for the new caution come from. Of the 22 it
would use, 15 come from one developer (Anthropic), one research group (Eleos AI Research), or
people tied to that group. Of the 7 from elsewhere, 5 have a stated tie to the same small
network. The first caution has the mirror problem: most of it rests on one essay by the head of
another developer's AI group. The research note says these are ordinary ties in a small field,
and that nothing found suggests anyone wrote to order.

## What the session told him

- *The choice:* tell the reader inside each caution, once on the About page, or both.
- *The recommendation:* both. Inside a caution, the tie is stated as a fact with no judgment.
  On the About page, one sentence covers both cautions: most of the first comes from one essay,
  and most of the second from one developer, one research group and people who work with it.
- *Why:* a reader who lands on a single entry never sees the About page, and a reader of the
  About page alone cannot tell which entries the tie applies to.
- *Confidence:* high.
- *Standard practice or judgment call:* the research note says the About page already does
  this for the first caution. The master session said it had not reopened the About page to
  confirm that.
- *Strongest alternative:* the About page only, which keeps the entries shorter. Its cost is
  that a reader of one entry never sees the tie.
- *Two things to weigh:* the entries get longer, and on some the tie is stated several times;
  and Anthropic made the model writing this, with four of the 22 sources being Anthropic's own
  pages chosen by a model Anthropic made.

The question put to him was "Where is the reader told how closely tied the sources are?" John:
"Both places". **Decided by: mixed.**

## What is ruled

1. **Inside each caution,** a source's tie to the developer, to the research group or to the
   other camp is stated as a fact, with no judgment on it.
2. **On the About page,** one statement covers both cautions: where most of the first comes
   from, and where most of the second comes from.

## Not covered by this ruling

- **The exact sentences,** in the entries and on the About page. A session drafts them and a
  different session checks them. The pages' sentences as a whole still wait for John.
- **Each tie is checked at its source before it is stated.** The research note gives each one
  from a page its writer opened; the writing session opens them again, and a tie that cannot
  be shown from an open page is left out.
- **How the site says that a model made by one of these developers chose the sources.** That
  belongs to the authorship notice ruled on 2026-11-18
  (`docs/rulings/2026-11-18-how-the-site-declares-ai-authorship.md`), whose exact sentences are
  not ruled, and to open question 7.
- **Points 6 and 7.**

Nothing on the site has been changed by this ruling. No model has been asked anything, no key
has been handled, and nothing has been spent or published.
