# Ruling: the repository is public, like the sibling sites' repositories

Ruled by John on 2026-11-19, in conversation with the master planning session. **Decided by:
John** (he rejected the session's recommendation and stated his own). Written by Claude
(Fable 5.2), an AI model.

This ruling replaces point 1 of `docs/rulings/2026-11-19-hosting-private-and-by-hand.md` ("The
repository on GitHub is private"). That file is left as it was.

## How it came up

John had ruled that the site's setup copies the sibling sites wherever it can, with every
difference explained (`docs/rulings/2026-11-19-three-points-on-the-first-release.md`). He then
asked: "Walk me through those rulings, assuming we don't know the reason for each of them. Why
does the repo need to be private?"

## What the session told him

- The one reason given had been that the repository holds the ten sealed questions, and the
  capture's method says they are not published while they are still being asked.
- Looked at again, that reason is weak. It does not protect the capture: the models asked are
  already built, are asked with no access to the web, and cannot be changed by anything
  published now. What it protects is a later comparison, if the same questions are ever put to
  newer models, and no such comparison is planned or ruled. And John never ruled the rule: his
  ruling of 2026-11-18 on publishing the answers says it "was not put to John here".
- A second reason, that the working documents say which were written by an AI model, is not a
  reason to differ from the siblings, whose public repositories hold documents of the same
  kind.
- What private costs: nobody can inspect the records or how the number is worked out, there is
  no public history of changes, and a reader cannot raise a problem on GitHub.
- The one argument that holds: private can become public with one click, and public cannot be
  undone.
- The session recommended staying private until the first release and deciding then, with low
  to moderate confidence, and said its earlier "high" confidence had been wrong.

John: "I see no reason to make any of this private. Let's follow the standard we have already
set on the other sites". **Decided by: John.**

## What is ruled

1. **The repository is public,** as the sibling sites' repositories are.
2. **The ten questions of the sealed capture need not be kept unseen.** The method's rule that
   they are not published while they are still being asked no longer stands. This is the part
   of open question 15 that concerned the capture's questions; the rest of that question, about
   Still Asking, stays open.

## What the session did before making it public

It searched every version of every file in the repository's history for anything shaped like a
key, for email addresses, and for a short list of words about private matters. It found one
key-shaped string, a made-up example in a document ("MADEUP" is in it), no email address in any
file, and no private matter. The address on every commit is John's own, as on the sibling
repositories. The capture's replies are kept outside every repository and are not affected.

## Not covered by this ruling

- **Publishing the site.** A public repository is not the site. The go to publish is still
  John's.
- **Publishing the capture's answers.** Allowed by the ruling of 2026-11-18 and not decided.
- **The other differences from the sibling sites,** which the session was walking him through
  one at a time when he ruled this.

## What follows

- The capture's documents are corrected, by a dated note, where they say the questions are not
  published. The ten message files and their checksums do not change.
- The repository needs the licence files the siblings carry. A session copies their form and a
  different session checks it.
