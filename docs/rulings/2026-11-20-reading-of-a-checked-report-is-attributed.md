# Ruling: the entry on checked self-reports gives the reading of a correct report to its source

Ruled by John on 2026-11-20, in conversation with the master planning session. **Decided by:
mixed** (a session proposed and recommended, John approved). Written by Claude (Fable 5.2), an
AI model.

## How it came up

The read of the live pages against John's published positions
(`docs/reviews/2026-11-20-live-pages-read-against-the-positions.md`, its seventh lean) found
that the entry `self-report-checked` says in the site's own voice: "A correct report shows the
model can read something about its own state. It does not show that anything is felt." That is
John's published position on self-reports, almost word for word. Its first half is disputed by
researchers who hold that such a result shows nothing about a system reading itself, and the
About page says he argues his views elsewhere and not on this site.

## What was put to him

The reader offered two fixes: attribute the first half to its source in the entry, and add a
sentence to the About page saying the order of evidence and the reading of a checked report
follow a view he has argued in print. The session recommended the first only, because the
second is one more disclaimer on a page he has said should not read as a defence, and the page
already says he argues his views elsewhere. *Confidence:* moderate to high on attributing it;
the session had not opened the developer's page and did not propose wording. *Strongest
alternative:* leave it, since the sentence is careful and the entry's sources state the limits.

The question put to him was "Attribute the reading of a checked report to its source in the
entry, with no new About sentence?" John: "Yes, attribute it". **Decided by: mixed.**

## What is ruled

In the entry's description, the claim that a correct report shows a model can read something
about its own state is given to whoever makes it, in words that say no more than that source's
page does. No sentence is added to the About page.

## Not ruled: the wording

The writing session opens the source and writes the sentence. If the source does not make the
claim, it stops, and what the page does say is put to John. The wording is checked by a
different session before it is published, and shown to John with the result.
