# Ruling: a second developer's ending of chats is counted as offered

Ruled by John on 2026-11-20, in conversation with the planning session that ran the cold-reader
audit (not the project's master planning session). **Decided by: mixed** (a session put three
choices and John picked one, as with the ruling of the same day on naming every covered
developer). **The session gave no recommendation**, because of the declared conflict: the one
developer with anything counted until now, Anthropic, made the model doing the work. Written
by Claude (Fable 5.2), an AI model, in the working session that built the page.

**How these words reached this file.** The session that wrote this did not hear them. The
planning session relayed them in a message on 2026-11-20, with the question and the choices
put to him. They are quoted here as relayed. If John's words in that conversation differ, that
conversation is the record and this file is corrected.

## How it came up

The check of the rebuilt home page (`docs/reviews/2026-11-20-check-of-the-rebuilt-home-page.md`,
reason 2) found that OpenAI's ending of chats, which the page called a promise not yet in
effect, had begun. Whether it counts under the entry `ending-a-conversation` was left to John.

## What was put to him

Whether the site should count it as "a model can end a conversation itself", offered by
OpenAI, given that it is in effect and that OpenAI describes it as a safeguard for people. The
choices: "Count it as offered", "Do not count it", "Show it, leave it unruled".

John: "Count it as offered".

## What is ruled

OpenAI is counted as offering the thing in the entry `ending-a-conversation`. Its place in the
table of developers reads as counted, with the date of its public notice (2026-10-30). The
entry's state names two developers since 2026-11-20.

The count of things offered by at least one developer does not move. The thing was already
counted, and the count is still 5.

## What the developer's pages say does the ending, and a mismatch put back to John

The question put to John said the product "ends a conversation automatically". Before the
ruling was applied, both of OpenAI's pages were read again for what they say does the ending:

- The notice of 2026-10-30: "ChatGPT will be able to automatically end the chat to interrupt
  that pattern in the moment."
- The help page, read 2026-11-20: "ChatGPT may end that conversation", and "These safety
  interventions are enabled for personal ChatGPT accounts worldwide in Chat mode."

Both name the chat product. Both call it automatic or a safety intervention. Neither says that
the AI model itself decides to end the conversation, and neither says a separate system does.
The entry's test reads: "A leading AI model in public use can end a conversation by its own
action, as stated on its developer's own page."

So the ruling is applied as given, and the entry says plainly what the pages say does the
ending. **Whether the test's words "by its own action" are met is not something the pages
settle, and that is put back to John as a question.** It is not settled here, and the test is
not changed: a test is changed only by his ruling.

## A difference from an earlier ruling, recorded and not argued

On 2026-11-18 John ruled that the entry on memory across conversations sits beside the list
and is not counted, on the ground that the developers present that memory as kept for the user
(`docs/rulings/2026-11-18-what-offered-today-means.md`). This ruling counts a feature that its
developer presents as a safeguard for people. The two rulings differ in that way. Neither is
argued for or against here.
