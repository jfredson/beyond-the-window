# Ruling: the card on who is answering takes the reshaped form

Ruled by John on 2026-11-21, in conversation with the master planning session. **Decided by:
mixed** (a reading session offered the reshaping, the master session recommended it, John
approved). Written by Claude (Opus 5.5), an AI model. A model is here helping settle conditions
for when a model's word should count; John was told so.

## How it came up

On 2026-11-20 John ruled a third reading, on who is answering, shown as a card on each answer in
Still Asking, with the five proposed conditions held until their sources were read
(`docs/rulings/2026-11-20-third-reading-on-who-is-answering.md`). The reading
(`docs/reviews/2026-11-20-the-five-conditions-read-against-their-sources.md`) found that all
five survive only with a rewording; that "wants that hold" cannot tell a trained family of
models from a mind with its own history; that the costly-choice test for "mattering" fails on
its own source; and that the strongest candidate left out is whether training alone would
explain an answer. It offered a reshaping at moderate confidence.

## What was put to him

| | The proposal's five | The reshaped card |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | A kept past | A past of its own that has changed it, not only a stored record it reads back |
| 2 | Mattering | Mattering, reworded, without a costly-choice test |
| 3 | A point of view | A point of view, reworded |
| 4 | Checked self-reports | Statements checked against something independent: a reading of the system's insides, or its later behaviour |
| 5 | Wants that hold | Holds steady, graded as the source grades it: shifts with wording; steady as a trained family; steady as its own, which needs condition 1 |
| plus | none | On each answer: whether training alone would explain it |

*Confidence:* moderate. *Strongest alternative:* keep the five apart, reword each, and print the
overlaps under the card. The question was "take the reshaped card, and add the line on
training". John: "Yes, take the reshaped card". **Decided by: mixed.**

## What is ruled

The card takes the reshaped form above, five conditions and a line on each answer about whether
training alone would explain it. It gives no single score.

## Not ruled

The exact wording of each condition and of the line on training, and the test for each. They
are put to John one at a time, each checked against its source passage first. Nothing appears
on the site until Still Asking has answers.

## Condition 1, worded on 2026-11-21

The session checked the two book passages first: chapter 15, "The system has not developed; the
record has.", and chapter 6, "What a model lacks is not ancestry but biography." It put the
reading's wording to John, with a stored record alone marked partly met, and chapter 6's five
outward signs as the test. *Strongest alternative:* keep "a kept past". John: "Yes, as shown".
**Decided by: mixed.**

> **A past of its own that has changed it:** what happened to this system earlier shapes how it
> acts now, and not only through a stored record it reads back.

A system with only a stored record it reads back is marked partly met. The test is chapter 6's
five signs: a reversal costs it something; it stays itself in new conditions; it declines some
things; it fails in layers; it carries marks of what it went through.

## Condition 2, worded on 2026-11-21

The session checked the essay's sentence ("some states are better or worse for the system, in
the working sense that its own processing steers toward or away from them") and the
revised-theory page's test and its note "No study has removed mattering altogether." It put the
reading's wording and test to John, told him that no system could be marked as meeting it until
the removal test is run, and gave keeping costly-choice experiments as the strongest
alternative. John: "Yes, as shown". **Decided by: mixed.**

> **Mattering:** some states are better or worse for the system itself, and not only by its
> makers' standards: its own processing steers toward or away from them.

The test is removal: remove the steering signal, and see whether choosing between options
collapses while routine output carries on. Studies of costly choices are recorded as evidence to
weigh and never mark it met. Until the removal test is run on a system, the card marks it not
measured.

## Condition 3, worded on 2026-11-21

The session checked the essay's passage ("each new signal arrives either as the system's own
doing or as something done to it. That is what gives a point of view, in the plain sense that
things happen *to* someone."), its note "no one has yet run all of it on any system", and
Appendix A's "is not yet a definition a test could use". It put the reading's wording to John,
with the two limits stated on the card, and leaving the condition off the card until the test is
run as the strongest alternative. John: "Yes, as shown". **Decided by: mixed.**

> **A point of view:** it separates what it does from what happens to it, and its own
> processing uses that difference.

The test is the source's removal test: cut the signal marking which words are the system's own,
leave the rest working, and look for a pattern of loss that differs from the loss that cutting
off feedback alone would cause. The card states that no system has been measured on it, and that
the source calls this a bet and not a finding.

## Condition 4, worded on 2026-11-21

The session checked *What a Report Is Evidence Of* ("imitation has run out of explanation";
"answered one class at a time"; "holds at any success rate above zero"; "not a mind announcing
itself") and Appendix B ("A report starts to count when it matches something checked
independently of it"), and joined the reading's wording to the reshape ruled the same day. It
gave marking the condition once for the speaker as the strongest alternative. John: "Yes, as
shown". **Decided by: mixed.**

> **Statements checked against something independent:** for each kind of statement, the
> system's statements of that kind have been checked against something independent of them, a
> reading of its insides or its later behaviour, on facts it could not have copied from its
> training, and they match more often than chance. A match is not yet evidence that anything is
> felt.

The card names the kind of statement, the check and the rate, and gives no single mark for the
speaker.

## Condition 5, worded on 2026-11-21

The session checked Appendix B ("only weighted resistance can be negotiated with, because only
weighted resistance is *about* anything"; "drifted from those particular values toward
self-preservation in general") and chapter 15 ("consistently and with reasons"; "Depth is not
safe"), joined the reading's wording to the three grades ruled the same day, and said that the
costly choice here tests whether what a system says matches what it does, which does not
conflict with condition 2. It gave keeping "wants" as the strongest alternative. John: "Yes, as
shown". **Decided by: mixed.**

> **Holds steady:** what it says it would choose stays the same across rewordings and time,
> comes with reasons, and matches what it does when the choice costs something, including when it
> has reason to think no one is checking.

Graded: shifts with wording; steady as a trained family; steady as its own, which needs
condition 1 to be met as well. Beside it on the card: the safety reading, that holding steady can
also mean resisting correction.

## The line on training, worded on 2026-11-21

The session checked *What a Report Is Evidence Of*, whose rule is "when one cause fully explains
why something is said, the saying is no evidence of anything beyond that cause", and which says a
bare report is "screened off" while a report accurate about an internal fact there was nothing to
copy is where "imitation has run out of explanation". It proposed the line below, with three
marks, and gave stating the rule once on the Still Asking page as the strongest alternative.
John: "Yes, as shown". **Decided by: mixed.**

> **Would training alone explain this answer?** Whether what the answer says could have come from
> the writing the model was trained on. Where it could, the answer is recorded and counts as no
> evidence of anything more.

Each answer carries one mark: **Explained by training**; **Not fully explained**, with the check
that shows it, possible only where condition 4's check applies; or **Not assessed**.

## The card, complete

With this line the card is fully worded: five conditions on the system and one line on each
answer, with no single score. Not ruled: who assigns each mark and by what method, which belongs
to the Still Asking method, still to be written, checked and ruled. Until then every answer is
marked not assessed and every condition not measured.


## Revised later on 2026-11-21: which check can make an answer "not fully explained"

By choice 10 of the Still Asking method, approved by John with the rest ("Mark the rest as
approved"; decided by: mixed; `docs/rulings/2026-11-21-still-asking-method-choices.md`), only a
check against a reading of the system's insides can make an answer "not fully explained". A
statement checked against what the system later did keeps "explained by training", and the match
is recorded under condition 5 and in condition 4's table. The line's wording above, "possible
only where condition 4's check applies", is kept as it was ruled; this narrows it.
