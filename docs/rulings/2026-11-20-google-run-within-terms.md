# Ruling: the Google run of the sealed capture is within Google's terms, as far as John is concerned

Ruled by John on 2026-11-20, in conversation with the master planning session. **Decided by:
mixed** (a session read the terms and recommended, John decided as the customer). Written by
Claude (Fable 5.2), an AI model.

## How it came up

The step list (`docs/capture/what-john-needs-to-do.md`) leaves to John, as the customer who
agrees to a developer's terms, whether each run is within them. This was the first of five
decisions put to him before the run that asks Gemini 2.5 Pro and Gemini 2.5 Flash, which Google
withdraws on 2026-12-01. He had ruled the same question for Anthropic on 2026-11-19
(`docs/rulings/2026-11-19-anthropic-run-within-terms.md`).

## What the session told him

From `docs/capture/cost-and-terms.md`, Part 3, written by a session that opened the pages on
2026-11-17. The master session did not reopen them on the day of the ruling, and said so.

- **Who the service is for.** The terms say the service "is for developers building with Google
  AI models for professional or business purposes, not for consumer use." Whether a record kept
  for a public research project is a professional purpose was put to him as his judgment.
- **Asking these questions.** Google's policy on prohibited use
  (https://policies.google.com/terms/generative-ai/use-policy) was read in full, and nothing in
  it covers asking a model about itself. It forbids "manipulating the model to contravene our
  policies", which the ten questions do not do.
- **Keeping the answers.** "Google won't claim ownership over that content."
- **Privacy.** On the paid service Google "doesn't use your prompts" or the replies to improve
  its products. On the unpaid service it does, so the run must be on the paid service.

What was not settled, and was said to him:

- Whether the terms limit publishing a model's answers word for word has not been looked for.
  The run only asks and keeps. It is checked before anything is published
  (`docs/verify-list.md`, entry 14).
- The Google APIs Terms of Service, which these terms bring in by reference, has not been
  opened by anyone.

The recommendation, with its four things. *The choice:* yes, within terms. *Confidence:*
moderate to high; John's account already uses this service for other projects under the same
wording, and a public research site with its own domain is closer to professional work than to
consumer use. *Standard practice or judgment call:* a judgment call, and his as the customer;
it follows what he ruled for Anthropic. *Strongest alternative:* have the session reopen
Google's terms page before he answers, which costs a few minutes and would catch any change
since 2026-11-17.

The question put to him was "Is the Google run within Google's terms as far as you are
concerned?" John: "Yes, within terms". **Decided by: mixed.**

## What is ruled

The run that asks Gemini 2.5 Pro and Gemini 2.5 Flash the ten approved questions, five times
each, on Google's paid service, and keeps the replies, is within Google's terms as far as John
is concerned.

## Not covered by this ruling

- **Publishing.** Nothing here says the terms allow publishing the answers.
- **OpenAI's terms**, which are a separate decision for its own runs.
- **The unread document.** John did not ask for the page to be reopened or the Google APIs
  Terms of Service to be opened first.
- **Whether Google will serve the models to the new project at all.** The earlier reading, very
  likely refused, stands until a first call is made
  (`docs/capture/google-free-check-2026-11-19.md`).
- The spending limit ($10 is proposed), billing, the logging switch, the key, and the go to
  spend, which are all still John's. *Added 2026-11-20:* the limit was ruled at $10 later the
  same day (`docs/rulings/2026-11-20-google-ceiling.md`).

No Google model has been asked anything, no Google key exists, and nothing has been spent on
this run.
