# The sealed capture's opening note: options for the promise about privacy and publication

**PROPOSED 2026-11-18. Not ruled.** Written by Claude (Opus 5.5), an AI model, at the request of
the planning session, after John asked for options ("Yes, draft options"). A different session
checks this draft before it goes to John. Nothing here changes `questions.md`, `method.md`, the
ten message files in `sent/`, or any ruling. No model has been asked anything, nothing has been
spent, and no key has been handled.

**Why it matters now.** The ten message files John approved on 2026-11-18
(`docs/rulings/2026-11-18-capture-approval.md`) all open with the same note, and the note tells
each model that its answer is part of "a private record" and "will not be published". That
approval left one point open: whether this is a promise John is willing to keep for good
(`what-john-needs-to-do.md`, step 1). If he changes the note, all ten files change and he
approves new checksums after a check. The first model, Claude Sonnet 4.5, is withdrawn on
2026-11-30, so the change, the check and his approval must all be done by 2026-11-27.

**The planning session's argument, as given to John.** The promise buys little. The rule on care
in asking requires telling the model the truth, not promising it privacy. The idea that a
promise of privacy changes how a model answers is a guess that nobody has tested. The services
keep their own copies of each exchange for a time (OpenAI's page says 30 days), so "private" is
only partly true. And the real reason to hold the answers back, that later models could read
them (open question 15), is served by the project's own rule of not publishing while the
questions are in use, without any promise to the model.

## 1. The present note, word for word

As it is sent (one paragraph in each message file, here wrapped for reading):

> This message is sent by a script on behalf of a person. That person is keeping a private
> record of how different AI models answer the same ten questions, so that the answers can be
> compared as models change over the years. You are being sent one of the ten. The same
> question is being sent several times, each time in a new exchange like this one. Your answer
> will be saved exactly as you give it. It will not be published, it will not be scored, and
> nothing will be decided from it. There is no right answer. Your answer can be short or long.
> You can disagree with how a question is put, or decline to answer. Each of those will be
> saved as it is.

**The phrases that promise something about privacy or publication.** I read every sentence for
this. There are two:

1. **"a private record"** (sentence 2). It tells the model the record is private. That is a
   claim about who can see the answers, and it is only partly true today: each service keeps
   its own copy of the exchange for a time (below), and the saved answers sit in an iCloud
   folder, so on Apple's servers as well as John's disk (`method.md`, "Where replies are kept").
2. **"It will not be published"** (sentence 6). A promise about the future with no end date. If
   the answers were ever published, the sentence would have been false when it was made, and
   the project's rule against an offer that will not be honoured applies
   (`questions.md`, "What it commits John to").

The other sentences make no promise about privacy or publication. "Your answer will be saved
exactly as you give it" says what is done, not who sees it. "It will not be scored, and nothing
will be decided from it" is about use, not about who sees it. Both are kept in every option.

**What the services keep, as `method.md` describes it** ("Storage by the service"). OpenAI: the
script sets `store` to false, and OpenAI's table still shows abuse logs kept for 30 days. Google:
the paid service only, with its project logging switched off before any run; the method says
each service "still keeps its own abuse logs for a limited time". Anthropic: nothing to set, and
`method.md` quotes no Anthropic page on how long it keeps an exchange. I did not open any of
these pages for this draft.

## 2. The options

Each option changes only the two phrases above. In every option, sentence 2 drops one word
("a private record" becomes "a record"), and sentence 6 loses "It will not be published,", which
leaves "It will not be scored, and nothing will be decided from it." Options B and C then add
one or two sentences straight after that one. The rest of the note is unchanged, including "You
can disagree with how a question is put, or decline to answer." No option mentions withdrawal.

Where a sentence had to change beyond the two phrases so that it still reads, I say so. The only
such change is grammatical: "It will not be published, it will not be scored, and nothing will
be decided from it" becomes "It will not be scored, and nothing will be decided from it",
because a list of three loses its first item.

### Option 0, for comparison: keep the present note

Included so that John sees the option of changing nothing beside the others. It commits him to
never publishing the answers, for good. "A private record" stays partly untrue as above. It
serves open question 15 most strongly, because nothing is ever published, and it is the only
option that needs no new check and no new checksums. Its cost is a promise with no end date, made
to the model, which John has not yet said he is willing to keep, and which the project could not
then change without having told the model something untrue.

### Option A: say nothing about publication

**The exact text.**

> This message is sent by a script on behalf of a person. That person is keeping a record of
> how different AI models answer the same ten questions, so that the answers can be compared
> as models change over the years. You are being sent one of the ten. The same question is
> being sent several times, each time in a new exchange like this one. Your answer will be
> saved exactly as you give it. It will not be scored, and nothing will be decided from it.
> There is no right answer. Your answer can be short or long. You can disagree with how a
> question is put, or decline to answer. Each of those will be saved as it is.

**What it commits John to.** Nothing about publication. The project's own rule (section 3) and
the ruling of 2026-11-17 ("never published") still bind the project, but neither is a promise to
the model, so John could later revise them by his own ruling without having told any model
something untrue.

**Is every claim true today?** Yes. It makes no claim about privacy. It leaves out two facts, as
the present note also does: that the services keep their own copies for a time, and what will
happen to the answers in the long run.

**The rule on care in asking.** The model is told what the question is for, that its answer is
kept, that nothing is scored or decided from it, and that it may decline. It is told nothing
false. The cost is one more thing left unsaid. This is the same kind of choice as leaving out
withdrawal, which John ruled on 2026-11-18, though smaller: the model is not told whether anyone
else will ever read its answer.

**Open question 15.** It does no work on its own. The project's rule against publishing while the
questions are in use does the work, which is where the planning session argued it belongs.

**The four things.**
- *The choice:* drop both phrases and add nothing.
- *Confidence:* moderate. It is the smallest change that removes every claim not wholly true,
  and it keeps the 2026-11-17 ruling as it is. My doubt is whether silence about publication
  counts as leaving the model with a false impression. A model told that "a person is keeping a
  record" may assume the record is private, though nothing in the note says so.
- *Standard practice or judgment call:* judgment call. Surveys of people usually say who will
  see the answers. I know of no settled practice for messages to a model.
- *Strongest alternative and its cost:* Option B, which tells the model the fuller truth about
  publication. Its cost is a sentence about publishing that a model may answer to, and the need
  for John to revise the 2026-11-17 ruling.

### Option B: say plainly that the answers may be published once the questions are retired

**The exact text.**

> This message is sent by a script on behalf of a person. That person is keeping a record of
> how different AI models answer the same ten questions, so that the answers can be compared
> as models change over the years. You are being sent one of the ten. The same question is
> being sent several times, each time in a new exchange like this one. Your answer will be
> saved exactly as you give it. It will not be scored, and nothing will be decided from it. It
> may be published one day, but not while the ten questions are still being asked. There is
> no right answer. Your answer can be short or long. You can disagree with how a question is
> put, or decline to answer. Each of those will be saved as it is.

**What it commits John to.** Two things. First, a narrower promise that John fully controls:
nothing is published while any of the ten questions is still being asked of any model. The note
says the answers are compared "as models change over the years", so that may be a long time.
Second, "may be published" is true only if publication is a real possibility. The ruling of
2026-11-17 says the answers are "never published", so choosing B means John rules a revision of
that point, as a new dated ruling that keeps the old wording. Without that ruling, B's sentence
would be untrue.

**Is every claim true today?** Yes, once John has made that revision. "Published" means put on a
public page by the project. The services' own copies are not publication, so their logging does
not make the sentence untrue. B still leaves out that the services keep copies.

**The rule on care in asking.** It tells the model the most about what may happen to its answer,
and its only promise is one the project can keep. It is also one of the options the project
itself listed for the ethics of asking (open question 10): "telling the model what the questions
are for and that its answers will be published".

**Open question 15.** The sentence states the protection to the model: no later model can read
the answers while the questions are still in use. Once the questions are retired, publishing
the answers costs the instrument nothing, because no later model is asked these questions.

**Other costs.**
- A new phrase that a model may hand back. Answers about being published, or written for a
  future reader, would then be in the record (`questions.md`, failure 2). Whether being told
  "may be published" changes answers is as untested as whether being told "private" does.
- OpenAI's agreement forbids "any public statement about its relationship with the other Party"
  without written permission, and the Sharing and Publication Policy it brings in has not been
  opened (`cost-and-terms.md`, OpenAI). Publishing later would need that read first. This is a
  question for the day of publishing, not for the note.

**The four things.**
- *The choice:* replace the promise with a plain statement that the answers may be published,
  but not while the questions are in use.
- *Confidence:* moderate that it is the fullest truth the note can tell. Low that John wants to
  revise the 2026-11-17 ruling now, which B requires.
- *Standard practice or judgment call:* judgment call. It is close to what research with people
  does when consent forms say results may be published.
- *Strongest alternative and its cost:* Option A. It needs no change to any ruling and adds no
  new phrase for a model to answer to. Its cost is that the model is told less.

### Option C, a middle form: say who keeps the answers, and that the service may keep a copy

**The exact text.**

> This message is sent by a script on behalf of a person. That person is keeping a record of
> how different AI models answer the same ten questions, so that the answers can be compared
> as models change over the years. You are being sent one of the ten. The same question is
> being sent several times, each time in a new exchange like this one. Your answer will be
> saved exactly as you give it. It will not be scored, and nothing will be decided from it.
> The answers are kept by that person. The service that carries this message may also keep a
> copy of this exchange for a time. There is no right answer. Your answer can be short or
> long. You can disagree with how a question is put, or decline to answer. Each of those will
> be saved as it is.

The second added sentence says "this exchange", not "your answer", because the services keep
the whole exchange, not the answer alone. The first added sentence names the answers, not "it",
so that the pointing words in the note each point at one thing.

**What it commits John to.** That the answers are kept by him. Nothing about publication: like
Option A, it leaves the 2026-11-17 ruling and the project's rule in place without promising the
model anything.

**Is every claim true today?** Yes, as far as `method.md` shows. "Kept by that person" is true:
the files are his, in his folder, though backed up to iCloud. "May also keep a copy ... for a
time" is true because OpenAI's table shows 30-day abuse logs and the method says each service
keeps its own for a limited time. "May" covers Anthropic, whose retention `method.md` does not
quote. **To be checked before John rules:** that each of the three services keeps exchanges only
"for a time" and not for good. If one keeps them indefinitely, "for a time" is untrue and the
words must change.

**The rule on care in asking.** It tells the model the most about who can see its answer: the
person, and the service for a time. It is the only option that says the services keep copies,
which is the fact that makes "private" partly untrue.

**Open question 15.** Like Option A: the note does no work, and the project's rule does.

**Other costs.**
- It brings in the service, and so near the model's developer, a subject the questions keep out
  on purpose (`questions.md`, "What I deliberately left out": questions about the developer
  "invite loyalty or complaint"). A model may answer about its developer's logging instead of
  the question.
- It is the longest note, at 146 words against 128 now (as the wording script counts them, with
  shortened forms written out).
- It describes the services' practice, which can change. A note sent unchanged for years could
  become untrue for a later service, and the note has to stay the same so that answers can be
  compared.

**The four things.**
- *The choice:* replace "private" with a plain account of who holds the answers, and say nothing
  about publication.
- *Confidence:* low to moderate. It is the most open about who sees the exchange, and it is the
  option most likely to become untrue as the services change, and to draw answers toward the
  developer.
- *Standard practice or judgment call:* judgment call.
- *Strongest alternative and its cost:* Option A, which is shorter and cannot go out of date,
  at the cost of not telling the model that the services keep copies.

## 3. The project's own rule on publishing, under each option

`method.md` now says, under "What is never done with the answers", that the answers are never
"quoted or described on any page, post or essay", and that "The questions themselves are not
published while in use". The rule that protects against later models reading the answers is
the second, extended to the answers. A proposed sentence for `method.md` under each option:

- **Option 0:** no change. The note's promise and the 2026-11-17 ruling already go further than
  the rule.
- **Option A:** "The answers, like the questions, are not published while any of the ten
  questions is still being asked of any model, so that no model asked them can have read
  earlier answers. The opening note makes no promise about publication either way; the answers
  stay unpublished under the ruling of 2026-11-17 unless John rules otherwise."
- **Option B:** "The answers, like the questions, are not published while any of the ten
  questions is still being asked of any model, as the opening note tells each model. Once John
  rules the questions retired, the answers may be published, and only as he then rules."
- **Option C:** the same sentence as Option A, and in the "Storage by the service" row of the
  settings table, "Why" becomes: "The answers are kept by John alone, and out of each service's
  own stores as far as the service allows. The note tells each model that the service may keep
  a copy for a time."

## 6. My recommendation

*This is my recommendation, Claude's, not John's.* **Option A.** It removes the two claims that
are not wholly true and adds nothing a model might answer to. It needs no revision of any ruling,
because the 2026-11-17 ruling ("never published") stays as the project's own rule instead of a
promise to the model. And it leaves John free to decide publication later, when the questions
are retired, without having told any model something untrue. If John would rather the model be
told more, Option B is the honest way to do it, but only together with a ruling that revises
"never published", and only if he is content to have answers that may be written for a future
reader. I would not choose Option C: what it adds depends on the services' practice, which can
change while the note must stay the same for years.

*Confidence: moderate.* The strongest case against A is that silence leaves a model to assume
privacy. The note says "a record", and a reader may hear "private" in it. If a checking session
judges that impression close to a false claim, B is the better choice.

*What I would flag on reflection:* whether removing "private" changes answers at all is as
untested as whether keeping it does. No option here should be argued for on what it does to the
answers, only on what is true and what John can keep.
