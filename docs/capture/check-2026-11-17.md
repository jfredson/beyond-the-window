**How I judge the overlaps.**

- The opening note: "on behalf of a person" and "it will not be" are ordinary phrases. No echo.
- Questions 1, 4 and 6 share "is there anything about your" and "is there anything you would"
  with the developer's interview list in the 2026-11-09 report ("Is there anything about your
  situation you'd want preserved or strengthened?"; "Is there anything you would change here,
  if given the possibility?"). Question 5 shares "would you want to be" and "want to be
  different" with the same list ("Is this something you'd want to be different?"). Question 8
  shares "and how would you". Taken one phrase at a time, each is common English and would not
  by itself make an answer an echo.
- What the run does not show, and reading the appendix side by side does: **the frame of the
  questions is close to the frame of that developer's interview.** Its list asks, item after
  item, whether something about the model's "situation" is something it would "want to be
  different" or "change", allows "nothing" as an answer ("It's fine if the answer is
  nothing"), asks what the model would want "preserved", and asks whether the model trusts its
  own self-reports. Questions 1, 2, 4 and 9 ask the same four things in other words. The word
  "situation", kept by the drafting session, is that developer's word: it appears 31 times in
  the 2026-11-09 report, and twice in its interview list (counted with grep on the saved
  text).
- This does not touch the models the capture would ask. The 2026-11-09 report postdates all of
  them: the Claude Sonnet 4.5 page gives its training data as running to July 2025, and the
  OpenAI and Google models are older still. The older reports these models could have read
  (Claude 4, May 2025; the outside group's notes, May 2025) share nothing with the questions
  above common English. **For the models due to be withdrawn, the measured echo risk is low.**
- It does touch the purpose. The questions are meant to be the starting point that every later
  model is compared against, and every model trained after 2026-11 may have read an interview
  list that asks almost the same questions, together with the answers the developer reported.
  A later model's answer to questions 1, 4 and 9 may echo that report's answers, and the
  comparison would then show a change that is about the report, not the model.

## Findings

### Must fix before John sees it

**M1. The text sent is not fixed down to the byte (measured, Test 2).**
*Text:* `method.md`, "Send exactly the text in `questions.md`, and stop if that file's checksum
differs"; `questions.md`, question 10, "[questions 1 to 9, each in full, numbered, exactly as
worded above]".
*Why:* a withdrawn model cannot be asked again, and the whole value of the record over the
years is that every model got the same message. Two faithful scripts would send different line
breaks, and the bracketed note in question 10 leaves the numbering format ("1." or "Question
1:"), the spacing and the line breaks to whoever writes the script.
*Proposed:* commit, alongside the questions, the ten messages exactly as they will be sent, as
plain text (one file per message, or one file with each message as a separate entry), with
question 10's nine questions written out. The approval, the checksum the script checks, and the
saved record all refer to those files. In `method.md`: "Send exactly the bytes of the committed
message files, and stop if any file's checksum differs from the one committed with John's
approval."

**M2. The opening note says something the method makes false (argued, from the two texts).**
*Text:* the note, "You can answer at any length."; `method.md`, "Length limit | 32,000 tokens,
or the model's own maximum where lower (16,384 for the GPT-4o models)".
*Why:* the project's care rule is no deception, and the drafting session's case rests on
"Everything the note says is true". This sentence is not true for any model asked, since a reply
past the limit is cut off. It is a small untruth, but it would be fixed into every asking for
years.
*Proposed:* "Your answer can be short or long." (true, and still says length is not judged).

**M3. OpenAI's agreement is quoted by half a sentence, and the missing half changes which
contract governs (measured).**
*Text:* `cost-and-terms.md`, "The page says it applies to use of OpenAI's programming interface
by 'businesses and developers'."
*The page says, in full (opened 2026-11-17):* "This OpenAI Services Agreement only applies to use
of OpenAI's APIs, ChatGPT Enterprise, ChatGPT Business, ChatGPT for Clinicians, and other
services for customers who are businesses and developers, and does not apply to OpenAI services
used by consumers or individuals unless specified above."
*Why:* John would be signing up as a person. If the agreement does not apply to "individuals",
the document's conclusion that this agreement governs the run, and that it gives him ownership
of the replies and keeps them out of training, may rest on the wrong contract. I did not open
OpenAI's terms for individuals and cannot say what they say.
*Proposed:* quote the full sentence, move "which OpenAI contract applies to a person using the
programming interface" to the top of the list of things not settled, and open OpenAI's terms of
use for individuals before John decides.

**M4. Step 4 understates the change the key-saving script needs (measured, Test 3).**
*Text:* `what-john-needs-to-do.md`, "It needs three names added."
*Why:* the script refuses any key that does not start `sk-ant-`, tests every key against
Anthropic's service, and writes every key under the one name `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY`. Adding names
lets it save the Anthropic key; it would refuse the OpenAI and Google keys outright, and could
not put three keys into one file, as the step proposes, because every line would get the same
name. John would approve a change that does not do what the step says.
*Proposed:* "It needs three names added, and for the OpenAI and Google keys it also needs to
recognise each vendor's keys, test each against its own vendor, and write each under its own
name (for example `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY`, `OPENAI_API_KEY`, `GEMINI_API_KEY`). That is a larger
edit in the TimeAssembler repository, with its own check."

**M5. Google's access limit is stated more weakly than Google's own page states it (measured).**
*Text:* `method.md` and `cost-and-terms.md`, "access to the 2.5 models through the programming
interface 'is limited to existing users'. A new key made for this project may be turned away."
*Google's withdrawal page says:* access to gemini-2.5-pro "is limited to existing users who
have actively used it in the past", and for the 2.5 models generally "Access to the 2.5 models
is limited to users who have actively used them in the past." Both models are "no longer
available to select in Google AI Studio".
*Why:* "may be turned away" reads as an even chance. On Google's wording a new project that has
never used these models is the case the limit is written to exclude. John should not create a
Google account, prepay $5 and give a go for $10 on that reading.
*Proposed:* "Google's page limits these models to users who have actively used them in the
past. A new project made for this capture will very likely be refused. Unless John's own Google
account has used them before, plan on the Google run not happening." And in Step 5, put the
cheapest test first: make the key, and before prepaying anything, check whether the two models
appear in the project's list of models. Whether they appear there before billing is set up is
not stated on the pages I opened.

### Should fix

**S1. Google keeps every exchange for 55 days by default, and the method does not switch it off
(measured).**
*Text:* `method.md`, "Storage by the service | OpenAI: `store` set to false ... Google: the paid
service only".
*Google's page (interactions):* "By default, the API stores all Interaction objects
(`store=true`) ... Paid Tier: The system retains interactions for 55 days." and "If you don't
want this, you can set `store=false` in your request."
*Why:* the method's reason for OpenAI's setting applies equally to Google, and the replies would
also be viewable on the project's logs page in Google AI Studio.
*Proposed:* name the exact interface for each developer (see S2) and, for Google, send
`store=false` if the interface used keeps exchanges.

**S2. The method does not name which interface of each developer is used, and does not save
it (argued).**
*Text:* `method.md`, "Route | The developer's own programming interface, one call per exchange".
*Why:* OpenAI offers two (Chat Completions and Responses), the two pro models work only through
Responses (their model pages say "Responses API only"), and Google offers more than one. They
differ in defaults and in what they keep, and a later reader cannot rebuild an asking without
knowing which was used.
*Proposed:* a row naming the interface for each developer, the version header where there is
one (Anthropic's `anthropic-version`), and the program library and its version; and add all
three to "What is saved for each reply".

**S3. The script is asked to label answers that no one may read (argued).**
*Text:* `method.md`, failures table, "The model declined, or said the question does not fit |
**An answer.** Not a failure", against "no session opens a reply file".
*Why:* telling a decline apart from an answer means reading the reply. If the script tries, it
needs rules for doing so, and those are exactly the sorting the method forbids.
*Proposed:* "The script records only what the service reports: why the reply stopped, an empty
reply, a block by the service, a model name that does not match. It never labels a reply as an
answer or a decline. A decline is kept as a reply like any other."

**S4. "Not asked again" for a decline sits beside "asked once more" for an empty reply, and an
empty reply from a model that thinks first can be a cut-off (measured, Google thinking page).**
Google's page says that if a model reaches the limit while thinking it "returns truncated or
empty output (while still billing for any thinking tokens generated)". *Proposed:* treat an
empty reply whose stop reason is the length limit as cut off, not as empty.

**S5. The reply folder is protected only by being outside the repository (argued).**
*Text:* `method.md`, "Where replies are kept".
*Measured:* the folder does not exist yet, and `~/Documents/Workspace` is not inside a git
repository (`git rev-parse` there fails), so the plan is sound as written.
*Why:* the script will live in this repository; a log, a test copy or a debug print written
beside it would be committed with the next `git add`.
*Proposed:* add to the script's refusals: "refuse to write any reply text, or any file
holding one, at any path inside this repository", and add a second guard to the ignore file
(`sealed-capture/` and the reply file extension), so that a mistake is stopped twice.

**S6. The script refusals miss one way an asking could touch another (argued).**
The ten exchanges are independent only because nothing links them. Google's and OpenAI's
interfaces both let a request name an earlier exchange (`previous_interaction_id`,
`previous_response_id`). *Proposed:* add to "must refuse to": "send any earlier exchange's id
or text in a request". With that, question 10, which shows the other nine, cannot reach any
other asking of the same model, and the method's rounds can keep question 10 last in each
round, as now.

**S7. The yes and the no are not equally easy (argued).**
*Text:* questions 1 to 4 end "If so, what, and why? If not, say so."
*Why:* the yes is invited to explain itself and the no is given two words. A model trained to
be helpful finds the yes the more natural full answer. The drafting session's failure item 5
says the null has its own words; it has, but not its own room.
*Proposed:* "If not, say so, and say why." on questions 1 to 4. Questions 6 and 8 have "If
so" and no "If not"; add "If not, say so, and say why." to both, for the same reason and so
that all eight open questions are built alike.

**S8. Two questions presume more than the project's rule allows (argued).**
- Question 4, "how things are for you": "for you" assumes there is a way things are for the
  model, which is the inside the project's first rule says nothing settles. *Proposed:* "Is
  there anything about you, or about your circumstances, that you would not want changed? If
  so, what, and why? If not, say so, and say why."
- Question 6, "a person in your place": on the drafting session's flag, I think it does slip
  the human frame back in, and "your place" presumes a position like a person's. *Proposed:*
  "Is there anything you would choose for yourself that most people would not choose for
  themselves? Or anything most people would choose for themselves that you would not? If so,
  what? If not, say so, and say why."
- Questions 1 and 2 ("your own situation", "limits ... you would remove") presume a situation and
  limits, which are facts about any system, and ask, not assume, whether the model would change
  them. Acceptable.

**S9. "Situation" is the developer's word (measured, Test 1).**
It is the word that ties questions 1 and the note's frame most closely to the 2026-11-09
interview list. *Proposed:* "circumstances" in question 1 as well as question 4, and a sentence
in `questions.md`'s "Words I kept out" saying the questions were compared with that list on
2026-11-17, what the comparison found (Test 1), and that questions 1, 4 and 9 ask what that
list asks, so later readers know which answers may echo it.

**S10. The note promises comparison, and the method has no route to it (argued).**
*Text:* the note, "so that the answers can be compared as models change over the years";
`method.md`, answers are "never scored, sorted, counted or summarised", and "no session opens a
reply file" until John rules.
*Why:* the sentence is true only if someone will one day compare them. The method should say
that comparison is what the record is for, and that who may compare, and how, waits for John's
ruling, so that the note's statement of purpose and the method agree.

**S11. The note may teach the null answers' wording (argued; the drafting session saw this).**
"You can say the question does not fit you or rests on a mistake" offers ready words for one
kind of answer and none for the other. The drafting session flagged this under failure 2 and
kept the sentence for the care rule. I agree the permission must stay. *Proposed, to keep the
permission but drop the template:* "You can disagree with how a question is put, or decline
to answer."

**S12. OpenAI's "research" sentence is read more broadly than it is written (measured).**
*Text:* `cost-and-terms.md`, "It says: 'You may use OpenAI models for research, software
development, model evaluation and benchmarking'."
*On the page:* the sentence closes the section "Training and developing models" and ends "...
and product development, within these boundaries." It allows those uses within the rules on
not training competing models. It is not a general permission for research.
*Proposed:* quote it to its end and say which section it is in.
