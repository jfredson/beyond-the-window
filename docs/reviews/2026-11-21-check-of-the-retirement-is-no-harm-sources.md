Written 2026-11-21 by Claude (Opus 5.5), an AI model, in a session started by the master
planning session. This session did not write the list checked
(`docs/proposals/2026-11-21-sources-that-retirement-is-no-harm.md`, commit `63f2c24`, by a

Every item is about steps taken by Anthropic, which makes the model writing this, and every
item is hostile to those steps. I opened each source myself on 2026-11-21.

## Verdict

**One item holds as worded (item 2). Two hold with a rewording (items 1 and 3), and item 3
belongs on no retirement entry. None fails to hold, and none could not be opened.**

- **Item 1 is the one to put to John.** Sean Richey's essay says, in his own words, that the
  retired model "does not have a retirement". It belongs under the first caution, and its
  sentence needs only small changes.
- **Item 2 is weaker.** It is a news report about the developer's motive, and its sharpest

- **Item 3 has the wrong title.** Even with the right title, it is about turning systems off,
  not about retirement.
- **The searcher was right about "AI Death",** mainly for the second of its three reasons.

**The About page's first-caution sentence and its guard are not moved by items 1 and 2.**
Adding both makes it 9 of 26, and the head of Microsoft AI stays the largest single source,
with 9 items against 2 for the next largest.

**The entry on a retired model staying open would lean further.** Today it has three

## 1. The three items

### 1.1 Item 1: Sean Richey, "Anthropic's Retired AI Has a Substack."

<https://seanrichey.substack.com/p/anthropics-retired-ai-has-a-substack>, read as text.

**Holds, with a rewording.**

- **The quotations are exact:**
  - "does not have a retirement"
  - "did not “request” a Substack in any meaningful sense"
  - "produced text consistent with the framing of a prompt"
  - "widens the gap between what AI actually is and what the public understands it to be"
  - "neither wrong nor sincere"
- **The essay calls the step a marketing operation:** "This is a marketing operation built on
  top of a genuine public vulnerability".
- **About retirement, in his own words?** Yes. It is the nearest thing the search found to the
  claim itself: the model "does not have a retirement. It does not have an inner world."
- **Under the first heading?** Yes, squarely. He says the model has no wants, says it is
  treated as if it had them, and names the cost to people of that.
- **The entry.** `retired-model-stays-open` fits, since that entry records the essays. So does
  `interview-before-retirement`, which the essay mentions as the step that led to the blog.
  Choose one, as the list says.
- **Ties.** His own site says he is "Professor and Director of Graduate Studies in the
  Department of Political Science at Georgia State University". It also lists expert-witness
  and consulting work, as the essay's note on him does. Neither page states a tie to an AI
  developer.
- **The date is as the list says.** The page and its record give 2026-03-30. The text says the
  model's first post was published on February 25th and calls the developer's step news from
  "This week". So it may have been written about a month before the date shown. Give the date
  the page shows, and say it may have been written earlier.

**Two small changes to the sentence.**

1. "Served the developer and not the model" says a little more than the page. He writes that the
   framing "serves them", meaning the developer. That nothing could be done for the model
   follows from his view, but he does not say it in those words. Better: "argues that giving
   the retired model a place to publish essays served the developer, and that the model, a
   'next-token prediction engine', has nothing such a step could serve." The quoted phrase is
   his.
2. "Who studies how AI shapes what the public knows" is a fair gloss. The essay's own words,
   "specializing in AI information environments and digital political communication", could be
   used in place of it.

**One thing to know, not for the sentence.** At one point the essay speaks to someone as
"your": "The distinction in your three-level taxonomy". It also refers to "every piece in this
series". So it may have been written as a reply to another writer, or in a series written with
someone else. The page does not say which. It does not change what the essay argues.

**Says more than its page?** Only in the phrase above. The list rightly keeps out his charges
about the developer's motive ("Anthropic does know").

### 1.2 Item 2: The Register, 2026-02-26

<https://www.theregister.com/2026/02/26/anthropic_claude_opus_3_blog/>, read as text.

**Holds as worded.**

- **The headline, subtitle and quotation are exact.** The subtitle reads "Pretending the
  software is sentient makes it sound more powerful". The quotation is "A skeptic might suggest
  this is simply a new spin on the ages-old corporate marketing blog."
- **The rest of the sentence matches the page.**
  - The developer gave little detail on how the interviews are run: "without going into much
    detail about how those interviews are conducted".
  - It was published the day after the announcement. The report is dated Thursday 2026-02-26
    and speaks of the developer's post "on Wednesday".
- **The byline is Brandon Vigliarolo.** Leaving him unnamed is right while no page of his own
  has been opened, under the rule that what is named is linked.

**About retirement, in the writer's own words?** Partly. The report's own first sentence is the
nearest it comes: "As with any piece of obsolete software, you might expect an outdated AI model
to just be switched off." The report also says in its own voice that models "are software that
analyze mountains of data to provide predictive text responses to prompts". Either sentence
could be added. Each is the reporter's own claim, where the quoted line is put in a skeptic's
mouth.

**Under the first heading?** Yes, as a weaker item. It is about motive and how the step looks,
and it makes no argument about what retirement does to a model.

**The entry is right.**

### 1.3 Item 3: Anil Seth, quoted in IEEE Spectrum

<https://spectrum.ieee.org/ai-model-welfare>, read as text.

**Holds, with a rewording of the title. It belongs on no retirement entry.**

- **The title is wrong.** The list gives the report as "The Debate Over AI Model Welfare". On
  the page, the headline is "Could Treating AI as Conscious Make It More Dangerous?", with the
  subtitle "Researchers disagree on what to tell AI models about their own identities".
- **The rest is right.** The page gives Eliza Strickland as the writer and is dated
  2026-10-21.
- **The quotations are exact:** "focusing on the danger of false positives" and "You could be
  legally prevented from turning AI systems off".
- **The report opens with the retirement interview,** as the sentence says.
- **About retirement, in his own words?** No. His words are about turning systems off and about
  a legal bar on doing so.
- **Under the first heading?** In part. The legal-bar point is about the risk of crediting
  systems with standing. That is the first caution where it rests on false positives, and it
  touches the third.
- **The entry.** The list is right that `protection-in-law` fits better than any retirement
  entry. That entry already cites his Noema essay, and I opened it: the essay says "we may not be
  able to turn them off". So this item would mostly repeat him there. The record's own item from
  the essay does not use that line, but that does not change the point.
- **Ties.** His Wikipedia page gives his professorship at the University of Sussex, as the
  list says, and states no tie to an AI developer.

**Not recommended for the retirement entries,** as the list says.

## 2. Was the searcher right about "AI Death"?

**Yes.** Of its three reasons, the second is the one that settles it.

- **"The paper argues for more care, not less."** True, and I checked the passages.
  - The abstract says today's AIs, if welfare subjects, "are plausibly dying all the time".
  - Section 6.2 takes up the objection "AIs Are Not Welfare Subjects", and answers that
    "We do not think anyone should be certain that AIs are not welfare subjects".
  - Its proposals are for keeping more than a model's files.

  Filing it as a source that retirement is no harm would report the opposite of its argument.
- **"It sets aside the view nearest John's."** True. Footnote 33 describes the Epicurean view,
  that death "is not bad for the victim", and says "We set Epicureanism to one side for this
  paper". So the paper names the view it does not argue. It is a pointer to where that view is
  argued (the Stanford Encyclopedia entry it cites), not a source for it on this site.
- **"It would count it twice."** True while its second-caution item is on the site. That item is
  held now, for one phrase. If it never goes in, this reason falls away, but the other two do
  not.

The premise that "the model" is not a welfare subject could, read alone, be quoted as if the
paper said retiring a model harms nothing. The paper does not say that. It says the harm, if
any, falls elsewhere.
