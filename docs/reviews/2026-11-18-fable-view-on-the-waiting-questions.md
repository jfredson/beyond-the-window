## 1. The opening note's promise (new checksums needed by 2026-11-27)

**My view: remove "It will not be published", and the word "private" with it (Option A), on one
condition that only John can confirm.** I agree with the Opus master and the drafting session on
the answer. My reason is different, and so is the condition.

**The reason I find strongest, which the earlier arguments did not make.** A promise is
something the other party can release you from. A model that has been withdrawn cannot be asked
to release John from anything. So "It will not be published", said to these models, is the one
kind of promise the project can never revisit by asking. The ruling of 2026-11-17 ("never
published") is different in kind: it is John's own rule, and he can change it by a new dated
ruling. Option A keeps the rule and stops turning it into a promise. Nothing about how the
answers are actually handled changes on the day the note changes.

**Why it matters in practice.** The essay gives as a reason for the whole project that "this
record cannot be made later". These would be the only word-for-word answers from these models
under a fixed set of questions. A promise never to publish, with the method's rule that the
answers are never "quoted or described", means the project could never show them or even say
what a comparison found. I think that is the future John is most likely to regret.

**The condition.** Silence is honest only if the project has no present intention to publish.
If John already expects to publish these answers one day, then dropping the promise quietly, in
order to keep that door open, is the choice that would look worst afterwards. In that case
Option B (telling the model the answers may be published) is the honest note, and it needs him
to revise the 2026-11-17 ruling now. So the real question for John is not about wording. It is:
**as things stand today, do you intend these answers to stay unpublished, while wanting to stay
free to rule otherwise later?** If yes, Option A. If you already expect to publish, Option B.
If you are sure you will never publish, the check's missing option (drop only "private", keep
the promise) is the right one.

**Where I differ from the earlier sessions.**

- **"Private" is not the real problem.** The check is right that "a private record" has a
  reading that is true today: a personal record that is not made public. I would not rest the
  case on that word. I would still drop it under Option A, because "a private record" beside no
  promise would hint at the promise that was just removed.
- **Option B costs more than its draft says.** The check's finding 8 is right, and I would put
  it more strongly. Published answers from earlier models about what they would change or keep
  will be read by later models and can shape how they answer Still Asking, whatever its
  questions are. So "may be published once the ten questions are retired" does not make
  publishing free. That is a reason for the project's rule to stay strict for a long time, and
  it is a reason not to pick B unless John really does expect to publish.
- **Two other sentences are promises of the same kind, and nobody has put them to John.** "It
  will not be scored, and nothing will be decided from it" also has no end date and is also said
  to a model that cannot release him. I would keep both. But the comparison method that is
  still to be written must then describe and never grade, and nothing about any model may be
  decided from the answers. John should know he is making these two promises as well.
- **I would not argue from what the promise does to the answers,** and here I agree with the
  drafting session. One thing is worth knowing all the same: telling a model its answer is
  private suggests it has a different answer for when it is not. A project that takes no
  position on whether a model has an inside is slightly more neutral without that suggestion.
  This is untested, and I give it no weight in the recommendation.

**The four things.**

- *The choice:* Option A, on the condition above.
- *Confidence:* moderate to high that the promise should go. Moderate on A over B, because
  that turns on John's intention, which I do not know.
- *Standard practice or judgment call:* judgment call. Research with people usually says who
  will see the answers, which favours saying something. No settled practice exists for messages
  to a model.
- *Strongest alternative and its cost:* change nothing. It needs no rebuild, no check and no
  new approval, with nine days left before 2026-11-27, and it matches the 2026-11-17 ruling word
  for word. Its cost is a promise that can never be revisited, made before John has said he
  means it for good.

**On the process.** The check says the options draft needs a fix round and another check before
John sees it. I would not spend the nine days that way. If John answers the question in bold
above, the choice is made, and the only work left is the edit, the rebuild, one check and his
approval of ten new checksums.
