## 2. The entry on checked self-reports (`53d9431`)

**What changed.** The description's last two sentences were in the site's own voice: "A
correct report shows the model can read something about its own state. It does not show that
anything is felt." They now read:

> The developer whose researchers ran such a test says its results suggest that models have
> "some genuine capacity to monitor and control their own internal states", and calls that
> capacity "highly unreliable and limited in scope". It says the results do not show whether
> any AI system might be conscious.

**I opened the developer's page** (`https://www.anthropic.com/research/introspection`,
published 2025-10-29) and read it whole.

| The new wording | The developer's page | Holds? |
|---|---|---|
| Its results "suggest that models have" the capacity | "Taken together, our experiments suggest that models possess some genuine capacity to monitor and control their own internal states." | Yes. The quoted words are exact, and "suggest" is the page's own word |
| It calls that capacity "highly unreliable and limited in scope" | "We stress that this introspective capability is still highly unreliable and limited in scope" | Yes, exact. It is said earlier on the page, of the same findings |
| The results do not show whether any AI system might be conscious | "our results don't tell us whether Claude (or any other AI system) might be conscious" | Yes |

**Does it say no more than the page?** Yes. Each claim is the page's, in the page's words or
close to them.

**Conditional language.** Yes. The site no longer says in its own voice what a correct report
shows. It says what a developer says its results suggest, in quotation marks.

**The right party.** Yes. The study is the developer's own, on its own models, and the entry
says so where it stands: "Shown in research by one developer on its own models".

**Does the developer's name now appear anywhere it should not?** No. The new sentences say
"the developer" and name no one. They appear on the entry's own page and in that page's
description and link preview, and nowhere else: not on the list page, the home page or the
page of acts. The name is on the entry's page where it already was. The built-site check
passes.

**Is the second half still said, and by whom?** Yes, in a changed form, and now by the
developer and not by the site.

- *Before:* the site said a correct report "does not show that anything is felt".
- *Now:* the developer is reported as saying its results do not show "whether any AI system
  might be conscious".
- *What that means.* The limit is now given by the party that made the claim. That is honest
  attribution, and it does leave the limit resting on the developer's word. The site still
  says in its own voice, on every entry page, that none of the kinds of evidence "settles
  whether an AI model wants anything, in either direction", and on the About page that none
  "is treated as settling anything". So the limit is not lost from the page.

**Does any other source on the entry dispute the reading?** None disputes this study. The
entry has three things that bear on it:

- A paper by two researchers at another developer, which argues that one kind of self-report
  by a model "is not introspection" and that another can count as a minimal case. It is about
  other tests, not this one.
- A caution from the developer's own page: when the researchers put a word into the
  transcript and planted the matching pattern, the model "accepts the response as its own"
  and gave a made-up reason.
- A line under what could not be confirmed: "An outside safety researcher's reading of this
  study. One search found none."

**Does the description misrepresent that by silence?** Not as far as the record goes. It
reports one party's reading as that party's, with that party's own two limits, and the page
says below that no outside reading was found and that no outside group has repeated the
result. The ruling says the reading is disputed by researchers who hold that such a result
shows nothing about a system reading itself. No such source is in the entry. If one is found,
it belongs on the page, and that is a search and not a rewording.

**A closer wording, offered.** Two things about the sentence as written:

- The first quotation is about a capacity "to monitor and control". The entry tracks only
  the monitoring: a report checked against a reading. The control comes from a different
  experiment on the same page.
- "That capacity" joins the conclusion at the foot of the page to a caveat near its head.
  They are about the same findings, so the join is fair, and a reader cannot tell they are
  two places.

Both halves can come from one passage near the head of the page, which says the research
"provides evidence for some degree of introspective awareness" in the developer's models, and
in the next sentence stresses that this capability "is still highly unreliable and limited in
scope". A wording from that passage:

> The developer whose researchers ran such a test says its research "provides evidence for
> some degree of introspective awareness" in its models, and stresses that the ability "is
> still highly unreliable and limited in scope". It says the results do not show whether any
> AI system might be conscious.

It says a little less than the wording as written, and it stays on what the entry tracks.
