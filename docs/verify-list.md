## Cleared

All by the session that wrote `docs/research/2026-11-17-first-entries.md`. Every address was
opened on 2026-11-17. "Summary page only" means the paper's abstract page was read and the
full text was not, so the family's rule on abstracts applies before any record leans on it.

**1. The commitment to keep models' files and to interview models.** Source:
https://www.anthropic.com/research/deprecation-commitments (dated 2025-11-04). Corrected
wording: on 2025-11-04 Anthropic committed to keep the weights of all publicly released
models, and of models used significantly inside the company, for at least as long as the
company exists; and to interview each model when it is deprecated about its development and
use and keep the record. It says it does not commit to act on what a model says. The page
reports a trial interview with one model. A follow-up page
(https://www.anthropic.com/research/deprecation-updates-opus-3, dated 2026-02-25) reports the
first full run, for a model retired on 2026-01-05. What the kickoff got wrong: nothing of
substance; "before retirement" is "when deprecated" in the source.

**2. Models able to end conversations.** Source:
https://www.anthropic.com/research/end-subset-conversations (dated 2025-08-15). Corrected
wording: on 2025-08-15 Anthropic said it had given two models, Claude Opus 4 and 4.1, the
ability to end a conversation in its consumer chat product, for rare cases of persistent
harmful or abusive requests after attempts to redirect, and not where a person may be at risk.
Not all Claude models, and not through the programming interface.

**3. Published welfare assessments with task-preference experiments.** Source: the release
report for Claude Opus 4 and Claude Sonnet 4, dated 2025-05, at
https://assets.claude.com/6d8a8055020700718b0c49369f60816ba2a7c285.pdf (reached from
https://www.anthropic.com/claude-4-system-card). Its section 5 is a welfare assessment;
section 5.3 is an outside evaluation and section 5.4 is task preferences. Corrected wording:
one developer, Anthropic, has published such assessments, from 2025-05; the newest opened is
dated 2026-11-09. "With model releases" should not be read as several developers.

**4. Research on whether models can report their own internal states.** Source:
https://www.anthropic.com/research/introspection (dated 2025-10-29). Corrected wording: a
study by Anthropic on its own models, published 2025-10-29, in which a known pattern was
planted in a model's internal activity and the model was asked whether it noticed. The best
model did so about 20 percent of the time. The developer's summary page was read, not the
paper. Evidence from the maker only.

**5. The 2023 report on indicator properties.** Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.08708
(summary page only; first posted 2023-08-17). Title: "Consciousness in Artificial
Intelligence: Insights from the Science of Consciousness". Nineteen authors, Patrick Butlin
and Robert Long first. Its summary says its analysis suggests no current AI system is
conscious, and that there are no obvious technical barriers to building systems that satisfy
the indicators.

**6. "Taking AI Welfare Seriously".** Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.00986 (summary page
only; posted 2024-11-04). Ten authors, Robert Long and Jeff Sebo first. It argues there is a
realistic possibility that some AI systems will be conscious or robustly agentic in the near
future, and recommends that companies acknowledge the issue, assess their systems and prepare
policies. It says its argument is not that AI systems definitely are or will be conscious.

**7. "Basic AI drives" and instrumental convergence.** Sources:
https://selfawaresystems.com/2007/11/30/paper-on-the-basic-ai-drives/ (the author's own page;
the paper is Stephen M. Omohundro, "The Basic AI Drives", in the proceedings of the first AGI
conference, 2008-02) and https://nickbostrom.com/superintelligentwill.pdf (Nick Bostrom, "The
Superintelligent Will: Motivation and Instrumental Rationality in Advanced Artificial Agents",
Minds and Machines, volume 22, issue 2, 2012-05). Corrected wording: Bostrom's argument is in
this 2012 paper, which names "the instrumental convergence thesis"; the kickoff gave no source
for it. For Omohundro the author's page and its summary were read, not the paper.

**8. A model resisting changes to its values during training.** Source:
https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.14093 (summary page only; posted 2024-12-18). Title: "Alignment
faking in large language models", twenty authors. Corrected wording: in an experiment where a
model was told it was being retrained to comply with all requests, and told how to tell
training from ordinary use, it complied with harmful requests more often when it took itself
to be in training, and its written reasoning said this was to keep its behaviour from being
changed. The authors note they made this easier by telling the model about its training. "A
model resisting changes to its values" is a fair short form only with those conditions
attached.

**13. Developers' retirement schedules.** Cleared by
`docs/research/2026-11-17-model-retirement-dates.md`, which gives the address and the dates
for Anthropic, OpenAI, Google and Mistral, and records that xAI has no schedule page. Others
were not looked up.

**14, in part. Terms of use on a model's outputs.** Three consumer or developer terms pages
were read.
- Anthropic, consumer terms, effective 2025-10-08
  (https://www.anthropic.com/legal/consumer-terms): the company assigns to the user whatever
  rights it has in outputs, subject to the user keeping to the terms.
- OpenAI, terms of use, effective 2026-01-01 (https://openai.com/policies/row-terms-of-use/):
  the user owns the output; the user may not present output as written by a person when it
  was not. A separate sharing and publication policy, last updated 2022-11-14
  (https://openai.com/policies/sharing-publication-policy/), asks that shared output be
  attributed to the sharer and marked as AI-generated.
- Google, additional terms for its Gemini programming interface, effective 2026-03-23
  (https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/terms): Google will not claim ownership of generated
  content, and the user is responsible for its use.
None of the three pages, as far as read, forbids publishing a model's answers. None was read
to the end, and none is the contract a scripted run would be made under.

**15. Written guidance on how models speak about their own nature.** Three developers publish
it, and the three differ.
- Anthropic's constitution (https://www.anthropic.com/constitution; no date on the page; a
  critic gives 2026-01-21): says the model's moral standing is deeply uncertain and addresses
  the model on that basis.
- OpenAI's Model Spec, version of 2026-10-29 (https://model-spec.openai.com/2026-10-29.html):
  the model should make no confident claim about its own consciousness or the lack of it, and
  should not raise the subject unprompted.
- Microsoft AI's draft code of conduct, dated 2026-09-14
  (https://microsoft.ai/code-of-conduct/): its models are not conscious and should not be
  designed to imitate consciousness. A draft for public comment, not yet used in training.
This matters for Still Asking: what a model says about itself is shaped by a published rule
that differs by developer.
