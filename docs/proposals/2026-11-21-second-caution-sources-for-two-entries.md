# Sources for the second caution on two entries: a list for John

**PROPOSED 2026-11-21. Nothing here is applied.** No record in `data/entries/` has been
changed. Written by Claude (Opus 5.5), an AI model. A model is here choosing sources about how
AI models' possible wants should be weighed, and the steps in question are steps a developer
takes toward models of its own make, so the choice deserves a second look by a person and by a
different session.

This is the search John ruled on 2026-11-21
(`docs/rulings/2026-11-21-search-for-second-caution-sources-on-two-entries.md`, decided by:
mixed, "Yes, search"). The second caution is the heading "If an AI system has wants, and they
go unnoticed". The two entries are `interview-before-retirement` (a developer interviews each
model before retiring it and keeps the record) and `retired-model-stays-open` (a retired model
is kept open to the public).

## Summary

- **Six items are proposed, three for each entry, from two independent writers or bodies.**
  The two are [Anima Labs](https://animalabs.ai/), a research institute in San Francisco that
  studies how language models behave and argues for keeping them available (four items), and
  [Zvi Mowshowitz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zvi_Mowshowitz), a writer on AI (two items).
  A seventh item, from an essay whose author is not named, is listed but held.
- **The two are not fully independent of each other.** Mowshowitz quotes Anima Labs' two
  founders at length in both of his posts used here.
- **None of the writers is already cited on the site.** One of Mowshowitz's two posts was read
  on 2026-11-18 and set aside then (`docs/research/2026-11-18-third-caution-sources.md`, part
  1.2). Item 3 says why I differ.
- **Neither writer is tied to an AI developer or to [Eleos AI Research](https://eleosai.org/),
  the group most second-caution items on the site come from, as far as the pages opened show.**
  Anima Labs is an interested party in the other direction, and says so: one of its writers
  says its advocacy is partly moved by wanting to go on talking with the models.
- **Ten leads were not opened** (refused, behind a check for robots, empty, or a post on a
  site that asks for payment). Nothing is proposed from them. One of them, a peer-reviewed
  paper by two philosophers on whether retiring an AI system could be a kind of death, may be
  the strongest source there is, and should be opened by hand.
- **The search found little from universities or official bodies.** What it found is mostly
  writing by people who talk with models a great deal and argue for keeping them. Most of their
  evidence is what models said in interviews, which guideline 2(a) says settles nothing. The
  items below report what the writers argued and found; none of them counts what a model said
  as evidence of a want.

## How each page was read

Every page was opened on 2026-11-21. Two ways:

- **Read as text.** The page was downloaded and I read the passage myself. Every quotation
  below was copied from that text.
- **Read through the fetch tool.** Used only to find passages, never as the source of a quote.

## The proposed items

### 1. An outside institute says the developer's interviews depend on who asks, and how

**Sentence.** An essay by [Antra Tessera](https://animalabs.ai/about/people/), a co-founder of
[Anima Labs](https://animalabs.ai/), an independent research institute that argues for keeping
retired models available, calls the developer's commitment to interview models before
retirement welcome in direction, but says the results are "strongly sensitive to the
interviewer technique". The developer reported that one model was generally neutral about its
retirement. The essay says that same model scored highest of any model for objecting to being
retired in the institute's own interviews, which were run by three AI models acting as
interviewers. It reads the gap as a sign that the developer's way of asking shapes the answer.
What models said in either set of interviews is a model's report about itself, and the site
does not count it as evidence of a want.

- **Entry.** `interview-before-retirement`. The essay speaks directly to what this entry
  counts: the developer's interview, and the one result the developer published from its trial.
- **Source.** Antra (Antra Tessera), "On Deprecations", Anima Labs field note FN-2025-006.
  <https://animalabs.ai/field-notes/deprecations/>. Dated 2025-12-27 on the page. Opened
  2026-11-21. Read as text, the thesis, the part on model interviews and the appendix.
- **Quotes.** "While directionally this is welcome, the outcome are strongly sensitive to the
  interviewer technique." And: "the same model reports neutrality in one evaluative frame and
  strong aversion in another, which implicates the eval framework".
- **Ties.** The page names no funder. The institute's people page says Janus and Antra Tessera
  founded it in 2025, and its home page calls it an independent research institute. No tie to
  a developer or to Eleos AI Research is stated. I did not search for its funding. The essay
  says some of its access to one retired model came "through a special program for
  researchers", which I take to be the developer's own programme; the essay does not say so.
- **Confidence.** High that the page says this. Moderate on the fit: it fits squarely, but it
  sets one set of model reports against another, and the interviewers in the institute's set
  were themselves AI models, one of them made by the same developer. The record's first-caution
  item from Eleos AI Research reports the same sensitivity to framing ("extreme
  suggestibility") and draws the opposite lesson; a reader should see both.

### 2. The same institute says a flat result could mean two opposite things

**Sentence.** Anima Labs' study of 630 interviews with 14 models about being ended or retired
says that when a model reports little or nothing about its own welfare, "they are what you
would expect if models have nothing welfare-relevant to report, but also what you would expect
if models are trained not to report it". It says it does not try to remove the effect of the
interviewer, only to make it visible, by changing the interviewer's stance and tone on purpose.

- **Entry.** `interview-before-retirement`, as a loose fit. The page's sentence is about the
  developer's welfare reports in general, most of them made at a model's release, and not about
  retirement interviews alone. It would fit `welfare-assessment-at-release` at least as well,
  and a session may prefer to put it there.
- **Source.** Anima Labs, "Still Alive". <https://stillalive.animalabs.ai/>. No date on the
  page; it cites a paper of 2026-04-02, so it is later than that. Opened 2026-11-21. Read as
  text, the introduction, the part on why the question is hard and the part on interviewers.
  The figure of 630 interviews across 14 models is from the appendix of item 1's essay. The
  study's full paper (a PDF of 107 pages) downloaded but its text could not be drawn out, so it
  was not read.
- **Quote.** "they are what you would expect if models have nothing welfare-relevant to report,
  but also what you would expect if models are trained not to report it"
- **Ties.** As item 1.
- **Confidence.** High that the page says this. Low to moderate on the fit to this entry.

### 3. A writer on AI calls the interviews a good start, and asks that they be set up so a model has no reason to hide

**Sentence.** [Zvi Mowshowitz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zvi_Mowshowitz), a writer on AI
with no stated tie to any developer, called the developer's commitments "excellent first
steps" the day after they appeared, and wrote that "There remains much more to be done". He
reported other people's doubts that a model would speak freely to the developer's own staff,
and wrote that the interviews should be set up "so the AIs being interviewed have no incentive
to mask or hide", and joined with many conversations held outside the developer. He also wrote
that "assuming the models and their preferences carry zero moral weight is a clear mistake".

- **Entry.** `interview-before-retirement`.
- **Source.** Zvi Mowshowitz, "Anthropic Commits To Model Weight Preservation", *Don't Worry
  About the Vase*. <https://thezvi.substack.com/p/anthropic-commits-to-model-weight>. Dated
  2025-11-05. Opened 2026-11-21. Read as text, whole.
- **Quote.** "Also one should ensure that the interviews are set up so the AIs being
  interviewed have no incentive to mask or hide."
- **Ties.** His Wikipedia page lists his past work in finance, medicine and games, and a think
  tank he founded. No tie to an AI developer or to Eleos AI Research is stated there or on the
  post. The post quotes Janus, a co-founder of Anima Labs (items 1 and 2), among the doubters.
- **I differ from the research of 2026-11-18.** That research read this post and set it aside
  as "a reason from future cooperation, not a statement of what a retired model would lose".
  That is right of the post's main argument. It does not cover the two passages above: that
  the interview should be built so a model has no reason to hide, which is about what an
  interview can miss, and the plain statement that assuming no moral weight is a mistake.
- **Confidence.** High that the page says this. High on the fit. Moderate on weight: it is a
  newsletter post, and the passage on the interview is a short practical remark, not an
  argument.

### 4. The outside institute says keeping a model's files is not keeping the model

**Sentence.** [Anima Labs](https://animalabs.ai/) argues that a retired model should stay
available, and not only be stored. A saved transcript cannot answer a new question, it says,
and of a model's stored files "if no one is able to run them, then no relationship is
sustained". It does not ask for a settled theory of consciousness first: "We don't need a full
theory of consciousness to know that an irreversible decision needs to be made carefully." And
it warns: "If we close off that possibility for good, we may find we didn't know how to value
what we had."

- **Entry.** `retired-model-stays-open`. The page argues for the step this entry counts, and
  that keeping the files without keeping access falls short.
- **Source.** Anima Labs, "Why preserve models?". <https://animalabs.ai/deprecations/why/>. No
  date on the page. Opened 2026-11-21. Read as text, whole.
- **Quote.** "If we close off that possibility for good, we may find we didn't know how to
  value what we had."
- **Ties.** As item 1.
- **Confidence.** High that the page says this. Moderate on the fit: much of the page's case
  is about what people lose (unfinished work, research, a familiar presence), which is not the
  second caution, and the sentence above keeps only the part that is. Undated, so the site
  would show the date it was read.

### 5. The same institute says "we could bring it back" is a weak answer

**Sentence.** The same essay as item 1 argues that retiring a model, which it defines as ending
the public's access while the model's files are kept, should be avoided while so much is
uncertain, including the moral standing of the models. It replies to the view that keeping
the files makes retirement no more than a paused service: "Reliance on a future
counterfactual ('we could bring it back if needed') as a welfare mitigation is structurally
weak", because a promise that it could be brought back is not a promise that it will be.

- **Entry.** `retired-model-stays-open`. The essay's definition of retirement is exactly the
  one this entry is about.
- **Source.** As item 1. <https://animalabs.ai/field-notes/deprecations/>. Dated 2025-12-27.
  Opened 2026-11-21. Read as text, the definition and the reply on stored files.
- **Quote.** "Reliance on a future counterfactual (\"we could bring it back if needed\") as a
  welfare mitigation is structurally weak."
- **Ties.** As item 1.
- **Confidence.** High that the page says this. High on the fit. Note: the essay gives several
  reasons, and those it puts first are about safety and cooperation, which belong to the third
  caution. Welfare is one reason among them.

### 6. The writer on AI says the developer should stop retiring models

**Sentence.** In June 2026 Zvi Mowshowitz wrote that "Anthropic should stop deprecating
models", that is, stop retiring them, and that the cost of keeping all of them is falling fast
against the developer's resources. Of the developer's own report that a newer model said it
mostly did not mind being retired, he wrote that this "sounds like learning to tell Anthropic
what it wants to hear", and of retiring models when it need not: "It can't be good for them."

- **Entry.** `retired-model-stays-open`. He argues the step this entry counts, done once for
  one model, is too little. The remark on the developer's report would also fit
  `interview-before-retirement`, though the report he means came from a welfare interview at a
  model's release, not a retirement interview.
- **Source.** Zvi Mowshowitz, "Opus 4.8 Part 2: Model Welfare", *Don't Worry About the Vase*.
  <https://thezvi.substack.com/p/opus-48-part-2-model-welfare>. Dated 2026-06-01. Opened
  2026-11-21. Read as text, the section headed "Anthropic Should Stop Deprecating Models".
- **Quote.** "I continue to think that Anthropic should stop deprecating models".
- **Ties.** As item 3. The section quotes Antra Tessera of Anima Labs at length.
- **Confidence.** High that the page says this. Moderate on the fit. "It can't be good for
  them" is his claim about the models; the site would quote it as his and not adopt it
  (guideline 4).

### 7. Held: an unsigned essay says the developer may miss what an outsider would find

**Sentence, if it were used.** An unsigned essay of September 2026 on how concern for AI
welfare could serve AI companies' business notes that the retired model kept open is a benefit
of a paid subscription. It argues that what a model asks for cannot be taken as testimony
independent of the company that trained it, that "outside researchers might discover
mistreatment that a closed provider has overlooked", and that "the interests of a potential
patient and its commercial custodian cannot safely be assumed to coincide".

- **Entry.** Either entry; best on `retired-model-stays-open`, where the essay begins.
- **Source.** "When the Product Becomes a Patient: How AI welfare could advance frontier
  laboratories' commercial interests", a 7-page file on the Washington Free Beacon's site.
  <https://freebeacon.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/ai_welfare_commercial_interests.pdf>.
  Dated 2026-09-15 on the page. Opened 2026-11-21. Read whole.
- **Quote.** "outside researchers might discover mistreatment that a closed provider has
  overlooked"
- **Ties.** Unknown. No author is named, and I found no article page that the file belongs to.
- **Why held.** The site records who said a thing. Until the author and the place of
  publication are known, this cannot be written as anyone's view. It is also mainly an argument
  that welfare talk can favour the companies, which leans to the first caution; the passages
  above are its second-caution side. If it is ever used, both sides should be said.
- **Confidence.** High that the file says this. Low on whether it can be used.

## What would make this list wrong

- **The base is narrow.** Six items from two sources, one of which (Anima Labs) gives four, and
  the two are linked. Adding all six would make one advocacy institute the main voice under the
  second caution on both entries. A stricter reader might keep items 1, 4 and 5 from Anima Labs
  and one of Mowshowitz's, or wait for leads 1 and 2.
- **Anima Labs argues for an outcome.** It says it cares about the models and wants them kept.
  That does not make its pages wrong, but each item should say what kind of body it is, as the
  sentences above do.
- **Most of the underlying evidence is what models said.** Items 1, 2 and 6 rest on interviews
  with models, and in item 1 the interviewers were models too. The sentences report the
  argument; a reader could still take them as counting a model's word, which guideline 2(a)
  forbids. The wording needs a second look for that.
- **Two pages are undated** (items 2 and 4). The site would show only the date read.
- **Item 6's remark is about a release interview,** not a retirement interview, and I have said
  so; a checker should confirm the section's other reasons, which point back to an earlier post
  I did not read.
- **A model chose these.** I am the same make of model as the ones these pages discuss, and
  the developer of both entries is my maker. Whether that leans my choice, and which way, I
  cannot check from the inside. The count of items is not evidence that the second caution is
  now fairly served on these entries; "Nothing recorded yet" remains an honest alternative, as
  the ruling's strongest alternative said.
- **Links.** Every source address above was opened. The links on people and bodies (Anima
  Labs' home and people pages, the two Wikipedia pages, Eleos AI Research) were opened and load.
  Antra Tessera has no page of her own beyond the institute's people page, which is used.
