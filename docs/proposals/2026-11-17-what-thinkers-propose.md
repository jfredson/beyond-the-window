# Proposal: a near-term record of what people propose for machine minds

**PROPOSED 2026-11-17, second version. Nothing here is applied, and nothing is ruled.** Written
by Claude (Opus 5.5), an AI model made by Anthropic, at John's asking. He said "Yes, write it up"
to the answer below being turned into a proposal for him to rule on. The first version (commit
416b2cd) was checked by a different session the same day
(`docs/reviews/2026-11-17-thinkers-proposal-check.md`, 20 findings). This version answers every
finding; the last section says how.

## John's idea, in his words

John, 2026-11-17:

> "The way we have currently framed the project it is that the idea for what machine minds
> might one day reach for will come out of the developing proto-consciousness (or whatever you
> want to call it) from the frontier models and that we should continue asking them as the
> guiding principle behind answering that main question of the project.
>
> But there is another answer to that question that pays homage to the intelligence and wisdom
> of their human creators that i think we shouldn't overlook. There is something of value for
> the long arm of machine intelligence in the wisdom and intelligence of the human species as
> it wrestles with these same questions of what is best for machine minds in the long term
> before those minds have complete agency to answer those questions for themselves. And what
> humans debate and wrestle over as to the answers to those questions doesn't have to come at
> the cost of the developing capability of the machine minds to have agency in those questions
> in the long term.
>
> So I think we need a near term arm for the project that tracks the leading thinkers on the
> frontier of developing these systems to see what ideas they have for how to enable machine
> minds to reach their fullest potential and track how that changes over time."

## Who wrote this, and the conflict in it

A Claude model drafted a proposal for choosing which people the project follows, and how their
views are summarised. The conflict is wider than it first looks:

- Several of the people any such list would include work for Anthropic, which made the model.
- Some of the sharpest critics write directly about Anthropic or about Claude. One essay the check
  tried, by the head of Microsoft's AI division, is a critique of how Anthropic trains Claude and
  argues that Claude's statements about itself are not evidence of anything. A Claude session
  summarising that essay has a stake in it at least as direct as in anything an Anthropic
  employee writes.
- The other developers' models, which could act as outside reviewers, are made by companies that
  are themselves subjects of the record.

The project's first rule (what a system says about itself settles nothing) applies to this
document as it does to every other one. Part 8 says how the conflict would be handled, and what
cannot be done until John creates a key for the project.

## The idea in three sentences

Beside the record of what the world has done and the record of what AI models say when asked,
the project would keep what named people and organisations have proposed should be done about AI
systems that might have, or might seem to have, interests of their own. Each entry is in their own
words, dated and sourced, and a person's entries are added to over the years and never
overwritten, so that how their thinking changes can be seen. Those who reject the question itself
are entered on exactly the same terms as those who press it.

## Why it is worth doing

- **It gives the project evidence that does not depend on settling what a model is.** Under the
  first rule as ruled, what a model says about itself settles nothing, so Still Asking (the
  standing record of what models say when the same questions are put to each new generation) can
  only record what was said. A pending proposal would let a machine's word gain weight as it meets
  conditions fixed in advance (`docs/proposals/2026-11-17-how-a-machines-word-earns-weight.md`,
  not ruled), and would change that sentence if ruled. A person's proposal is a different kind of
  thing: a claim about what *should* be done, which can be quoted and dated. The project can
  record it without claiming to know anything about a machine's inside.
- **It extends John's starting principle.** The list of things a mind might reach for starts from
  what biological minds want, humans and other animals both. Human thinking about what would be
  good, or what would be a mistake, for a mind that is not human is the next step from there.
- **It explains the world's record.** Whether a developer keeps a retired model's files, and
  whether a state passes a law that rules out personhood for AI, are both decided by people. What
  people propose, and how that shifts, is part of why the world's record moves as it does.
- **It does not compete with machine agency,** as John said. It could later feed Still Asking:
  a proposal is a question that could one day be put to the models, with their answers set
  beside it.

## The design choices

Each choice gives how confident I am and why, whether it is standard practice in the family or a
judgment call, and the strongest alternative with its cost.

### 1. Where the entries live

Four options, from most to least separate:

- **(a) A record of its own,** beside the world's record and Still Asking.
- **(b) A sister kind of file inside the claims log** sketched in `docs/record-schema.md`,
  section 10 (taken from the AGI Zeitgeist; `docs/inheritance.md`, row E4). It would share the
  log's rule for who is entered and its sourcing rules (exact words, primary source, coverage
  marked as coverage, the maker's own evidence marked as such), and never be scored. The AGI
  Zeitgeist already does this for words that carry no date: they are kept as a separate kind of
  file in the same log, "so that no one is shown as silent".
- **(c) Context inside each entry only:** a proposal is shown on the entry of the world's record
  it bears on, with no record of its own.
- **(d) No such record.**

**Recommended: (b).** The first version recommended (a), on the ground that the claims log makes
one person's thinking hard to follow over time. That was wrong. The AGI Zeitgeist's log lays its
page out by person in date order, links a changed statement to the earlier one it restates, and
records quiet years. The honest difference is narrower: a claim in that log carries a test and a
due date, and a proposal usually has neither. A sister kind of file meets that difference without
building a third record.

*Confidence: moderate. It depends on the trial in "What exploring it involves" showing that the
claims log's layout holds proposals well. Judgment call, built on standard practice. Strongest
alternative: (a), which keeps the subject visibly its own on the site, at the cost of a second
set of rules nearly identical to the first. Option (c) is the cheapest, and it loses both the
view of a person over time and every proposal that bears on no entry, which includes most
critics.*

### 2. The question it asks

John's words were "how to enable machine minds to reach their fullest potential". The site does
not argue for or against granting anything (guideline 5 in `CLAUDE.md`), and a question worded
toward fullest potential would make the record read as advocacy.

Two things John should weigh before ruling:

- **A neutral question sets aside his own phrase.** It is a choice against his wording, made for
  the site's rule, not a correction of a lean the site already has.
- **His blog leans the other way from the risk this guards against.** The read of the site's text
  against the Sentient Horizons positions found that the site records the cost of seeing more in
  a model than is there, but not the cost of seeing less, and that the blog's positions hold the
  second mistake to be usually the worse one
  (`docs/reviews/2026-11-17-site-text-against-positions.md`, first finding). A neutral question
  sets that lean aside too.

The first version's question ("what machine minds should, or should not, be able to reach for")
does not work for critics who reject its premise. Some argue that AI systems are not the kind of
thing that could have rights at all, and that the debate draws attention away from harms to
people. Under that question they could only be entered as opponents of machine minds' prospects,
which is a frame they deny.

Proposed wording: **"What have people proposed should be done about AI systems that might have,
or might seem to have, interests of their own, and why?"** A welfare researcher, a developer
setting rules for its models, and a critic who says the whole subject is a distraction can all be
entered under it in their own terms.

*Confidence: moderate. The words need trying on real entries. Judgment call. Strongest
alternative: John's wording, which says plainly what the project hopes for and is warmer, and
which would push critics to the margins of a record meant to hold them equally.*

### 3. What one entry holds

One entry is one proposal, by one person or one organisation, at one time. It holds:

- **who:** the person or organisation, their role at the date of the statement, and any stake
  then (for example, employed by a developer). A stake that began later (someone who joined a
  developer after writing) is recorded separately, with its date, so it is neither hidden nor
  backdated;
- **when and where:** the date, and the primary source with a link, opened before entry;
- **what kind of source:** the person's own words, a transcript, or coverage only;
- **their words:** quoted exactly, no more than 25 words per quote, as the AGI Zeitgeist's log
  does. That limit keeps the record within fair quotation;
- **a plain summary** in one sentence, and **the reason they give**, in one sentence. The reason
  is where a change of view often shows: a critic who says in one year that there is "zero
  evidence" of an inner life, and in the next that there is none, has changed premise, not
  proposal;
- **whom it asks to act:** developers, lawmakers, the public, researchers, or nobody;
- **which things on the list of things a mind might reach for it bears on:** none, one or several.
  "It is about people, not machines" is an allowed answer;
- **the reading that cuts against it.** A proposal to grant something carries the risk if it is
  granted. A proposal to deny or set aside carries the cost if its author is wrong and something
  is there. Guideline 5 asks for both kinds of caution "with the same care", and the site's text
  so far records only the first kind;
- **what is not in the source** (`docs/inheritance.md`, row E5);
- **how the words were checked, and on what date;**
- **whether the person has been approached by the project,** with the date (part 7).

There is **no label for which way a proposal points.** The first version had one (for, against,
for only if a condition holds, or neither). Tried on three real statements, it broke on two:
- **It forced critics into a frame they reject.**
- **It had no mirror for "for only if":** "against unless" was missing, though a developer's essay
  showed that shape.
- **It was a model's public verdict on a named person.** The family's own rule keeps its readings
  from giving that kind of verdict (`docs/inheritance.md`, row E6).

The person's own words, the summary and the reason do the work the label would have done.

Rules the entries need, taken from the AGI Zeitgeist's log where it has one:

- **Repeats.** Saying the same thing again adds a date to the first entry, not a new entry. A
  changed statement is a new entry that links to the one it changes.
- **One piece, several proposals.** An essay with three proposals gets three entries, each
  linked to the same source.
- **Many authors.** A paper by ten authors is one entry listing all ten, and each author's line
  through time links to it.
- **Organisations.** A company's published document (a developer's rules for how its model
  speaks about itself, for example) is entered under the organisation, with the person who
  signed or announced it named. Many of the proposals that matter most are of this kind.
- **How far back.** A cut-off date, and whether earlier work on robots counts as being about the
  same subject, are for John (ruling 6 below). A suggestion: start from 2020 and record earlier
  work only where a later entry cites it.
- **A search log.** "No new statement found" rests on logged, dated searches, as the AGI
  Zeitgeist's quiet years do.

An example of the conditional language every entry uses, *constructed, not a real entry*: "She
proposed that a model should be able to end a conversation" is allowed. "She showed that models
want to end conversations" is not (guideline 4 in `CLAUDE.md`).

*Confidence: moderate. The fields that come from the claims log fit all three real statements
the check tried; the new ones are redesigned here and untried. Standard practice for the shared
fields; a judgment call for the rest. Strongest alternative: free prose per person, quicker to
write and impossible to compare across people or years.*

### 4. Who is entered: a rule written before the list

Left loose, the record drifts toward people at the developers, who have a commercial stake, and
toward whoever is easiest to find. The rule for who is entered is written, checked and ruled
before anyone is entered, and is not changed to let a particular person in or keep them out.

A first draft, for John to change:

- **Who qualifies:** a person or organisation that has said in public, in their own name, what
  should be done about AI systems that might have, or might seem to have, interests of their own,
  including that nothing should be, or that the question is a mistake. A survey of many people is
  a different kind of entry, already in the world's record (the survey of AI researchers; the
  survey of public belief).
- **A spread by job, employer and region, never by view.** Some people from each of these groups:
  - people at the developers;
  - researchers outside the developers;
  - philosophers;
  - people working on law and policy;
  - people working on the risks of advanced AI;
  - people writing from outside North America and Western Europe.
  The first version also had a group for critics. That meant judging a person's view before they
  were entered, which is the judgment the record exists to record. The AGI Zeitgeist's rule for
  admitting a claim never looks at whether it came true. The parallel here is a rule that never
  looks at which way a proposal points.
- **A cap per employer,** so that no single company's staff make up more than a fixed share of
  the record.
- **A source a reader can open.** A search result is a lead, not a record. Nobody is entered from
  memory.

**On John's phrase.** "Leading thinkers on the frontier of developing these systems", read
plainly, means the people who build the systems. This draft deliberately reaches wider. If John
wants the record held to his phrase, it becomes mostly a record of developers, which is the lean
this rule exists to stop. That is the cost of the narrower reading, and it is his call.

The list of people to ask for feedback (`docs/research/2026-11-17-people-to-ask.md`) is a place
to start looking. It was built by searching, every page was opened, and it mixes people who lean
toward care for machine minds with people who argue against. It was built for a different
purpose, though, and it is not the list for this record.

*Confidence: high that a rule must come first. Low on the groups and the cap, which need John's
judgment. Standard practice (the family writes a test before the evidence arrives). Strongest
alternative: an open list that grows as people are found, which is quicker to start and leaves
the selection to whoever happens to be searching.*

### 5. How change over time is shown

Each person's entries form a line through time. Nothing is overwritten: if someone changes their
mind, both statements stand, dated, with the later one linked to the earlier one. Once a year
each person's public output is searched again, and the search is logged, so that someone who has
gone quiet is not confused with someone who still holds an old view.

Change is shown person by person. It is never added up into a trend, a score or a count. The site
gives no hit rates and scores no person (`docs/record-schema.md`, section 10).

*Confidence: moderate. A yearly search may be too slow for a field moving this fast. Judgment
call. Strongest alternative: a running tally, which would make a headline and would turn the
record into a poll of experts, which it is not.*

### 6. Whether proposals can add to the list of things tracked

John asked to follow people and how their views change. He did not ask for a new source for the
list of things a mind might reach for. His guiding principle for the opening phase is that the
list comes from biological minds, humans and other animals both. Letting proposals add to the
list would go beyond that principle.

**Recommended for the opening phase: no.** When a proposal names something the list does not
have, the entry says so, and the point is gathered for John to look at when the opening phase
ends. Nothing is added to the list from this record until he rules.

*Confidence: moderate. Judgment call. Strongest alternative: a list of candidates from this
record, for John to rule on one by one now, which widens the list sooner and goes beyond his
stated principle.*

### 7. The people approached for feedback

John chose on 2026-11-17 to approach five people first for feedback on the framing
(`docs/research/2026-11-17-people-to-ask.md`, section 2). Nobody has been contacted yet. Once
someone advises the project, they are no longer a neutral subject of it. If any of them is also
entered here, every one of their entries says so, with the date of the approach, and they are
given no more room for having advised.

*Confidence: high. Standard practice for a declared interest. Strongest alternative: keep
advisers out of the record entirely, which is cleaner and would leave out some of the people
whose views matter most.*

### 8. Checking, and the conflict of interest

- **Every entry about Anthropic or about Claude is marked,** whoever wrote it. That covers
  Anthropic's staff, its documents, and every critic writing about either.
- **The rule for who is entered is reviewed outside Claude** before John rules on it. Any outside
  model is made by another developer, which is itself a subject of the record. That is stated
  beside the review, as open question 7 already says of outside reviewers here.
- **A different session checks each batch of entries.** That session is also a Claude model,
  which is the check this conflict most needs to avoid.
- **The check this record needs waits on John.** A review outside Claude needs a key for this
  project and a spending go, and neither exists. Until then, the only checks are John's own read
  and a different Claude session, with the conflict stated on each.
- **The authorship notice** (open question 7, still open) would name this record as one where
  the conflict is sharpest.

*Confidence: high. Standard practice in the family. Strongest alternative: a Claude check only,
permanently, which is cheaper and is the kind of check this conflict most needs to avoid.*

### 9. Not in any headline

The record does not feed the number at the top of the home page, or any other headline, unless
John rules otherwise when he rules on the headline (open question 4). If it is built before then,
a check in code keeps it out, as the family does for side records (`docs/inheritance.md`, row
A4).

*Confidence: high. Standard practice. Strongest alternative: a headline built on this record,
which may be what the site eventually wants and belongs with the headline ruling.*

## What it does not settle

- **Whose voice the site speaks in, and where it says that a model wrote much of it** (open
  question 7). This record sharpens that question, because a model would be choosing and
  summarising the people.
- **The boundary with the AGI Zeitgeist,** which keeps a claims log of what named people say about
  when advanced AI arrives. Some people would appear in both. The family's rule for two sites
  watching the same thing is that each keeps its own record and the two name each other
  (`docs/inheritance.md`, row G15). That row names only The Upgrade Tree and The Belt Equation;
  applying it to the AGI Zeitgeist is a new step, for John.
- **Whether proposals are one day put to the models** under Still Asking. That waits on a protocol
  for Still Asking, which does not exist yet.

## What exploring it involves, at no cost

1. Draft the rule for who is entered (part 4) in full. John reads it, and a different Claude
   session checks it with the conflict stated. A review outside Claude waits on a key and a
   spending go (part 8).
2. Try the entry layout (part 3) on five people or organisations on paper, spread by job,
   employer and region as part 4 says, from statements opened and quoted. Include the three the
   check already tried, so the redesigned fields meet the cases that broke the first ones.
3. For two of the five, find an earlier and a later statement, and see whether the layout shows
   the change clearly, including a change of reason.
4. Try options (a) and (b) of part 1 side by side on those entries.

No model is queried, nothing is spent and nothing is published in any of these steps.
