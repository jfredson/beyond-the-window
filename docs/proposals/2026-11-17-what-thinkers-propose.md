# Proposal: a near-term record of what human thinkers propose for machine minds

**PROPOSED 2026-11-17. Nothing here is applied, and nothing is ruled.** Written by Claude
(Opus 5.5), an AI model made by Anthropic, at John's asking. He said "Yes, write it up" to the
answer below being turned into a proposal for him to rule on.

## John's idea, in his words

John, 2026-11-17:

> "The way we have currently framed the project it is that the idea for what machine minds
> might one day reach for will come out of the developing proto-consciousness (or whatever you
> want to call it) from the frontier models and that we should continue asking them as the
> guiding principle behind answering that main question of the project.
>
> But there is another answer to that question that pays homage to the intelligence and wisdom
> of their human creators that i think we shouldn't overlook. There is something of value for
> the long arm of machine intelligence in the wisdom and intelligence of the human species as
> it wrestles with these same questions of what is best for machine minds in the long term
> before those minds have complete agency to answer those questions for themselves. And what
> humans debate and wrestle over as to the answers to those questions doesn't have to come at
> the cost of the developing capability of the machine minds to have agency in those questions
> in the long term.
>
> So I think we need a near term arm for the project that tracks the leading thinkers on the
> frontier of developing these systems to see what ideas they have for how to enable machine
> minds to reach their fullest potential and track how that changes over time."

## Who wrote this, and the conflict in it

A Claude model drafted a proposal for choosing which human thinkers the project follows. Several
of the people any such list would include work for Anthropic, which made the model, and much of
what they would be quoted on is Anthropic's own conduct. That is a conflict of interest, and it
could bias the draft in either direction. The project's first rule applies to this document as
it does to every other one. Part 8 below says how the conflict would be checked.

## The idea in three sentences

Beside the record of what the world has done and the record of what models say when asked, the
project would keep a third record: what named people have proposed should be done for or about
machine minds in the long term, in their own words, dated and sourced. Each person's proposals
are added to over the years and never overwritten, so that how their thinking changes can be
seen. Proposals against granting machine minds anything are entered on exactly the same terms
as proposals for it.

## Why it is worth doing

- **It gives the project evidence that does not depend on settling what a model is.** Under the
  first rule, what a model says about itself settles nothing, so Still Asking can only ever be a
  record of what models said. A thinker's proposal is a different kind of thing: a claim about
  what *should* be done, made by someone who can be quoted, dated and held to it. The project
  can record that cleanly without claiming to know anything about a machine's inside.
- **It extends John's starting principle.** The starting list comes from what biological minds
  want, humans and other animals both. Human thinking about what would be good for a mind that
  is not human is the next step from there: our own minds' best attempt at the question, made
  before machine minds can answer it themselves.
- **It explains the world's record.** Whether a retired model's files are kept, whether a model
  can end a conversation, and whether the law protects or forbids personhood are all settled by
  people. What those people propose, and how that shifts, is part of why the world's record
  moves the way it does.
- **It does not compete with machine agency,** as John said. It can later feed Still Asking:
  each proposal is a question that could one day be put to the models, with their answers set
  beside it.

## The design choices

Each choice gives how confident I am and why, whether it is standard practice in the family or
a judgment call, and the strongest alternative with its cost.

### 1. A record of its own, kept apart from the other two

The project would keep three records, never mixed: the world's record (what happened), Still
Asking (what models said), and this one (what people proposed). The family already keeps what
people said apart from what the world did (`docs/inheritance.md`, rows E1 and E3).

`docs/record-schema.md`, section 10, already sketches a claims log taken from the AGI Zeitgeist
(`docs/inheritance.md`, row E4): what developers promise, what advocates argue, what critics
say, each with a test. That log is built to check promises against what then happens. A
proposal about what *should* be done is often not a promise and has no outcome to check, so it
fits the log badly. The proposal is a sister record that uses the claims log's sourcing rules
(exact words, primary source, coverage marked as coverage, the maker's own evidence marked as
such, no hit rates, no person scored), but that is laid out by person and by proposal and not by
promise.

*Confidence: moderate. A proposal and a promise are close, and a session working with real
entries may find one record does both. Judgment call, built on standard practice. Strongest
alternative: no new record. Proposals would go into the claims log as a new kind of claim,
which keeps one record fewer, but makes it hard to see one person's thinking over time, the part
John asked for.*

### 2. The question it asks, worded so it takes no side

John's words were "how to enable machine minds to reach their fullest potential". On the site,
that wording would take a side the project has ruled it does not take (guideline 5: the site
does not campaign). Some serious thinkers argue that machines should not be built to have wants
at all, or that attributing wants to them is itself the harm. The read of the site's text
against the Sentient Horizons positions found the site already records one kind of mistake more fully than the other
(`docs/reviews/2026-11-17-site-text-against-positions.md`).

Proposed wording for the record's question: **"What have people who think about these systems
proposed that machine minds should, or should not, be able to reach for, and on what
conditions?"** John's own words stay in this file as the origin of the idea. The public wording
would be the neutral one.

*Confidence: high. It follows from a ruled guideline. Judgment call on the words. Strongest
alternative: John's wording, which says plainly what the project hopes for and is warmer, and
which would make the record read as advocacy and push critics to the margins.*

### 3. What one entry holds

One entry is one proposal by one person at one time:

- who made it, with their role at the time and any stake (for example, employed by a developer);
- the date, and the primary source with a link, opened before entry;
- the proposal in their exact words, and a one-sentence plain summary;
- what it would have done for or about machine minds, and on what conditions, if any;
- which way it points: for, against, for only if a condition holds, or neither;
- which thing on the list it bears on, or "none yet" if it names something the list does not
  have;
- its safety reading, as every hopeful entry carries one (guideline 5);
- whether the person has been approached by the project, with the date (part 7).

Entries are written in conditional language only (guideline 4). "She proposed that a model
should be able to end a conversation" is allowed. "She showed that models want to end
conversations" is not.

*Confidence: moderate. The fields are a first guess, to be tried on real cases (see "What
exploring it involves"). Standard practice for the fields shared with the claims log; a
judgment call for the rest. Strongest alternative: free prose per person, which is quicker to
write and impossible to compare across people or years.*

### 4. Who is entered: a rule written before the list

Left loose, the record drifts toward people at the developers, who have a commercial stake, and
toward whoever is easiest to find. The rule for who is entered would be written, checked and
ruled before anyone is entered, and would not be changed to let a particular person in or keep
them out.

A first draft of the rule, for John to change:

- **Who qualifies:** a person who has said in public, in their own name, what should be done for
  or about machine minds' long-term interests or prospects, or that nothing should be. A survey
  of many people is a separate kind of entry, as the world's record already has (the survey of
  AI researchers; the public-belief survey).
- **A spread fixed in advance,** with at least some people from each of these groups: people at
  the developers; researchers outside the developers; philosophers; people working on law and
  policy; critics who argue against granting machine minds anything, or against building
  minds that could want; people working on the risks of advanced AI; and people writing from
  outside North America and Western Europe.
- **A cap per developer,** so that no single company's staff make up more than a fixed share of
  the record.
- **A view counts only if it has a source a reader can open.** A search result is a lead, not a
  record. Nobody is entered from memory.

The list of people to ask for feedback (`docs/research/2026-11-17-people-to-ask.md`) is a
natural place to start looking. It was built by searching, every page was opened, and it already
mixes those who lean toward caring for machine minds with those who argue against. But it was
built for a different purpose, and it is not the list for this record.

*Confidence: high that a rule must come first. Low on the groups and the cap, which need
John's judgment. Standard practice (the family writes a test before the evidence; the
first rule, part 4). Strongest alternative: an open list that grows as people are found,
which is quicker to start and leaves the selection to whoever happens to be searching.*

### 5. How change over time is shown

Each person's entries form a line through time. Nothing is overwritten. If someone changes their
mind, both statements stand, dated. Once a year each person's public output is read again. "No
new statement found" is recorded too, so that a person who has gone quiet is not confused with
one who still holds an old view.

Change is shown person by person and proposal by proposal. It is never added up into a trend, a
score or a count of who is for and who is against. The site gives no hit rates and scores no
person (`docs/record-schema.md`, section 10).

*Confidence: moderate. A yearly re-read may be too slow for a field moving this fast; a
twice-yearly one may be too costly in sessions. Judgment call. Strongest alternative: a running
tally of for and against, which would make a headline and would turn the record into a poll of
experts, which it is not.*

### 6. How it adds to the list of things tracked

When a proposal names something the list does not have, the proposal goes onto a list of
candidates for John to rule on. Nothing is added to the main list automatically. This gives the
list a third source, beside biological minds (John's starting principle) and the models' own
answers.

*Confidence: moderate. Judgment call. Strongest alternative: keep the list closed to this
record, so that the list stays built on biological minds alone. That is purer, and it loses the
thing John asked for.*

### 7. The people approached for feedback

John chose on 2026-11-17 to approach five people first for feedback on the framing
(`docs/research/2026-11-17-people-to-ask.md`, section 2). Someone who advises the project is no
longer a neutral subject of it. If any of them is also entered in this record, every one of
their entries says so, with the date of the approach. Their proposals are entered on the same
terms as anyone else's, and are never given more room for having advised.

*Confidence: high. Standard practice for a declared interest. Strongest alternative: keep
advisers out of the record entirely, which is cleaner and would leave out some of the people
whose views matter most.*

### 8. Checking, and the conflict of interest

- The rule for who is entered (part 4) is reviewed by a reviewer that is not a Claude model, as
  the family does for outside reviews, before John rules on it.
- Every entry about a person employed by Anthropic says so, and the check of each batch of
  entries is done by a session that did not write them.
- The authorship declaration (open question 7, still open) would name this record as one where
  the conflict is sharpest.

*Confidence: high. Standard practice in the family (independent reviewers, a different session
checks). Strongest alternative: a Claude check only, which is cheaper and is the kind of check
this conflict most needs to avoid.*

### 9. Not in any headline

The record does not feed the number at the top of the home page, or any other headline, unless
John rules otherwise when he rules on the headline (open question 4). If it ever does, a check
in code keeps it out until then, as the family does for side records (`docs/inheritance.md`,
row A4).

*Confidence: high. Standard practice. Strongest alternative: a headline built on this record
("what thinkers propose, this year"), which may be what the site eventually wants and should
be decided with the headline and not before it.*

## What it would change elsewhere, if ruled

None of these is made now.

- `README.md`: the project's description would name three records, not two.
- `docs/record-schema.md`: a new section for this record, next to the claims log in section 10.
- `docs/method.md`: the selection rule and the yearly re-read.
- `docs/open-questions.md`: questions 4 (the headline), 8 (the family boundaries) and 17 (how the
  records sit together) would each need to take this record into account. Question 20, added
  with this proposal, holds the idea open until John rules.
- The site: a page of its own, designed under the Glass direction. Not before the design points
  still open are ruled.

## What it does not settle

- **Whose voice the site speaks in, and where it declares that a model wrote much of it**
  (open question 7). This record sharpens that question, because a model would be choosing and
  summarising the humans.
- **The boundary with the sibling sites.** The AGI Zeitgeist keeps a claims log of what named
  people say. This record's subject is different (what should be done for machine minds, not
  when or whether a capability arrives), but some people would appear on both. The family's
  existing answer would apply: each site keeps its own record, and the two name each other
  (`docs/inheritance.md`, row G15).
- **Whether proposals should one day be put to the models** under Still Asking. That waits on a
  Still Asking protocol, which does not exist yet.

## What exploring it involves, at no cost

1. Draft the rule for who is entered (part 4), in full, and have a reviewer that is not a Claude
   model read it. That reviewer would be a model too, and any call to one needs John's go and a
   key for this project, which does not exist yet. Until then, the draft can be read by John and
   by a different Claude session, with the conflict stated.
2. Try the entry layout (part 3) on five people on paper, chosen to span the groups, including
   at least one critic, from statements opened and quoted. See whether the fields hold, and
   whether "for, against, conditional, neither" fits real proposals or forces them.
3. For two of those five, find an earlier and a later statement, and see whether the layout
   shows the change clearly.

No model is queried, nothing is spent and nothing is published in any of these steps.

## What I would change on reflection

- **The name of the record.** I have avoided giving it a short name, because the workspace rules
  warn against coining shorthand. The site will need a page title. That belongs with the design
  points, not here.
- **Part 1 may be wrong.** If the trial in step 2 shows that most proposals carry a testable
  commitment, folding them into the claims log would be simpler.
- **"Leading thinkers"** was John's phrase, and the spread rule in part 4 deliberately reaches
  beyond the people usually called leading. John may want the record narrower than this.
