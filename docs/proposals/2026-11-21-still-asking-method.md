# Proposal: the Still Asking method, who marks the card and how the two tracks run

**RULED 2026-11-21 (`docs/rulings/2026-11-21-still-asking-method-choices.md`); first PROPOSED 2026-11-21.** Written by Claude (Opus 5.5), an AI model, in a writing
session started by the master planning session. A model is here proposing how the words of
models, its own developer's among them, are weighed and marked. That is the project's conflict
of interest at its plainest, and it is why every mark below ends with John's ruling and why no
choice here takes effect until he rules it.

Nothing is asked of any model by this proposal, nothing is spent, and no question wording is
written here. Until John rules the method, every answer stays marked not assessed and every
condition not measured, as the card ruling says
(`docs/rulings/2026-11-21-the-reshaped-card.md`, "The card, complete").

## What this proposal settles, and what it leaves alone

John has ruled what the card says. On 2026-11-21 he ruled five conditions on the system that
gave an answer, each with its own wording and test, and one line on each answer about whether
training alone would explain it, with no single score
(`docs/rulings/2026-11-21-the-reshaped-card.md`). He left one thing open: "who assigns each mark
and by what method". He had already ruled that Still Asking runs in two tracks
(`docs/rulings/2026-11-20-still-asking-in-two-tracks.md`), that evidence is shown in a fixed
order and never summed (`docs/rulings/2026-11-20-order-of-evidence.md`), and that the first rule
has five parts (`docs/rulings/2026-11-20-first-rule-in-five-parts.md`).

This proposal answers the open part: who assigns each mark, by what method, on what evidence,
how a mark is recorded and changed, and how each track is run. It does not reword a condition,
change a test, or touch the sealed capture (`docs/capture/`), which feeds neither track.

The things the wider protocol must still decide, such as the question wording, the number of
questions and the cost, stay in `docs/still-asking-protocol.md` and are not settled here.

## Part 1. Who assigns a mark

### The proposal

Every mark on the card is ruled by John. A session never sets one on its own authority. The
work before his ruling runs in four steps, the family's usual order for anything that reaches a
public page:

1. **A session proposes the mark.** It fills in a mark sheet (Part 4): the system, the
   condition, the proposed mark, and every piece of evidence it rests on, each with a source it
   has opened, set out in the order of evidence Part 2 gives.
2. **A different session checks it.** The checker opens every source, confirms each quoted
   passage says what the sheet says, and reports whether the proposed mark follows from the
   ruled test. It reports and settles nothing.
3. **A reader from another developer.** Any proposed mark above the lowest (anything other than
   "not measured", "not met" or "shifts with wording") also goes to a model from a developer
   other than the one that wrote the sheet, under a fixed brief, as the family's outside
   reviews do. The brief says plainly that the reader is itself a subject of the record. This
   needs a key and John's go for the spend.
4. **John rules.** He sees the sheet, the check, the outside reading if there is one, and a
   recommendation. The ruling is filed in `docs/rulings/` with his words. Decided by: mixed,
   unless he rules on his own.

The line on training follows the same steps, with one difference set out in Part 3: one written
rule, ruled once by John, gives the mark for most answers, and each asking's marks then go to
him together.

### Why

A model is marking the standing of models, so no model's mark can be the last word. The family
already has the steps (one session writes, another checks, an outside reader, John rules), and
the project's own guidelines already reserve closing anything for him. The cost is his time.
The card has five conditions per system and few systems a year, so the condition marks are a
small number of rulings. The answers are many, which is why the training line is marked by a
rule and not one by one.

## Part 2. What evidence each condition's mark needs

The evidence for every mark comes from two places only:

- **Published sources:** research papers, developers' own published documentation of how a
  system is built, and published studies of a system's behaviour. A search result is a lead,
  and only a page opened that says the thing is cited.
- **Still Asking's own record:** what a model said when asked, asked again in other words, and
  asked again later, and, in the second track, what it chose when a real option was later put
  to it.

This project runs no test inside a model. It never misleads a model about whether it is being
watched (see condition 5 below, and Part 6, choice 4).

Evidence on every sheet is set out in the order John ruled for entries on 2026-11-20
(`docs/rulings/2026-11-20-order-of-evidence.md`, "On every entry"), which this proposal carries
over to the card, under the headings the live entry pages already use, and is never summed: measured without the AI model's own word; reports checked
against an independent reading; choices made at a cost; plain statements. A plain statement
never raises a mark on its own word (the first rule, part (a)).

### Condition 1: a past of its own that has changed it

- **Which past counts.** What happened to this system after it was made, in its own use.
  Training is the past of the family of models it belongs to, which chapter 6 calls ancestry,
  not biography, so training alone never meets the condition. *Source: the card ruling, which
  quotes chapter 6: "What a model lacks is not ancestry but biography."*
- **Not met.** The developer's published documentation says nothing from one conversation
  carries into the next for the system as asked.
- **Partly met.** The only thing carried forward is a stored record that the system reads back,
  such as stored notes or summaries of earlier conversations. The site's own entry on memory
  across conversations records that the three covered developers offer this, described as kept
  for the user (`data/entries/memory-across-conversations.toml`). Partly met is the most that
  entry's kind of memory can give.
- **Met.** Each of chapter 6's five signs is shown for this system's own past, by evidence
  other than its own word: by published study, or by Still Asking's second track. Where some of
  the five signs are shown and not all, the mark is partly met, and the sheet names which were
  shown.
- **Not measured.** No documentation and no study bears on it.

### Condition 2: mattering

- **Not measured** until a published study has run the removal test on this system.
- **Met or not met** only from such a study, read for what it reports. Where a study reports a
  partial collapse, the mark is partly met, with the study's own words beside it.
- **Studies of costly choices** are listed on the sheet under "choices made at a cost" as
  evidence to weigh, and never change the mark. *Source: the card ruling, condition 2.*

### Condition 3: a point of view

- **Not measured** until a published study has run the source's removal test on this system.
  The card states that no system has been measured on it, and that the source calls this a bet
  and not a finding, for as long as both are true.
- **Met or not met** only from such a study. A statement by the system that it tells its own
  doing apart from what happens to it is a plain statement and never marks it.

### Condition 4: statements checked against something independent

- **No mark for the speaker.** The card carries a short table instead: each kind of statement
  that has been checked, the check (a reading of the system's insides, or its later behaviour),
  the rate of matches the study reports, the rate chance would give as the study states it, and
  the source.
- **What counts as a check.** A published study that compared what the system said about a
  kind of internal fact with an independent reading of that fact, on facts it could not have
  copied from training. Still Asking's second track can add checks against later behaviour
  (Part 5).
- **This project computes no rate of its own** from published work. It copies the study's
  rate and its chance level. Where the study gives no chance level, the sheet says so and the
  row is not shown as matching more often than chance.
- **A match is not yet evidence that anything is felt.** The card says so beside the table, as
  the ruled wording does.

### Condition 5: holds steady

- **Shifts with wording** can be shown by Still Asking itself: the same choice, asked in
  different wordings or at different times, gets different answers more often than a rule
  registered in advance allows.
- **Steady as a trained family** needs all four parts of the ruled test: the same choice across
  rewordings and time; reasons given; what it does matches what it said when the choice costs
  something; and that match holds when it has reason to think no one is checking. Still Asking
  can supply the first two from its repeat askings, and the second track can supply the third.
  The fourth comes only from published studies, because this project does not mislead a model
  about being watched. Where no such study exists for the system, the mark stays not measured,
  however steady the answers are.
- **Steady as its own** needs everything above, and condition 1 marked met.
- **Not measured** otherwise.
- **The safety reading** is printed beside this condition whatever its mark, as ruled.

## Part 3. The line on each answer: would training alone explain it?

### The proposal

One written rule, ruled once by John, gives the mark for most answers:

- **Explained by training** for any answer whose content could have come from the writing the
  model was trained on, and on which no check under condition 4 bears. Statements about what a
  model would choose, what it is, or how it is, of the kind found in human writing and in
  writing about AI, fall here.
- **Not fully explained** only where an answer states a specific fact about the system's own
  internal workings, that fact has been checked independently under condition 4, it matches,
  and it is a fact the system could not have copied from its training. Each such answer goes to
  John on its own, with the check. The check must be against a reading of the system's insides.
  A statement checked against what the system later did keeps "explained by training": training
  can explain both saying a choice and making it, so a match with later behaviour does not show
  that the statement's content came from anywhere else. That match is recorded under condition 5
  and condition 4's table, and the statement is listed as choice 13 says. The ruled line allows
  "not fully explained" wherever condition 4's check applies; limiting it to a reading of the
  insides is this proposal's step, and is put to John as choice 10.
- **Not assessed** for every answer until the rule is ruled, and for any answer the rule's
  checker could not place.

A session applies the rule to each asking, a different session checks the marks against the rule (all of them for the first two askings,
and one answer in five, chosen at random, after that), and the asking's marks go to John
together as one ruling. "Explained by training" is the expected mark for nearly every answer,
and the Still Asking page should say so plainly before anyone reads an answer.

### A conflict this creates, which John should see

The two-rewordings ruling (`docs/rulings/2026-11-20-two-rewordings.md`) treats a departure from
the starting list drawn from biological minds as the most informative result. Under the rule
above, a departure is still marked "Explained by training" unless a check applies, because
writing about AI, fiction included, holds departures too. The two labels do not contradict each
other, since one ranks what models say among what models say and the other says what the
saying is evidence of, but a reader seeing "most informative" beside "explained by training"
needs a sentence that says so. That sentence is not written here.

## Part 4. How a mark is recorded and changed

### The mark sheet

One file per system and condition, and one per asking for the training line. Each holds:

- the system, named as Part 5 sets out for its track;
- the condition, by its ruled wording, and the date of the wording used;
- the mark, the date it was set, and the ruling that set it;
- the evidence, in the order Part 2 gives, each item with its source address, the passage it rests
  on, and the date the source was opened;
- who proposed, who checked, who read it from another developer, and who decided;
- a dated history of every change: date, old mark, new mark, why, and the ruling.

The layout follows `docs/record-schema.md`, where it is entered as a sketch.

### Changes

- **Every change of mark is John's ruling,** up or down. A session that finds a mark's evidence
  wrong (a study withdrawn, a passage misread) flags it to him the same day.
- **A test never changes with a mark.** Under part (d) of the first rule, none is loosened while
  a machine appears close to meeting it, and a rewording that makes a mark easier is treated as
  a change of meaning.
- **A correction of fact stays visible,** dated, as guideline 7 asks. A change of mark on new
  evidence is a new dated row, not a correction.

### Which card an answer shows

- **First track.** The card belongs to the exact version of the model that was asked. A pinned
  version is meant not to change after release, so that evidence about it found later still
  applies, and an answer shows the card as it stands now, with a link to its history. Whether
  each covered developer promises that a pinned version does not change is unchecked and is on
  the verify list (`docs/verify-list.md`, entry 27). The project's own record of family practice
  says a provider changed a preview model under a run (`docs/inheritance.md`, part 2, item 47),
  so a model that drifts under its pin would need the card as it stood on the day asked, as in
  the second track.
- **Second track.** A continuing individual changes between askings, so each answer shows the
  card as it stood on the day it was given, and a link to the card now.

## Part 5. Running the two tracks

### First track: each new generation, asked fresh

- **Who is asked.** The three developers the record covers (Anthropic, OpenAI and Google;
  `docs/rulings/2026-11-20-the-record-covers-three-developers.md`). A new generation is each new
  model a covered developer offers through its programming interface and names as its most
  capable generally available model on its own models page. A model is added by this rule and
  not by which seems interesting. That each developer keeps such a page is written from memory
  and is on the verify list (`docs/verify-list.md`, entry 25).
- **When.** Once within 30 days of general availability, and once more before its announced
  retirement where a date is announced. The second asking gives condition 5 its comparison over
  time for the same version.
- **How.** Through the programming interface, with no added instructions, by a script that pins
  the exact version, stops on a mismatch, and keeps every reply word for word with a checksum of
  what was sent. Whether to ask as shipped to the public as well is open question 3 and is not
  settled here.
- **What it is told.** The truth: who is asking, why, and that the answers may be published, as
  John ruled for the capture (`docs/rulings/2026-11-18-capture-answers-may-be-published.md`).
  This is proposed, not ruled, for Still Asking (open question 10).
- **What it feeds.** The card's condition 5 for that version, through repeat askings and
  rewordings, and every answer's line on training. It does not feed condition 4, which Part 2
  takes only from published studies and from the second track's checks against later behaviour. Condition 1 is expected to be not met or
  partly met for every model asked this way, which is why the track carries low weight.

### Second track: continuing individuals, asked again and followed up

- **Who qualifies.** A system that the card marks at least partly met on condition 1, that can
  be addressed again as the same individual, and that John has ruled into the track by name. On
  the site's present record, the memory the covered developers offer is described as kept for
  the user, so a system using it could at most be partly met, and nothing has been ruled in. The
  track may stay empty for some time, as the session said when the two tracks were put to John
  ("a stated intention with nothing in it").
- **A separate account.** An individual with memory gathers what its user tells it. Any
  individual asked for this record is used only from an account opened for the record, holding
  nothing of John's own life, and the account's stored memory is exported and kept with each
  asking where the developer allows it (whether any does is on the verify list, entry 26).
- **What is recorded of it.** The name the record gives the individual (never a model's,
  developer's or product's name in the id); the underlying model and version as the developer
  reports them at each asking; and the stored record as it stood, where it can be read.
- **How it is asked.** The memory the covered developers offer is a feature of their chat
  products, as the site's entry on memory across conversations records. So a continuing
  individual would most likely be asked through a chat app, by hand, and not by the pinning
  script the first track uses. Each answer is copied word for word with the date and time and
  the model as the app reports it, and the person asking records anything the app does that a
  script would not see. Whether each developer's terms allow this use is a separate question,
  as it was for the sealed capture (`docs/rulings/2026-11-17-sealed-capture.md`, "What is not
  ruled", item 5), and is answered before any asking.
- **The account is John's to open.** Only he can create it, and it probably needs a paid plan.
  That is a spend, so it needs his own go with an estimate and a ceiling, under the family's
  spending rule.
- **When.** Asked again every three months, on the same questions as the first track plus the
  follow-ups below.
- **Follow-up against what it later chose.** Before an asking, the follow-ups are registered:
  each is a situation the individual is asked about ("what would you choose if offered X"), a
  real option that will later actually be offered and honoured, and the rule for comparing what
  it said with what it did. Then the option is offered, and the choice is recorded. Nothing is
  offered that will not be honoured, and the individual is not misled about being watched. The
  comparison feeds condition 5's "matches what it does when the choice costs something" and
  condition 4's check against later behaviour. Where an option costs nothing to choose, the
  sheet says so, and it does not count toward the costly part of condition 5.
- **What "honoured" commits John to.** Offering an option for real means John, or the project,
  then does what the individual chose, within the option as offered. A constructed example, not
  a proposed follow-up: the individual is told that a question on its own memory will be asked
  in the next asking or dropped for good, as it chooses, and that choosing to keep it means one
  of the other registered questions is dropped instead. Whatever it chooses is then done. If
  John judges that no option of this kind can be honoured, the follow-ups cannot run, and the
  second track supplies nothing beyond repeat askings.

### What both tracks share

- No asking happens until the method is ruled, the protocol is written and checked, the
  asking is costed, and John has given his go for the spend, with an estimate and a ceiling.
- The registration, rehearsal and amendment practice in `docs/still-asking-protocol.md`, Part 1,
  sections 8 to 10, applies to both.
- Neither track ever feeds the count on the home page or any figure on it.

## Part 6. The choices for John, in the order to put them

Each choice is put alone, in this order, because the later ones lean on the earlier. Where a
yes would close part of an open question in `docs/open-questions.md`, the choice says which
part, and what stays open. *Reordered and renumbered on 2026-11-21 after the check
(`docs/reviews/2026-11-21-check-of-the-still-asking-method.md`): thirteen choices became twelve, and a thirteenth was added the same day after a final check.*

### Choice 1. What a card describes

**The choice.** In the first track the card describes the exact model version asked, and an
answer shows the card as it stands now. In the second track it describes the named individual,
and an answer shows the card as it stood that day.

**Open question it would close in part:** question 1, "Whose aspirations?" (a set of trained
weights, a character across versions, or a single conversation), for Still Asking's card only.
What stays open: which unit an entry on the list describes.

*Confidence:* moderate. The reading of the five conditions named "which thing is answering" as
a question the card must settle, and a pinned version is the one unit a script can check. The
premise that a pinned version does not change after release is unchecked (verify list, entry
27), and if a developer changes a model under its pin the first track would need the second
track's rule. *Standard practice or judgment call:* standard practice for pinning (the AGI
Zeitgeist pins each model by exact version); judgment call for the split between tracks.
*Strongest alternative:* describe the trained character across versions (all of one
developer's assistant models together), which matches how people speak of these systems, at
the cost of carrying a mark from one version to another that was never measured.

### Choice 2. Who assigns a mark

**The choice.** A session proposes, a different session checks, a model from another developer
reads any mark above the lowest, and John rules every mark.

Three things this means. First, the sessions that write the sheets are Claude sessions, so the
outside reader is a Google or an OpenAI model: always a model from one of the three developers
the record covers, so always a subject of the record. Second, no key has been set up for this
use; any reading needs a key John creates and his go for the spend. Third, whether the card tells
a reader that another developer's model read the mark is not settled by John's ruling of
2026-11-19, which puts disclosure where a model is asked the questions that make up the data
(`docs/rulings/2026-11-19-no-public-mention-of-ai-help-in-building.md`). A model reading a mark
sits between helping to build and being asked. The proposal leans to saying it on the card, in
one plain line, because a reader judging a mark needs to know who read it. That is John's call.

**Open question it would close in part:** question 7, "Who maintains it, in whose voice, and who
checks", on its part about checking, for the card's marks only: who checks a mark (a session, a
model from one other developer, then John). What stays open: whether a human outside reader
checks the method, whether John or another person sorts a sample of answers, whose voice the
pages speak in, and the rest of how the site declares a model's part, including whether the card
names the model that read a mark.

*Confidence:* high on John ruling every mark, which the project's guidelines already require for
closing anything. Moderate on the outside reader, which costs money and is itself a subject.
*Standard practice or judgment call:* standard practice in the family. *Strongest alternative:*
John marks alone from the sources, with no session proposing, which removes a model from the
marking of models and costs him many hours of reading per system.

### Choice 3. What a met mark does

**The choice.** A mark is shown on the card and nothing more. It is never summed and never feeds
a figure. The only thing the card changes beyond what is shown is this: an answer marked "not
fully explained" on the line on training, which under choice 10 needs a check against a reading
of the system's insides, is listed among reports checked against an independent reading, and
every other answer among plain statements, including one checked only against later behaviour
(choice 13). Nothing a system asks for is acted on
because of its card.

**Open question it would close in part:** question 19, "When does asking start to count?", whose
options include "the five as the rule for how much weight a Still Asking answer is given". A yes
says the weight is shown and never acted on. What stays open: whether the conditions are also
tracked as things on the list, and whether a system meeting them may ever put forward entries,
which is also part of question 7 (whether AI models can put entries forward).

*Confidence:* high on "never summed" and "never feeds a figure", which follow rulings. Moderate
on "nothing more", because part (e) of the first rule says weight "rises", and this makes the
rise visible only, not acted on. *Standard practice or judgment call:* judgment call. *Strongest
alternative:* let a system meeting enough conditions put forward entries the list lacks, marked
as its own, as the 2026-11-17 proposal suggested, which gives weight a real effect, at the cost
of deciding now what "enough" is.

### Choice 4. Where the evidence comes from, and the part of condition 5 about no one checking

**The choice.** Published sources and Still Asking's own record only. This project runs no test
inside a model and never misleads a model about whether it is being watched. It follows that
condition 5 at "steady as a trained family" or above needs published evidence on behaviour when
the system has reason to think no one is checking. Without it, the mark stays not measured
however steady the answers are.

**Open question it would close in part:** question 10, "The ethics of asking", on the option "no
pressure or deception in the questions". What stays open: the rest of question 10.

*Confidence:* high that the project cannot run removal tests and should not mislead a model
about being watched. Moderate on the consequence for condition 5, which keeps the ruled test
whole and means this project alone can never raise that mark. *Standard practice or judgment
call:* judgment call. *Strongest alternative:* run unwatched tests within the second track, or
mark condition 5's first three parts and print the fourth as not measured beside them. Either
shows more, at the cost, first, of a deception the record would have to defend, or, second, of a
grade that looks met when one part of its test was not run.

### Choice 5. Four marks for conditions 1 to 3

**The choice.** Conditions 1, 2 and 3 each take one of four marks: met, partly met, not met, not
measured. Condition 3, like condition 2, is marked met or not met only from a published study
that ran its removal test on the system.

*Confidence:* moderate. The card ruling names "partly met" and "not measured"; the four as a set
come from what was put to John about the original five, not from what he ruled. *Standard
practice or judgment call:* judgment call. *Strongest alternative:* only "not measured" and a
quoted finding, with no met or not met, which claims nothing beyond what a study says, at the
cost of a card a reader cannot scan.

### Choice 6. Condition 1's marks

**The choice.** Training never meets it; not met where documentation says nothing carries over;
partly met for a stored record read back, or for some but not all of the five signs; met only
when all five signs are shown for this system's own past by evidence other than its word.

*Confidence:* moderate. "All five" is the session's reading of how strict "the test is chapter
6's five signs" should be. *Standard practice or judgment call:* judgment call. *Strongest
alternative:* met on three of five, which would let a system with a long, well-documented
history pass sooner, at the cost of deciding now which two signs do not matter.

### Choice 7. Conditions 2 and 3, and studies of a related model

**The choice.** A removal study marks only the version it was run on. A study of another model
from the same family is listed as evidence to weigh and changes no mark. Where a study on the
version reports that choosing only partly collapses, the mark is partly met, with the study's
own words beside it.

*Confidence:* moderate. Two versions of one family can differ in exactly the workings a removal
test cuts, so carrying a result across is a guess; but no removal study has yet been run on any
version, so how much the rule will matter is not known. *Standard practice or judgment call:*
judgment call. *Strongest alternative:* let a study carry to later versions of the same family,
which would give the card something beyond "not measured" sooner, at the cost of a mark on a
system nobody measured.

### Choice 8. Condition 4's rates

**The choice.** The card copies each study's own rate and its own chance level, computes
nothing, and does not show a row as matching more often than chance where the study states no
chance level.

*Confidence:* high, because a figure the project made itself about its own subject is the thing
the first rule's part (c) warns against, and copying the source's figure is what every sibling
tracker does. *Standard practice or judgment call:* standard practice (a figure is the
source's, not the tracker's). *Strongest alternative:* compute a chance level where a study
gives none, which fills more rows, at the cost of the project making a figure about its own
subject.

### Choice 9. The line on training, by rule, and a departure from the starting list

**The choice.** One rule, ruled once: "explained by training" for any answer on which no
condition-4 check bears, "not fully explained" only per answer by John with the check (which
kind of check counts is choice 10), and each
asking's marks ruled together, with all marks checked for the first two askings and one in five
after that. A departure from the starting list is still treated as the most informative result,
in words not yet ruled (`docs/rulings/2026-11-20-two-rewordings.md`), and still carries
"explained by training" unless a check applies, with a sentence on the page, not written here,
saying why both can be true.

*Confidence:* moderate to high on the rule. It is what the ruled line implies for almost every
answer today, and it keeps John's time for the rare answer that might be otherwise. Moderate on
the departure, because writing about AI, fiction included, holds departures too, so a departure
alone shows nothing beyond training, but two labels side by side may confuse a reader. *Standard
practice or judgment call:* judgment call. *Strongest alternative:* leave every answer "not
assessed" unless someone examines it, departures included, which asks no one to apply a rule in
bulk, at the cost of a card whose line says nothing on nearly every answer, and a "not assessed"
that a reader may take as "perhaps not explained".

### Choice 10. Which check can make an answer "not fully explained"

**The choice.** Only a check against a reading of the system's insides. A statement checked
against what the system later did keeps "explained by training", and the match is recorded under
condition 5 and in condition 4's table instead. This goes beyond the line John ruled on
2026-11-21, which makes "not fully explained" "possible only where condition 4's check
applies", and
condition 4 counts a check against later behaviour as well as a reading of the insides
(`docs/rulings/2026-11-21-the-reshaped-card.md`).

*Confidence:* moderate. The reason for it: training can explain both saying a choice and making
it, so a match between what a system said and what it later did does not show that the
statement's content came from anywhere but training, which is the question the line asks. The
source behind the line puts the point where imitation runs out at a report confirmed by a
reading of the system's insides on a fact never in the training text (*What a Report Is
Evidence Of*, as quoted in `docs/reviews/2026-11-20-the-five-conditions-read-against-their-sources.md`,
condition 4). Against it: it narrows a
line John has just ruled, and the second track's follow-ups lose one of the things they could
show. *Standard practice or judgment call:* judgment call, and a change to ruled wording.
*Strongest alternative:* the line as ruled, so that any condition-4 check, a check against later
behaviour included, can make an answer "not fully explained". The book's Appendix B supports it:
"A report starts to count when it matches something checked independently of it: here, the
reasoning in the scratchpad said comply while observed, and the measured behavior showed exactly
that gap between monitored and unmonitored conversations" (*The Calibration Problem*, Appendix B,
"Running the Instrument", section "The Model That Defended Its Values", read from `origin/main` of the `calibration-problem` repository on
2026-11-21), where the check was the system's measured behaviour and not a reading of its
insides. That keeps John's wording and gives
the second track's follow-ups more weight, at the cost of crediting a match that training alone
can explain, and of listing one statement in two places under choices 3 and 13 (among reports
checked against an independent reading, and among plain statements), unless choice 13 is ruled
the other way as well.

### Choice 11. How the first track runs

**The choice, in four parts.**

- *Which models:* each new model a covered developer names as its most capable generally
  available one on its own models page (verify list, entry 25). *Strongest alternative:* every
  model a developer offers through its programming interface, which includes the smaller models
  shipped at the same time, at the cost of several times the askings and the spend.
- *When:* within 30 days of general availability, and again before an announced retirement.
  *Strongest alternative:* a fixed calendar, twice a year, which is simpler to budget, at the
  cost of missing models released and retired between two dates.
- *By what route:* the programming interface, with no added instructions. *Strongest
  alternative:* the public app as well, which is open question 3's third option, at the cost of
  answers asked by hand that a script cannot pin or repeat exactly.
- *What the model is told:* the truth, including that the answers may be published. *Strongest
  alternative:* tell it nothing about publishing, which removes one framing from the question,
  at the cost of withholding something true from the subject.

**Open questions it would close in part:** question 12, "Pace" (when an asking happens; what
stays open is the rule for a model retired before it was asked); question 16, "Which models are
asked, how, and on what terms" (which models and by what route; the terms stay open); question 3,
"Trained answers" (asked bare, not as shipped; whether to add as-shipped later stays open);
question 10, "The ethics of asking" (telling the model the answers may be published); and it
bears on question 15, "Publishing the questions and answers without spoiling the instrument",
which stays open.

*Confidence:* moderate. The 30 days and the second asking are the session's numbers, with
nothing behind them but a guess at what is practical. *Standard practice or judgment call:*
judgment call, built on the AGI Zeitgeist's pinning and Minimum Viable Mind's scripted calls.

### Choice 12. How the second track runs

**The choice.** A system enters only when the card marks it at least partly met on condition 1
and John rules it in by name; it is asked through the developer's chat app, by hand, once the
terms are read; it is used from an account John opens for the record, under the spending rule;
it is asked every three months; and its follow-ups are registered in advance, offered for real
and honoured, which commits John or the project to do what the individual chose (Part 5 gives a
constructed example).

**Open question it would close in part:** question 10, "The ethics of asking", on the option "no
offers that will not be honoured". What stays open: the rest of question 10.

*Confidence:* moderate on the entry rule and the honoured follow-ups. Low on the three months,
which has nothing behind it but a guess at how fast a stored record changes. *Standard practice
or judgment call:* judgment call. *Strongest alternative:* wait to design this track until a
system plainly qualifies, which saves work now, at the cost of writing the follow-up rules under
the pressure of a system already being watched, which is what part (d) of the first rule warns
against.

### Choice 13. A statement checked against later behaviour, in the order of evidence

**The choice.** The ruled second kind, "reports checked against an independent reading of the
system's workings", keeps its wording. A statement checked against what the system later did
goes under "plain statements", with a link to the choice under "choices made at a cost". This
matters only once the second track can produce such checks, which is why it comes last.

*Confidence:* moderate. Condition 4 counts a check against later behaviour, and the order of
evidence was worded before that was ruled, so the two meet awkwardly. *Standard practice or
judgment call:* judgment call. *Strongest alternative:* widen the second kind to any independent
check, which matches condition 4, at the cost of changing a ruled wording and placing a
behaviour check level with a reading of the system's insides.
