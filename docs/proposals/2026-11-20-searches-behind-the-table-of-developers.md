## Three judgments, each bearing on a developer other than Anthropic

**1. OpenAI's notice on ending a chat is written as an act under "a model can end a
conversation itself", and is not counted as offered.** It is a promise that had not taken
effect on the day of the notice, which the ruling of 2026-11-18 says is not counted. Two
things a reader should know, and the act's words and source say both: the product ends the
chat, by an automatic safeguard, and the reasons given are the safety of people and say nothing
of the model. *Confidence:* moderate that it belongs in this row. *Strongest alternative:*
leave it out as a different kind of thing, a safeguard for people, and mark the place "nothing
found". That is the reading less generous to OpenAI. Whether the change has since taken effect
was not checked, and if it has, whether the entry's state should name OpenAI is John's to
rule: I did not touch the state.

**2. OpenAI's research on models saying when they break instructions is written as an act
under "a model's report about its own inner state is checked", and is not counted.** The
report it studies is about what the model did, checked against what it did. It is not a
report about a state read inside the model, which is what the entry's test asks for.
*Confidence:* low that it belongs in this row. *Strongest alternative:* "nothing found", with
this research named in the search's words as the nearest thing. I wrote it in because the
other choice would give Google a date in this row and OpenAI none, on a distinction a reader
could fairly dispute.

**3. Google's paper is written as an act in the same row, and is not counted.** It is by two
of Google's researchers, about Google's own models, and it concerns a setting the researchers
set, which is what the entry's test describes. It gives example answers and no rate, and the
test asks for a rate. *Confidence:* moderate. *Strongest alternative:* "nothing found",
because the paper calls itself a conceptual analysis and not a study.

With all three written in, the row on checked self-reports shows a date for every developer
and "not counted as offered" three times. That is accurate, and it is the row a checker should
read first.

## What could not be settled

- **Whether OpenAI keeps a retired model open.** Its help page says models retired from its
  chat product "will continue to be available through the OpenAI API", and its page on
  retirements lists the older model's versions as due to shut down on 2027-01-14. So that model
  is withdrawn from one product and still usable through another. Whether that meets the
  entry's test turns on what "retired" means, and the place still says "Withdrawal: January
  29th, 2026". The help page shows no date, so nothing could be written as an act. John should
  read this place himself, as the earlier write-up already asked.
- **Whether OpenAI's automatic ending of chats has begun.** The notice said "the next few
  weeks" on 2026-10-30.
- **Earlier release reports.** For OpenAI and Google only the two newest were opened. A
  welfare section in an older report would not have been found by opening those.
- **Google's chat product,** for the row on retired models. Only its pages for developers were
  found.
- **Anything not in English, and anything said only in an interview or a talk.** Every search
  was in English and looked for a published page.
- **Internal practice.** A developer may keep files, or interview a model, and say nothing in
  public. The table records what is public.
