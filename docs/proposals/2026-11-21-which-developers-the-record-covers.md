# Which developers the record covers: a rule a reader can check

**PROPOSED 2026-11-21, not ruled.** Written by Claude (Opus 5.5), an AI model, in a working
session started by the master planning session. A different session checks it before it goes to
John.

**Revised later on 2026-11-21** after the check by a different session
(`docs/reviews/2026-11-21-check-of-which-developers-the-record-covers.md`): the Forum's members
corrected from four to six, the conflict stated as things and not places, and the should-fix
items taken in. Choice 3 now recommends waiting on Rule C.

**How it came up.** On 2026-11-20 John ruled that the record covers three developers, Anthropic,
OpenAI and Google, chosen as quick to start, with no rule for adding a fourth
(`docs/rulings/2026-11-20-the-record-covers-three-developers.md`). On 2026-11-21 he asked: "Are
we being shortsighted by only counting Anthropic, OpenAI and Google?", and approved drafting a
rule a reader can check, with the list it produces.

**What was done for this draft.** On 2026-11-21 this session opened three public rankings of AI
models, the pages of the organisations that run them, two lists of companies kept by others, two
published positions on how AI models should be treated, and the public page of every developer
named below. That is reading to draft a proposal. No record, page or data file was changed, no
search was made for the record itself, and no money was spent. The phone check in part 3 was
made by changing the published home page inside the browser only, and nothing was saved.
Anything below written without opening its source is in `docs/verify-list.md`, entries 28 to 35.

**What the record counts today.** The record counts 5 things (`data/headline.json`, read
2026-11-21), and Anthropic offers all five. Since 2026-11-20 OpenAI also offers one of those same
five (a model can end a conversation itself). In the table of developers that makes 6 places
marked as offered, Anthropic's 5 and OpenAI's 1, but still 5 things, every one of them
Anthropic's. So the brief's point stands: the developer behind the model doing much of the work is
behind every thing counted.

## What the sibling trackers do

- **The AGI Zeitgeist** does not choose models itself. Its measure of how fast AI is getting
  better follows an outside list (METR's results file, which names the models; see
  `agi-zeitgeist/docs/weekly-reading.md`, the reading for the first measure). It adds an AI
  release to its record only when a statement already in the record names it (amendment A21,
  `agi-zeitgeist/docs/amendments.md`). The weekly reading's decision 5 turned down "append every
  frontier release" because "'frontier' is a judgment".
- **The Belt Equation** keeps a list of missions to follow, and "adding or removing a mission is
  John's" (rule 4.20, in `belt-equation/docs/proposal-missions-to-watch-2026-10-20.md`). There
  is no outside rule.

So the family has two habits: follow a list someone else keeps, where a good one exists; and
where none does, the list is John's, by ruling. The 2026-11-20 ruling took the second. This
proposal asks whether a list someone else keeps is now good enough to take the first.

## Part 1. Three candidate rules

### Rule A. A named public ranking of how capable models are, read on a stated date

*Wording, as a reader would see it:* "The record covers every developer whose best model is in the
top five on [ranking], read on [date]. The list is read again every [period]. A developer once
covered stays covered."

A ruled wording also has to name exactly what is read: which board (Arena has an overall board
as well as the text-chat board read here, which was its lab view), which filters (Artificial
Analysis's table shows "Status: Current" by default, and it was read that way), and whether a
model must be on public sale to count. Meta's best model on Artificial Analysis, "Muse Wonder Full
Max", has no price or speed listed and may not be open to the public. On today's figures this
changes nothing: Meta's next model scores 44 and would still be fifth.

Three rankings were opened on 2026-11-21. Each ranks models, so a developer's place below is the
place of its best model.

|---|---|---|---|

| 2 | Meta (1,511) | OpenAI (62) | OpenAI (173) |
| 3 | Anthropic (1,506) | Google (61) | Google DeepMind (170) |

The scores are each ranking's own units and cannot be compared across columns. Arena's lab
ranking was read as shown. For the other two, the leaderboard tables were read in the browser and
each developer's highest-placed model was taken. Epoch's table gives an uncertainty range with
each score; Artificial Analysis's does not.

**What the rankings produce today:**

- **Top five:** the same five developers on all three rankings, in different orders: Anthropic,
  OpenAI, Google, Meta and Moonshot.
- **Top ten:** nine developers are in the top ten of all three: the five above, and Alibaba,
  DeepSeek, xAI (called SpaceXAI by two of the rankings) and Z.ai. Three more are in one
  ranking's top ten only: Xiaomi (Arena), Tencent (Artificial Analysis) and Mistral AI (Epoch).
- **Where the scores break:** on two of the three rankings there is a clear gap after the third
  developer. On Artificial Analysis the third scores 61 and the fourth 50. On Epoch, Google
  DeepMind's best model has a range of 167 to 173 and Meta's best a range of 159 to 164, so the
  two ranges do not overlap. A rule that took "the developers above the first clear gap" would give
  today's three on those two rankings. Arena disagrees: there Meta is second and Moonshot is ahead
  of OpenAI, and the fourth to tenth labs sit within 13 points of each other.
- **How close the line at five is.** On Arena, fifth (OpenAI, 1,485) and sixth (Alibaba, 1,484)
  are one point apart, and ninth and tenth tie. On Artificial Analysis, fifth (Moonshot's model,
  46) is 3 points above sixth (Alibaba's, 43), and no uncertainty is given. On Epoch AI, fifth
  (Moonshot, range 159 to 164) and sixth (DeepSeek, range 157 to 162) overlap, so Epoch's own
  figures do not separate them, and Meta's range overlaps both. So Moonshot's place in the top
  five rests on a 3-point lead on one ranking and an overlapping range on another. Agreement
  between three rankings guards against one being pushed; it does not guard against a line drawn
  inside the rankings' own margins. The gap after the third developer, on two rankings, is a
  firmer place for a line than five.

**Weaknesses.**

- *Rankings move.* Arena's page was updated on 2026-11-19. Artificial Analysis's own post, shown on
  [its About page](https://artificialanalysis.ai/about) when this session loaded it twice on
  2026-11-21, says one xAI model "scores 46" on its index; its leaderboard the same day shows that
  model at 35. The session that checked this draft loaded the same page that day and did not see
  the post, because the posts shown change from one load to the next, so the figure is seen and
  not reliably reproducible. The point does not rest on it: rankings are revised, so a rule must
  name the date it was read and keep a saved copy of what it read.
- *Rankings can be pushed.* Arena ranks by people's votes between two answers in a chat, which
  measures what people prefer in a chat, not capability in general. A developer can try many
  versions before release (written from memory; entry 28 on the verify list). The other two are
  built from test scores, and a developer can train toward a known test.
- *The owners have ties.* [Arena's About page](https://arena.ai/company/about) says it became a
  company, Arena Intelligence Inc., in 2025, with "$250 million in venture funding", led by
  Felicis and UC Investments. [Artificial Analysis](https://artificialanalysis.ai/about) calls
  itself "the independent benchmarking company", names its individual backers, and says it helps
  "developers and companies". [Epoch AI's transparency page](https://epoch.ai/about/transparency)
  says it is a nonprofit funded mostly by grants and donations, and lists Google DeepMind, OpenAI,
  xAI and Anthropic among organisations it has worked with on paid projects. It also says it
  "partnered with OpenAI to create the leading AI benchmark in mathematics", and that it invests
  "part of our funds in semiconductor and AI stocks". [Epoch AI's index page](https://epoch.ai/eci)
  says the paper behind the index "was funded by Google DeepMind, and written in collaboration
  with researchers from their AGI Safety & Alignment team", while saying the index is Epoch's own
  and Epoch has "full rights over" it.
- *Names do not match.* The same developer is "SpaceXAI" on two rankings and "xAI" on the third,
  and Moonshot's model is listed under "Kimi" on one. Google's models are under "Google" on two and
  "Google DeepMind" on the third. The rule has to say which name the record uses.
- *It measures the wrong thing for this site, in one way.* A ranking says who builds the most
  capable models. It does not say whose models the most people use, which bears on the cautions
  as much.

**The strongest form of Rule A**, and the one recommended under choice 2: name all three rankings,
and cover every developer in the top five of at least two of them. No single owner then decides,
and pushing one ranking is not enough. Today it gives the five above.

### Rule B. A list of companies kept by someone else

The brief suggested a rule based on who releases generally available frontier models. No page
opened for this draft lists those without a judgment by whoever keeps it. The nearest are two
lists of companies that call themselves frontier developers.

- **[The Frontier Model Forum](https://www.frontiermodelforum.org/about-us/)**, "an
  industry-supported non-profit", funded "by fees from its member firms". It defines a frontier
  model as one that outperforms "all other models that have been widely deployed for at least 12
  months", which it calls "intentionally more expansive". Its
  [membership page](https://www.frontiermodelforum.org/membership/), opened 2026-11-21, says: "The
  current members of the FMF are Amazon, Anthropic, Google, Meta, Microsoft, and OpenAI." (Its home
  page shows only four of their logos, which a first reading for this draft took for the whole
  list.)
- **[The Frontier AI Safety Commitments](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/frontier-ai-safety-commitments-ai-seoul-summit-2024/frontier-ai-safety-commitments-ai-seoul-summit-2024)**
  of the 2024 summit in Seoul, page updated 2025-02-07. Twenty organisations: Amazon, Anthropic,
  Cohere, Google, G42, IBM, Inflection AI, Meta, Microsoft, Mistral AI, Naver, OpenAI, Samsung
  Electronics, Technology Innovation Institute, xAI, Zhipu.ai, and later Magic, Minimax, 01.ai and
  NVIDIA.

**Weaknesses.** Both lists are joined by choice, so a developer that stays out is never covered,
however capable its models. The Forum's list gives Amazon and Microsoft, which place outside the
top ten on all three rankings, and of the five that all three rankings put in their top five it
leaves out Moonshot only. So it differs from the recommended Rule A by two developers in (Amazon,
Microsoft) and one out (Moonshot), which is closer than it first looks. The
Seoul list was fixed in 2024 and 2025, includes companies that are not among the leaders on any
ranking opened, and leaves out Moonshot, DeepSeek and Alibaba. The Forum is funded by the
companies on its list, and Anthropic is one of them.

### Rule C, an addition to A or B. A developer that has published a position on how its models should be treated

*Wording:* "The record also covers any developer that has published, on its own pages or through a
leader writing in that role, a position on whether its models could have interests of their own,
or on how they should be treated. A position against counts the same as a position for."

This works only beside Rule A or Rule B, since it says nothing about developers that have said
nothing. What it produces from pages this project has already opened:

- **Anthropic:** its page on letting models end some conversations says the feature came mainly
  from its work on possible AI welfare (already a source in the record).
- **OpenAI:** [a post by Joanne Jang](https://reservoirsamples.substack.com/p/some-thoughts-on-human-ai-relationships),
  dated 2025-06-05, on her own newsletter, which says she leads "model behavior & policy at
  OpenAI" and is subtitled "how we're approaching them at OpenAI". It says OpenAI's guidance asks
  the model to acknowledge the complexity of consciousness when asked. OpenAI's Model Spec was
  opened by an earlier session (verify list, entry 15).
- **Microsoft:** [an essay by Mustafa Suleyman](https://mustafa-suleyman.ai/seemingly-conscious-ai-is-coming),
  dated 2025-08-19, titled "We must build AI for people; not to be a person", on his own site.
  He writes "Today at Microsoft AI we build AI", and calls belief that AIs are conscious, strong
  enough that people "advocate for AI rights, model welfare and even AI citizenship", "a dangerous
  turn". Microsoft AI's draft code of conduct, dated 2026-09-14, was opened by an earlier session
  (verify list, entry 15).
- **Google, and every other developer:** nothing in the pages this project has opened. A public
  statement on the subject by xAI's leader is remembered and was not opened (entry 30).

No search was made for other developers' positions, because a position found that way is the kind
of thing that would go into the record, and a search for the record needs John's go.

**Weaknesses.** It rewards a developer for talking about the subject. Two of the three positions
above are on personal sites, so whether a leader is speaking for the company is a judgment. Its
strength is that it brings in the one developer found so far with a published position the other
way from Anthropic's, which is what guideline 5 asks for: all three cautions, not one.

## Part 3. What adding developers costs

**Searches.** The home page's table has 7 rows, one for each thing on the List that a developer
could do. Each covered developer needs one place per row, so each new developer adds 7 places.
When the record was widened to three on 2026-11-20, 14 places were searched
(`docs/proposals/2026-11-20-searches-behind-the-table-of-developers.md`). Each place took one to
three web searches and the opening of the developer's own pages where a search led to one, and the
home page promises that every "nothing found" rests on two or more searches. So each new developer
costs at least 14 searches and 7 places written into the records.

| Rule | New developers | New places | Searches, at least |
|---|---|---|---|
| Rule A, top five of two rankings | 2 | 14 | 28 |
| The same, with Rule C | 3 | 21 | 42 |
| Rule A, top ten of all three | 6 | 42 | 84 |
| The Forum's list | 3 | 21 | 42 |

**Time.** The time the 14 places took on 2026-11-20 is not recorded. An estimate, not measured
(entry 34): 15 to 30 minutes per place, searching, reading and writing, so two to three and a half
hours of a session's work per developer, plus a checking session at about half that. The
checking session makes searches of its own to test each "nothing found", which the table above
does not count. Each later
thing added to the List costs one place per covered developer, so a wider record makes every new
thing dearer, and the search made again before each release grows the same way.

**A harder kind of search.** Several developers that Rule A brings in publish much of their own
material in Chinese (written from memory, entry 35). A search in English that finds nothing for
them says less than the same search says for OpenAI. The records would have to say in which
language each search was made.

**Money.** None. The searches are made by a working session on the subscription, as on
2026-11-20. Covering a developer in the record does not mean asking its models anything: which
models Still Asking puts its questions to is a separate open question, and that one does cost
money.

**The page.** The page's code is written for three developers, and for five at most: the three
boxes near the top and the table's phone layout are set to three columns (`site/src/styles/rebuild.css`,
lines 18 and 52), and the code refuses more than five ("the breakdown is written for five
developers at most", `site/src/components/Rebuilt.astro`, line 62). Six or more needs that code
changed.

**Whether it still fits on a phone.** Checked on the published home page
(https://beyondthewindow.org/) on 2026-11-21, at a phone width of 375 pixels and a computer width
of 1,280. More developers were stood in for by copying the third column's places in the browser.

| Developers | Phone: table height | Phone: as screens (812 pixels tall) | Computer: table height |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3, as now | 1,273 pixels | about 1.6 | 705 pixels |
| 5 | 1,900 pixels | about 2.3 | 1,072 pixels |
| 10 | 3,156 pixels | about 3.9 | 1,530 pixels |

On a phone the present layout puts three places on a line and wraps the rest to the next line,
each labelled with its developer's name. Nothing spills sideways at five or at ten; the table only
gets longer. Putting five side by side instead makes each place 62 pixels wide and two places
spill; ten side by side makes the page wider than the phone (410 pixels). On a computer, five fit
in the table's 900-pixel width; ten push it to 1,060 pixels, past the column it sits in.

So five fit on both without a new design. Ten fit on a phone only as a long scroll, and on a
computer need a different layout, such as one small table per thing.

## Part 4. How the conflict of interest is handled under each rule

**The conflict.** Anthropic made the model doing much of this project's work, including this
draft, and every one of the 5 things the record counts is Anthropic's; OpenAI offers one of the
same five. The model named for Anthropic on Arena's lab ranking, Claude Opus 5.5, is the model
that wrote this proposal and the check of it. That is said here, inside the repository, and not
on any public page.

**This draft's own part in it.** This session read the rankings before proposing a rule, so the
choice of rule was made knowing what each gives. Anthropic's best model is first on two of the
three rankings. Rule A does not change Anthropic's place in the record, since it is covered under
every rule, but the choice of ranking is the kind of choice a conflicted writer could tilt, which is
why the recommended form uses all three.

- **Under Rule A,** the rule is a published reading anyone can repeat, so who is covered is not
  this project's judgment. One ranking owner (Epoch AI) lists Anthropic as a paid client, as it
  does OpenAI, Google DeepMind and xAI. Using all three rankings means no single owner decides.
- **Under Rule B,** the list is kept by an industry body that Anthropic is a member of. That is a
  reason against Rule B.
- **Under Rule C,** the rule favours developers that talk about how models are treated, and
  Anthropic talks about it most. Against that, the one developer it adds today, Microsoft, has
  published the opposite view, so the record would gain a developer whose places may say "has said
  it will not", which no place says now.
- **Under every rule,** the practice of 2026-11-20 carries over: other developers are searched
  with more searches than Anthropic's places first had, their own pages are opened, and where a
  judgment is needed it is made toward recording what they have done, with the stricter reading
  written beside it. A wider record will make Anthropic's column look more alone, because most new
  developers will show "nothing found". That is what the record says, and the page should say
  plainly that a search can miss what exists.

## Part 5. The choices for John, in the order to put them

**Choice 1. Widen the record by a rule, or keep the three and say why on the About page.**

- *The choice:* cover the developers a stated, checkable rule gives, read on a stated date.
- *Confidence:* moderate. All three rankings agree on a top five that includes two developers the
  record leaves out, Meta and Moonshot. Meta is second on the chat ranking. A reader who knows the
  field will ask why they are missing.
- *Standard or judgment:* judgment call following a family habit. The AGI Zeitgeist follows an
  outside source for one of its measures, but that is a source for a measure, not a list of whom
  to cover. The family's nearest precedent for a list of whom to follow is The Belt Equation's
  missions, which are John's by ruling.
- *Strongest alternative:* keep the three and say why on the About page. It costs no searches and
  no code. A checkable reason exists today: the three are above the clearest gap on two of the three
  rankings. Its cost: the chat ranking contradicts it, the gap can close at the next release, and
  "nothing found among three" still reads to many as "nothing found".

**Choice 2. Which rule.**

- *The choice:* Rule A in its strongest form: every developer in the top five of at least two of
  the three named rankings (Arena's lab ranking, Artificial Analysis's intelligence index, Epoch
  AI's capabilities index), read on a stated date. Today: Anthropic, OpenAI, Google, Meta and
  Moonshot.
- *Confidence:* low to moderate. The three agree today, which is the best defence against any one
  being pushed, and the "two of three" wording settles a disagreement without a judgment. But the
  line at five falls inside the rankings' own margins: Moonshot is fifth by 3 points on one
  ranking and inside an overlapping range on another (Part 1, "How close the line at five is").
  A rule drawn at the clear gap after the third developer would be firmer, and would give today's
  three on two of the three rankings, with the chat ranking disagreeing.
- *Standard or judgment:* judgment call on the rankings and the number five; standard practice to
  follow an outside list.
- *Strongest alternative:* the top ten of all three, nine developers. It covers more of the field,
  including DeepSeek, Alibaba and xAI, at three times the searches and with a page that needs a new
  layout on a computer.

**Choice 3. Rule C beside it.**

- *The choice:* not yet. Rule C is ruled only after a search for developers' published positions
  is made, with John's go (asked for in choice 5), so that its list rests on a search and not on
  pages this project happened to have opened.
- *Confidence:* moderate that it should wait. The rule brings in the only developer found with a
  published position against Anthropic's (Microsoft), which guideline 5 favours. But two of its
  three positions are on personal sites, Joanne Jang's post gives her role as of 2025-06-05 and
  whether she still speaks for OpenAI in it was not checked, and no other developer's positions
  were looked for.
- *Standard or judgment:* judgment call. No sibling has a rule like it.
- *Strongest alternative:* Rule C now, which adds Microsoft today. It brings the opposite view into
  the table at once, at the cost of 7 more places and a code change for six developers, on a list
  this proposal's author doubts is complete.

**Choice 4. How often the list is read again, and whether a developer leaves.**

- *The choice:* read the rankings again every six months, and before each release; a developer once
  covered stays covered unless John rules otherwise, so no place already searched disappears.
- *Confidence:* moderate. Rankings change weekly, and a record that dropped a developer when it
  slipped one place would lose dated work.
- *Standard or judgment:* judgment call.
- *Strongest alternative:* read at each release and drop a developer that falls out. It keeps the
  record exactly in line with the rule, and costs dated places a reader could have used.

**Choice 5. The go for the searches and the code.**

- *The choice:* a go for the 14 places for Meta and Moonshot, made by a session and checked by a
  different one, and for one bounded search for developers' published positions on how their
  models should be treated, so that Rule C can be put to John on a searched list. Five developers
  need no change to the page's code; a sixth, if Rule C later adds one, does. No money.
- *Confidence:* high that this is the cost, moderate on the time.
- *Standard or judgment:* standard practice (the go of 2026-11-20 for the first 14).
- *Strongest alternative:* add the developers to the record now and mark their places "not yet
  searched", searching later. It shows the wider list at once, and the 2026-11-20 searches showed
  how a table full of "not yet searched" reads.

**Not in this proposal:** the wording of the sentence that says who is covered, and where it goes
(the 2026-11-20 ruling leaves it open), and which models Still Asking asks, which is a separate
open question.

## What I would change on reflection

- Five is a round number, and the case for it is only that the three rankings happen to agree on
  five today. If they agreed on four or six, I would have proposed that.
- Rule C was the master session's suggestion, and its list today is built from pages this project
  happened to have opened. Choice 3 now asks for a search for positions before it is ruled.
- If the check of 2026-11-21 had not found the Forum's membership page, Rule B would have gone to
  John with four members instead of six. A logo strip on a home page is not a member list.
