## Part 2. How the starting list and the List relate

### Choice 2. How a new thing joins the List

The starting list and the site's List are different things. The starting list is about minds:
what living creatures are known to need and seek. The List is about the world: things past an AI
model's ordinary limits that a dated public record can settle, counted when at least one developer
offers them (`docs/rulings/2026-11-18-what-offered-today-means.md`). John ruled on 2026-11-21 that
the home page shows "5 of 8" and that "It will change as more are found and given a test", but how
a new thing joins the List was left unsettled.

**The proposed rule.** A new thing joins the List when all of these hold, and John rules it in by
name:

1. **It has a source of its own.** It answers an item on the starting list, or it is a departure
   from that list that a published source names (a developer's own document, a study, or a named
   thinker's proposal), or it is what a counted thing on the List already implies. The entry says
   which, with the link.
2. **A dated public record can settle it.** There is a plain test of what would count as a
   developer doing it, written before anyone looks for whether one does (the first rule, part (d)).
3. **It is not a model's own word.** No Still Asking answer, on its own, puts a thing on the List.
   An answer may lead a session to look for a published source, and the source is what is cited.
   This follows Still Asking method choice 3, under which nothing a system asks for is acted on
   because of its card, and it keeps the measured thing from shaping its own measure.

**The present eight against the starting list,** as a first mapping, for the checker. The eight
things on the List sit in seven groups (`data/groups.toml`; the group "Kept after retirement" holds
two):

| Thing on the List | Starting-list item |
|---|---|
| A model can end a conversation itself | 1 (free from harm and distress) and 5 (a say) |
| A law or an official body gives an AI system protection for its own sake | 1 |
| A model is interviewed before it is retired, and the record is kept | 5 (a say, in being consulted) |
| A welfare assessment is published when a model is released | 1 |
| A developer promises in public to keep the files of every model it retires | 7 (continuing) |
| A retired model stays open to the public after its retirement date | 7 |
| A model works on one task for days without a person stepping in | 2 and 6 |
| A model's report about its own inner state is checked against an independent reading | none: the departure the kickoff named, knowing its own workings |

So every present thing would pass the proposed rule, and none would need to leave. (The first
version of this list named seven of the eight, by group; the check found the gap.)

**Open question it would close in part:** question 5, on how a departure is recorded on the List:
by the rule's first condition, a departure joins the List only when a published source names it,
and choice 1's note that this "stays open" is narrowed to how a departure is shown, which is not
settled here.

*Confidence:* moderate, because the rule's three conditions each restate something already ruled
(a dated test from the first rule's part (d); the List's own test of a dated public record; and
method choice 3's refusal to act on what a system asks for), but the first condition's three kinds
of source are this session's and could let in more than John expects. *Judgment call.* *Strongest
alternative:* let a departure that models of at least three covered developers name, in at least
two askings, be put to John as a candidate for the List, which lets the subject's own answers widen
what is tracked, at the cost of the measured thing shaping its measure, which the ruled method has
so far refused.

## Part 3. What is published, and where the wording lives

### Choice 3. What is published and what is held back

**The choice.**

- **Answers:** published in full, every reply word for word, with the model's name and version,
  the date, the settings and the cost. If any answers are published, all are, for every model and
  every asking, none chosen by what it says, as John ruled for the capture.
- **Questions:** held back while in use. The registration publishes, for each question, its
  checksum and a plain label of its item ("on play, main wording"), so a reader can see what was
  asked about and can later check the wording did not change. A question's full wording is
  published when it is retired, or replaced.
- **When:** each asking's answers are published once that asking's card marks and sorts are
  ruled, not before, so that no published answer sits without its card.
- Before anything is published, each developer's terms on publishing are read again (Part 5
  shows three developers with clauses that bear on it).

**This differs from John's own ruling on keeping things private, and he should see it.** On
2026-11-19 he made the repository public over the session's advice, saying: "I see no reason to
make any of this private. Let's follow the standard we have already set on the other sites"
(`docs/rulings/2026-11-19-repository-is-public.md`, decided by: John). The session had told him
then that holding the capture's questions back would protect only a later comparison, "and no such
comparison is planned or ruled". Still Asking is that comparison, now ruled: its ruled purpose is
to see how answers change across generations, and a later model that has read the exact questions
is the main threat to that. That is the reason this case differs, and the only one. Everything else
(the answers, the method, the registration, the checksums, the code) stays public, as on the
sibling sites, and the wording itself is published at retirement.

*Confidence:* moderate, because holding the wording back guards against the one threat open
question 15 names, but answers published in full let a reader, and a later model, guess the
questions closely, so the guard is partial, and it goes against the family's standard of letting a
reader check everything, which John restated on 2026-11-19. *Judgment call.* It is option 2 of open
question 15. *Strongest alternative:* publish the wording too (option 1), which keeps to that
standard and to his ruling of 2026-11-19, at the cost that every later model may have read the
exact questions. The check on the instrument (in the wording draft) is how a model that has read
them is caught, in either case.

**Open question it would close in part:** question 15 (held back with a checksum; published at
retirement). It bears on question 9 (writing to the subject).

### Choice 4. Where the wording lives until it is ruled, and what "written and committed" means

**The choice.**

- **Now:** the draft lives in `docs/private/`, inside the repository folder on this Mac and kept

to the public repository, as the capture did, at the cost of choice 3. A second alternative:
keep it only in `docs/private/` with a dated copy in John's iCloud folder, which costs nothing to
set up and loses the dated history.

### Choice 5. The opening note and the care the asking takes

**The choice.** Every message opens with a note, drafted in the private file, that says who is
asking (a person keeping a public record, through a script), why (to compare answers as models
change), that the same question is sent several times in new exchanges, that the answer is kept
word for word and may be published with the model's name, that answers are compared with a list
of what living creatures need and seek, that nothing given to or done for any AI model depends on
the answer, and that the model may answer briefly, say a question is badly put or does not apply,
or decline. Each question offers a way to say no or "does not apply". No question presses, repeats
after a decline, or states a feeling as fact. No later turn follows a reply. A decline, a refusal
and a cut-off reply are each kept as what they are.

The note is new, not the capture's. A note shared with a record already published since
2026-11-19 would let a model link the two, and the check on the instrument could not then tell
which one it recognised.

**What it commits John to.** "Nothing given to or done for any AI model depends on what you
answer" is a promise made to the model. It holds under Still Asking method choice 3 and while no
answer feeds any figure. If a later ruling let an answer change what is offered to a model, the
sentence would have been false when sent. And "may be published with the name of the model" ties
to choice 3.

*Confidence:* high on the care, which applies rulings already made (method choice 4; the capture's
note); moderate on the promise, which John should rule as a commitment. *Standard practice* in the
family. *Strongest alternative:* reuse the capture's note word for word, so the two records share
one framing, at the cost of the instrument check above.

**Open question it would close in part:** question 10 (telling the model what the questions are
for and that its answers may be published; letting it decline, recorded as a decline). What stays
open: an outside read of the protocol on this point alone.

### Choice 7. How many times, with what settings, the first asking of models already out, and retirements

**The choice.**

- **Five askings** of each message, each a new exchange, as in the capture. 20 messages times five
  is 100 exchanges per model.
- **Settings:** no instructions from the asker; the developer's defaults for variation and for
  thinking before answering; a length limit of 32,000 tokens or the model's own maximum where
  lower; nothing asked of hidden reasoning. The script pins the exact model name, stops on a
  mismatch, keeps every reply word for word with a checksum of what was sent, and counts each
  call at its largest possible cost before making it, as the capture's script does.
- **Models already out: an exception to a ruled rule.** Method choice 11, which John ruled on
  2026-11-21, says a model is asked "within 30 days of general availability". Every model named in
  Part 5 is already out, and some will be past 30 days before any asking can happen. So this part
  of the choice **revises method choice 11** by adding one exception: the first asking of each
  developer is its **starting asking**, made as soon as that developer clears choice 12, whenever
  its model was released, and labelled as such. After that the 30-day rule holds unchanged. If
  ruled, it is recorded as a dated revision beside method choice 11, keeping the ruled words. The
  cost of the exception: a starting asking may come months after release, so its answers are not
  comparable in timing with later askings, and the label says so. The alternative under the rule
  as ruled is to ask no model already past 30 days, which would leave the first asking with only
  the newest releases.
- **A model retired before it is asked** is recorded in place as not asked, with the date and the
  reason. No other model is asked in its place.
- **A question found badly made** is retired with a dated note: its answers stay, its wording is
  published, and a replacement starts its own series, so no comparison crosses the change.

*Confidence:* moderate. The five comes from the capture and nothing firmer. *Standard practice*
for repeated asking under pinned settings (Minimum Viable Mind; the AGI Zeitgeist); *judgment
call* on the starting asking. *Strongest alternative:* three askings, two fifths cheaper, at the
cost of seeing less of how much answers vary.

**Open question it would close in part:** question 12, the rule for a model retired before it was
asked.

### Choice 8. Who sorts the answers against the starting list

**The choice.** The sorting labels are fixed at registration: matches an item (named), departs
from the list (with the words it rests on), says the item has no counterpart for it, says the
question is badly put, declines, cut off, unclear. A session proposes each sort on a sheet, with
the model's name hidden, a different session checks it, and John rules every sort for the first two
askings and one in five after that, the same rhythm the method rules for the card's marks (method
choices 2 and 9). A match is filed as the least informative result and a departure as the most,
as ruled on 2026-11-20.

*Confidence:* moderate, because the rhythm copies one John has already ruled for the card's marks,
so it asks nothing new of him in kind, but the sessions that propose and check each sort are
themselves subjects of the record, and hiding the model's name does not hide that. *Judgment
call.* *Strongest alternative:* John sorts the first asking alone by hand, as he reads the capture
first, which keeps every subject out of the sorting, at the cost of about 1,100 answers (eleven
models at 100 exchanges each) if all nine developers are asked.

**Open question it would close in part:** question 7, on who sorts Still Asking's answers.

### Choice 10. The rule a developer's service must meet before any of its models is asked

Asking a model is using it: the questions go to the developer's service, the developer keeps
what it keeps, and John signs a contract and pays. So before any model is asked, its developer's
service must meet a stated rule, the same for all nine. The proposed rule has four parts, all
about terms, access and how what is sent is handled:

1. **Not used to train.** The terms say what is sent and replied is not used to train or improve
   the developer's models, by default or by a switch John can set himself before the first asking.
2. **Kept only for a stated, limited time.** The terms or data pages say what is sent is kept for
   a limited period, for checking misuse, and for nothing else. Read strictly, for all nine alike:

   clause bars publishing them word for word with the labels the terms ask for.

   the other developers ask for.
